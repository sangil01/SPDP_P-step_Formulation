## Experimental motivation

Let

$$
Z(y)=C(y)+fK(y),
\qquad
K(y)=\sum_{e:u_e=0}y_e,
$$

where $C(y)$ is the travel cost and $f=500$ is the fixed cost per vehicle. Define

$$
z^*=\min_{y\in\mathcal Y_I} Z(y),
\qquad
c_{\min}=\min_{y\in\mathcal Y_I} C(y),
$$

and, for the minimum feasible fleet size $k_{\min}$,

$$
\phi_{IP}(k_{\min})
=\min\{C(y):y\in\mathcal Y_I,\ K(y)=k_{\min}\},
$$

$$
\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})
=\min\{C(y):y\in\mathcal Y_{LP},\ K(y)=k_{\min}\}.
$$

The global travel-cost optimum may require more than $k_{\min}$ vehicles, in which case

$$
\phi_{IP}(k_{\min})>c_{\min}.
$$

Thus no integer solution simultaneously attains the minimum fleet size and the global minimum travel cost. The LP relaxation can nevertheless combine fractional route structures so that $K(y)=k_{\min}$ while $C(y)\le c_{\min}$. This artificial coexistence of the two integer objectives is the target of the cuts developed below. In the remainder, $c^*=c_{\min}$.

## Costed path-segment concatenation cuts

Consider a branch-and-bound node at which the local bounds fix $K(y)=k_{\min}$. **The purpose of the following separator is to identify a nearly selected path structure that cannot be retained in a $k_{\min}$-route solution without incurring additional variable cost.**

### Path-segment construction

Let $F$ contain the service-to-service edges used to construct the path segments. For any candidate $F$, define its LP deficit by

$$
D_F(\bar y)=\sum_{e\in F}(1-\bar y_e).
\tag{1}
$$

For a directed path segment $P$, let

$$
H(P)=\sum_{e\in E(P)}t_e
$$

be its internal duration. A fast lower bound on the duration of any complete route containing $P$ consecutively is

$$
\underline H(P)
=
\underline h^0_{i,\sigma}+H(P)+\underline h^d_{j,\sigma'},
$$

where $(i,\sigma)$ and $(j,\sigma')$ are the first and last node-states of $P$. To obtain the labels, regard each node-state copy as a vertex and each multigraph edge $e$ as an arc of length $t_e$. One Dijkstra search from the start depot gives $\underline h^0_{i,\sigma}$ for every node-state, and one search from the end depot in the reversed graph gives $\underline h^d_{j,\sigma'}$. Service-node uniqueness is relaxed in these searches (ignore unique visit since there are many same service nodes because of there state), so every feasible route containing $P$ has duration at least $\underline H(P)$.

The set $F$ is generated for an edge threshold $\tau$ and a total deficit budget $\varepsilon\in[0,1)$ as follows.

1. Initialize $F=\varnothing$ and add every service-to-service edge locally fixed to one at the current branch-and-bound node. Such edges have $\bar y_e=1$ and contribute zero to (1). If they violate any of the admissibility conditions below, the node has no feasible integer descendant.
2. Take the nonfixed edges satisfying $\bar y_e\ge\tau$ as candidates and sort them in nonincreasing order of $\bar y_e$.
3. Examine each candidate $e=(u_e,v_e)$ in that order. Add it to $F$ only if:
   - $u_e$ has no successor and $v_e$ has no predecessor in $F$;
   - the addition is consistent with the post-service states of the edges already incident to $u_e$ and $v_e$;
   - the addition does not create a directed cycle; 
   - (optional) the merged path segment $P'$ satisfies $\underline H(P')\le T$; and
   - $D_F(\bar y)+(1-\bar y_e)\le\varepsilon$.
4. Take the maximal directed paths induced by $F$ as the path segments.
5. Add every service node not incident to $F$ as a singleton path segment.

Every nontrivial component of $F$ is a state-consistent directed path, and these components together with the singleton path segments partition the service nodes.

Duration screening is optional in step 3. When it is used, a candidate edge is rejected if the merged path segment $P'$ satisfies $\underline H(P')>T$; if this occurs using only locally fixed edges, the current branch-and-bound node is infeasible. The converse does not hold: $\underline H(P')\le T$ does not prove that $P'$ can be completed, because the shortest relaxed prefix and suffix may violate service-node uniqueness or may be mutually incompatible.
Duration screening is not required for validity. The auxiliary problem only needs to provide a lower bound on the variable cost of integer solutions satisfying $y_e=1$ for every $e\in F$. Omitting route-duration feasibility enlarges the auxiliary feasible set and can only decrease its minimum cost, so the resulting bound and cut remain valid, although potentially weaker.

The parameters $\tau$ and $\varepsilon$ serve different purposes. The threshold $\tau$ controls each candidate separately, whereas $\varepsilon$ bounds the cumulative deviation of all selected edges from one. When a nonfixed edge $e$ is considered, its addition increases the deficit by $1-\bar y_e$. The final test in Step 3 therefore maintains $D_F(\bar y)\le\varepsilon$ throughout the construction.

Because candidates are processed in nonincreasing order of $\bar y_e$, their individual deficits are nondecreasing. If an otherwise admissible edge exceeds the remaining deficit budget, no later candidate can fit, and the construction for that value of $\varepsilon$ terminates. Repeating the procedure with several budgets produces path-segment sets of increasing extent: a small $\varepsilon$ retains only edges extremely close to one, whereas a larger $\varepsilon$ may create longer path segments at the price of weaker cut activation.

The reason for controlling (1) appears directly in the cut. At a node with $K(\bar y)=k_{\min}$, the conditional penalty is multiplied by

$$
1-D_F(\bar y).
$$

The deficit measures how strongly the edges in $F$ are represented by the current fractional solution. It is zero when $\bar y_e=1$ for every $e\in F$, and increases as the selected edges become more fractional. In the cut derived below, the conditional strengthening at the current LP solution is scaled by $1-D_F(\bar y)$. Since $K(\bar y)=k_{\min}$ implies $C(\bar y)\ge\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})$, a candidate with $D_F(\bar y)\ge1$ cannot separate $\bar y$. The restriction $\varepsilon<1$ excludes such candidates during construction. It is only a necessary screening condition; actual violation is tested after the conditional lower bound associated with $F$ has been computed.

The predecessor, successor, endpoint-state, and deficit tests take constant time. Under the degree conditions, adding $u_e\rightarrow v_e$ creates a cycle exactly when its endpoints already belong to the same path component, which can be tested with a disjoint-set data structure. If optional duration screening is used, the two shortest-path searches are performed once and each subsequent duration-bound test takes constant time. In either case, candidate sorting preserves the overall complexity

$$
O(|E|\log |E|).
$$

### Conditional concatenation bound

Let $\mathcal P(F)=\{P_1,\ldots,P_m\}$ be the path segments induced by $F$, including singleton path segments, and let

$$
c(F)=\sum_{e\in F}\bar c_e.
\tag{2}
$$

Suppose that an integer solution satisfies $y_e=1$ for every $e\in F$. The degree and state-continuity equations then force each $P_i$ to occur consecutively in a route. Because the path segments cover all service nodes, every remaining selected service-to-service edge connects the end of one path segment to the beginning of another. Thus a solution using exactly $k_{\min}$ routes must concatenate the $m$ path segments with exactly $m-k_{\min}$ inter-segment connectors.

Let $\underline\beta(F,k_{\min})$ be any certified lower bound on the total variable cost of these connectors, subject to state compatibility and the route-duration limit. Then

$$
K(y)=k_{\min},\qquad y_e=1\quad(e\in F)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C(y)\ge B_F,
\tag{3}
$$

where

$$
B_F=c(F)+\underline\beta(F,k_{\min}).
\tag{4}
$$

The bound in (3) is conditional on retaining $F$; it is not a lower bound for every $k_{\min}$-route solution.

#### For $\underline\beta(F,k_{\min})$ - 1. Polynomial matching bound

For every ordered pair of distinct path segments $(P_i,P_j)$, inspect the original multigraph edges from the last service node of $P_i$ to the first service node of $P_j$. An edge $e$ defines a directed connector $a=(i,j,e)$ if its source and destination post-service states agree with the terminal state of $P_i$ and the initial state required by $P_j$, respectively. Set $\bar c_a=c_e$ and $t_a=t_e$. Parallel connectors are retained unless one is dominated in both cost and duration. If an endpoint state is not fixed, as for a singleton segment, all compatible state alternatives are retained; allowing inconsistent alternatives to be combined later only relaxes the problem. A connector may also be discarded when the certified test

$$
H(P_i)+t_a+H(P_j)>T
$$

holds. This duration test is optional: omitting it enlarges the connector set and preserves the validity of the lower bound.

To obtain a bipartite graph, create a left copy of every path segment as a possible connector tail and a right copy as a possible connector head; connector $a:i\rightarrow j$ joins the left copy of $P_i$ to the right copy of $P_j$. In any feasible concatenation, each path segment has at most one selected outgoing and one selected incoming connector. Moreover, a route containing $r$ path segments uses $r-1$ inter-segment connectors, so $k_{\min}$ routes covering all $m$ path segments use exactly $m-k_{\min}$ connectors. Their connectors therefore form a cardinality-$(m-k_{\min})$ bipartite matching. Minimizing connector cost over all such matchings gives

$$
\underline\beta^{\mathrm{match}}(F,k_{\min})
=
\min\left\{
\sum_a \bar c_a z_a:
\begin{array}{l}
\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\delta^+(i)}z_a\le1 \quad(i=1,\ldots,m),\\[1mm]
\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\delta^-(i)}z_a\le1 \quad(i=1,\ldots,m),\\[1mm]
\displaystyle\sum_a z_a=m-k_{\min},\quad z_a\ge0
\end{array}
\right\}.
\tag{5}
$$

This is a min-cost bipartite matching problem and is polynomially solvable. **It may select connector cycles, combine inconsistent endpoint-state alternatives at an unfixed segment, and violate the duration limit of a complete route. Its feasible set therefore contains every feasible path-segment concatenation.** Consequently,

$$
\underline\beta^{\mathrm{match}}(F,k_{\min})
\le \beta(F,k_{\min}),
\tag{6}
$$

where $\beta(F,k_{\min})$ denotes the exact minimum connector cost. If no matching of cardinality $m-k_{\min}$ exists, the path segments cannot be retained in any $k_{\min}$-route solution.

An aggregate-duration strengthening is obtained from the residual connector-time budget

$$
R_F=k_{\min}T-\sum_{i=1}^m H(P_i),
\tag{7}
$$

where $H(P_i)$ is the duration of path segment $P_i$. Every feasible concatenation into $k_{\min}$ routes satisfies

$$
\sum_a t_a z_a\le R_F,
$$

because its total route duration is at most $k_{\min}T$ and all omitted depot-boundary durations are nonnegative. Adding this necessary condition directly to (5) gives

$$
\underline\beta^{\mathrm{agg}}(F,k_{\min})
=
\min\left\{
\sum_a\bar c_a z_a:
z\text{ satisfies (5)},\quad
\sum_a t_a z_a\le R_F
\right\}.
\tag{8}
$$

This remains a polynomially solvable LP, although the additional resource constraint may destroy the integrality of the matching polytope. Its feasible set is contained in that of (5) and still contains every feasible path-segment concatenation. Therefore,

$$
\underline\beta^{\mathrm{match}}(F,k_{\min})
\le
\underline\beta^{\mathrm{agg}}(F,k_{\min})
\le
\beta(F,k_{\min}).
$$

If (8) is infeasible, the path segments cannot be retained in any $k_{\min}$-route solution.

#### For $\underline\beta(F,k_{\min})$ - 2. Exact path-segment MILP

To obtain an exact formulation, the model selects one feasible endpoint-state configuration for each path segment and requires all incident connectors to use that configuration. It additionally enforces $k_{\min}$ depot-to-depot paths and the duration of each path. For a non-singleton segment, its internal edges in $F$ fix its endpoint states, so it has one configuration. For a singleton segment, each feasible post-service state defines one configuration. Let $\mathcal S_i$ be the endpoint-state configurations of $P_i$, and let $A_F$ contain every state-compatible connector $a$ from a configuration $(i,\rho)$ to a configuration $(j,\eta)$. Connector $a$ has variable cost $\bar c_a$ and duration $t_a$.

Let $x_{i\rho}$ select configuration $(i,\rho)$, let $s_{i\rho}$ and $\ell_{i\rho}$ indicate that it is respectively the first and last segment of a route, and let $z_a$ select connector $a$. The cumulative duration at the end of configuration $(i,\rho)$ is $B_{i\rho}$. A depot-incompatible start or end variable is fixed to zero; otherwise, $t^0_{i\rho}$ and $t^d_{i\rho}$ denote the corresponding depot-connection durations, and the associated time inequality below is imposed. The virtual depot edges have zero variable cost in the present SPDP, so they do not appear in the objective. With valid big-$M$ coefficients, the exact connector model is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta(F,k_{\min})=\min\quad
&\sum_{a\in A_F}\bar c_a z_a\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal S_i}x_{i\rho}=1
&& i=1,\ldots,m,\\
&s_{i\rho}+\sum_{a\in\delta^-(i,\rho)}z_a=x_{i\rho}
&&i=1,\ldots,m,\ \rho\in\mathcal S_i,\\
&\ell_{i\rho}+\sum_{a\in\delta^+(i,\rho)}z_a=x_{i\rho}
&&i=1,\ldots,m,\ \rho\in\mathcal S_i,\\
&\sum_{i,\rho}s_{i\rho}=\sum_{i,\rho}\ell_{i\rho}=k_{\min},\\
&B_{i\rho}\ge t^0_{i\rho}+H(P_i)-M(1-s_{i\rho})
&&i=1,\ldots,m,\ \rho\in\mathcal S_i,\\
&B_{j\eta}\ge B_{i\rho}+t_a+H(P_j)-M(1-z_a)
&&a:(i,\rho)\rightarrow(j,\eta),\\
&B_{i\rho}+t^d_{i\rho}\le T+M(1-\ell_{i\rho})
&&i=1,\ldots,m,\ \rho\in\mathcal S_i,\\
&0\le B_{i\rho}\le Tx_{i\rho}
&&i=1,\ldots,m,\ \rho\in\mathcal S_i,\\
&x_{i\rho},s_{i\rho},\ell_{i\rho},z_a\in\{0,1\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$

Positive service-to-service transition durations make explicit cycle-elimination constraints unnecessary, so (9) is an exact contracted 2-index formulation on the path segments. Let $\underline\beta^{\mathrm{MILP\text{-}LP}}(F,k_{\min})$ denote its LP-relaxation value. Since this relaxation projects into the matching feasible set in (5),

$$
\underline\beta^{\mathrm{match}}(F,k_{\min})
\le
\underline\beta^{\mathrm{MILP\text{-}LP}}(F,k_{\min})
\le
\beta(F,k_{\min}).
$$

If the aggregate constraint $\sum_a t_a z_a\le R_F$ is also imposed, the resulting LP relaxation dominates (8):

$$
\underline\beta^{\mathrm{agg}}(F,k_{\min})
\le
\underline\beta^{\mathrm{MILP\text{-}LP+agg}}(F,k_{\min})
\le
\beta(F,k_{\min}).
$$

#### For $\underline\beta(F,k_{\min})$ - 3. Exact subset dynamic program

When $m$ is small, $\beta(F,k_{\min})$ can be computed exactly by first evaluating every feasible single-route subset and then partitioning the path segments among the routes. Let $M=\{1,\ldots,m\}$. For $Q\subseteq M$, define $g(Q)$ as the minimum connector cost of a state-consistent route that contains exactly the path segments indexed by $Q$ and has duration at most $T$; set $g(Q)=+\infty$ if no such route exists.

The values $g(Q)$ are obtained by a resource-constrained Held--Karp labeling algorithm. For $i\in Q$, $\rho\in\mathcal S_i$, and integer $\theta\le T$, let

$$
L(Q,i,\rho,\theta)
$$

be the minimum connector cost of a partial route that starts at the depot, visits exactly the path segments in $Q$, ends at configuration $(i,\rho)$, and has duration $\theta$ through the end of $P_i$. For every depot-compatible configuration, initialize

$$
L\bigl(\{i\},i,\rho,t^0_{i\rho}+H(P_i)\bigr)=0.
$$

For $j\notin Q$ and each connector $a:(i,\rho)\rightarrow(j,\eta)$, apply

$$
\begin{aligned}
&L\bigl(Q\cup\{j\},j,\eta,\theta+t_a+H(P_j)\bigr)\\
&\quad\leftarrow
\min\left\{
L\bigl(Q\cup\{j\},j,\eta,\theta+t_a+H(P_j)\bigr),
L(Q,i,\rho,\theta)+\bar c_a
\right\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{10a}
$$

provided that the new duration does not exceed $T$. A label can complete a route only if its terminal configuration is depot-compatible and $\theta+t^d_{i\rho}\le T$; hence

$$
g(Q)=
\min_{\substack{i\in Q,\ \rho\in\mathcal S_i,\ \theta\le T\\
\theta+t^d_{i\rho}\le T}}
L(Q,i,\rho,\theta).
\tag{10b}
$$

In the SPDP, virtual depot edges contribute no variable cost to $C(y)$, because the cost of initiating a route is represented separately by $fK(y)$. They are therefore used in the labeling algorithm only to enforce depot compatibility and route duration. For a fixed $(Q,i,\rho)$, a label is discarded when another label has no greater duration and no greater cost.

After computing $g(Q)$ for all $Q\subseteq M$, let $\Gamma(q,Q)$ be the minimum connector cost of partitioning the path segments in $Q$ into exactly $q$ nonempty feasible routes. Set

$$
\Gamma(0,\varnothing)=0,
\qquad
\Gamma(0,Q)=+\infty\quad(Q\ne\varnothing),
\qquad
\Gamma(q,\varnothing)=+\infty\quad(q>0),
\tag{11a}
$$

and, for $q\ge1$ and $Q\ne\varnothing$,

$$
\Gamma(q,Q)
=
\min_{\substack{\varnothing\ne R\subseteq Q,\ \min Q\in R\\g(R)<+\infty}}
\left\{
g(R)+\Gamma(q-1,Q\setminus R)
\right\}.
\tag{11b}
$$

Here $\min Q$ denotes the smallest path-segment index in $Q$. Because the routes are unlabeled, choosing the blocks of a partition in different orders gives the same solution. For example, the partition $\{1,2\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{3,4\}$ would otherwise be generated by selecting either block first. Requiring $\min Q\in R$ selects first the unique block containing the smallest remaining index and recursively imposes a canonical order on the other blocks. Every partition contains exactly one such block, so this symmetry restriction removes only duplicate representations and does not change the optimum.

Recursion (11b) assigns the segments in $R$ to one route and partitions $Q\setminus R$ among the remaining $q-1$ routes. Every feasible $k_{\min}$-route concatenation induces such a partition of $M$, and each block costs at least its corresponding value $g(R)$. Conversely, the single-route realizations attaining the finite $g(R)$ values of any partition form a feasible concatenation because different routes share no path segment. Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\beta(F,k_{\min})
=
\Gamma(k_{\min},M).
}
\tag{12}
$$

If this value is infinite, retaining all edges in $F$ is incompatible with a $k_{\min}$-route solution. Otherwise it may be used directly as $\underline\beta(F,k_{\min})$. For integer durations, the route-labeling stage is exponential in $m$ and pseudo-polynomial in $T$, while the direct partition recursion requires $O(k_{\min}3^m)$ time. The exact dynamic program is therefore used only below a small path-segment limit, typically after the polynomial bounds fail to produce a useful cut. 

### Costed path-segment cut

Assume that (4) gives $B_F>\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})$ and define

$$
\Delta_F=B_F-\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})>0.
\tag{13}
$$

The following inequality is valid for every integer solution:

$$
\boxed{
C(y)
\ge
\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})
\left[1-\bigl(K(y)-k_{\min}\bigr)\right]
+\Delta_F
\left[
1-\bigl(K(y)-k_{\min}\bigr)
-\sum_{e\in F}(1-y_e)
\right].
}
\tag{14}
$$

To prove validity, first consider an integer solution with $K(y)=k_{\min}$. If $y_e=1$ for every $e\in F$, inequality (14) reduces to $C(y)\ge B_F$. Otherwise, $\sum_{e\in F}(1-y_e)\ge1$, so its right-hand side is at most $\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})\le C(y)$. If $K(y)\ge k_{\min}+1$, both bracketed terms are nonpositive and the right-hand side is at most zero, so (14) follows from the nonnegativity of the variable cost.

At a node satisfying $K(\bar y)=k_{\min}$, inequality (14) separates the current LP solution exactly when

$$
C(\bar y)
<
\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})
+\Delta_F\bigl(1-D_F(\bar y)\bigr).
\tag{15}
$$

If the path segments cannot be concatenated into $k_{\min}$ routes, the corresponding infeasible-segment cut is

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{e\in F}y_e
\le
|F|-1+K(y)-k_{\min}.
}
\tag{16}
$$

### Separation procedure

Before the main branch-and-bound search, solve the 2-index LP with $K(y)=k_{\min}$ and store its optimal value $\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})$. At a node where the local bounds fix $K(y)=k_{\min}$, the separator proceeds as follows:

1. construct candidate path forests $F$ from locally fixed and near-one edges, using one or more deficit budgets $\varepsilon<1$;
2. extract their maximal path segments and add singleton segments for uncovered service nodes;
3. compute a certified connector lower bound with  the LP relaxation of (9) augmented by the aggregate constraint;
4. if the polynomial bounds remain inconclusive and the path-segment count is sufficiently small, solve the exact MILP (9) or compute the exact dynamic-programming value (12);
5. add (16) if the selected method certifies that $F$ cannot be retained in any $k_{\min}$-route solution; otherwise, for each finite bound $B_F>\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})$, add (14) exactly when

   $$
   \phi_{LP}(k_{\min})
   +\bigl(B_F-\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})\bigr)
   \bigl(1-D_F(\bar y)\bigr)
   -C(\bar y)>\epsilon_{\mathrm{sep}};
   $$

6. reoptimize and repeat only if the new LP solution produces a distinct violated path-segment family.

The value $\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})$ is known and certified even when the global travel-cost optimum $c^*$ is unknown. A single cut remains conditional on retaining its particular edge set $F$; alternative low-cost fractional structures may therefore require repeated separation. Path-segment generation may be heuristic, whereas validity depends on the path-forest conditions and the certified connector bound used in (4).
