
## 1. The fixed-$K$ conditional gap

Let

$$
Z(y)=C(y)+fK(y),
\qquad
K(y)=\sum_{e:u_e=0}y_e,
$$

where $C(y)$ is variable travel cost, $f$ is the fixed cost per vehicle, and $K(y)$ is the number of depot departures. For the integer feasible set $\mathcal Y_I$ and its LP relaxation $\mathcal Y_{LP}$, define

$$
c_{\min}=\min_{y\in\mathcal Y_I} C(y),
\qquad
k_{\min}=\min_{y\in\mathcal Y_I} K(y),
$$

and the travel-cost value conditional on using the minimum fleet:

$$
\phi_{IP}(k_{\min})
=\min\{C(y):y\in\mathcal Y_I,\ K(y)=k_{\min}\},
$$

$$
\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})
=\min\{C(y):y\in\mathcal Y_{LP},\ K(y)=k_{\min}\}.
$$

The problematic instances satisfy

$$
\phi_{LP}(k_{\min})\le c_{\min}<\phi_{IP}(k_{\min}).
\tag{1}
$$

Thus the LP combines two properties that no integer solution can attain simultaneously: $K(y)=k_{\min}$ and $C(y)\le c_{\min}$. The resulting branch-and-bound lower bound stalls near

$$
c_{\min}+fk_{\min},
$$

although every integer solution with $k_{\min}$ routes costs at least $\phi_{IP}(k_{\min})+fk_{\min}$. For A8, for example,

$$
\phi_{LP}(2)=298.11< c_{\min}=301<\phi_{IP}(2)=314.
$$

In BnP, the LP bound is increased only up to 301, which is $c_{\min}$. These values diagnose the gap only. Neither $c_{\min}$ nor $\phi_{IP}(k_{\min})$ is used as a cut coefficient or right-hand side.

## 2. Why the original relaxation permits the gap

### 2.1 Why can the LP attain $c_{\min}$ or less?

Let $e=(u_e,v_e)$ have duration $t_e$ and tail/head post-service states $\sigma_e^-$ and $\sigma_e^+$. The original formulation assigns a time label $B_{v,\sigma}$ to each node-state and imposes

$$
B_{v_e,\sigma_e^+}
\ge
B_{u_e,\sigma_e^-}+t_e-M_e(1-y_e)
\tag{4}
$$

on each internal edge, together with the depot departure and return rows and

$$
0\le B_{v,\sigma}\le T.
$$

For an integer edge, $y_e=1$ and (4) propagates time exactly. For a fractional edge with $y_e=0.9$ and a representative coefficient $M_e=T+t_e\approx500$, however,

$$
B_{v_e,\sigma_e^+}
\ge
B_{u_e,\sigma_e^-}+t_e-50.
$$

The row then imposes almost no useful ordering when $t_e$ is comparable to or smaller than the $50$ units of slack. Along a fractional path or circulation, this slack accumulates over its edges. Consequently, the per-route work-time restriction becomes nearly inactive in the LP relaxation. By assigning fractional values to the low-cost edges, the LP gains enough big-$M$ slack to approximate route structures that are feasible only without the per-route duration limit. On A8 this gives

$$
\phi_{LP}(2)=298.11<c_{\min}=301.
$$

### 2.2 A second consequence: fractional subtour-like circulations

The same time rows also eliminate positive-duration subtours in an integer solution. If $y_e=1$ on every edge of a directed cycle $C$, summing (4) around $C$ yields the contradiction

$$
0\ge\sum_{e\in C}t_e>0.
$$

For a fractional cycle, the summed inequality becomes

$$
0\ge
\sum_{e\in C}t_e
-
\sum_{e\in C}M_e(1-y_e).
\tag{5}
$$

Hence the cycle is allowed whenever its accumulated big-$M$ slack covers its duration. With $y_e\approx0.9$ and $M_e\approx500$, a set containing $|C|$ such edges receives approximately $50|C|$ units of slack.

This creates a structural weakness in the LP relaxation: a large amount of flow can circulate on edges internal to a service-node set $S$ while only a small amount of flow enters $S$ from outside. Such a component need not be a strictly disconnected subtour. It is a **fractional subtour-like circulation** that remains weakly connected to the depot. Degree and state-balance equations may still be satisfied because the circulation preserves both flow and onboard state, while the accumulated big-$M$ slack in (5) absorbs its positive duration. This motivates adding connectivity inequalities.

The basic connectivity inequality

$$
y(\delta^-(S))\ge1
\tag{6}
$$

eliminates a weakly connected service component whenever its entering flow is less than one. The lower bound of one, however, uses neither vehicle capacity nor the work-time limit. The following sections therefore strengthen this connectivity requirement by deriving larger right-hand sides from capacity and travel-time information whenever those resources imply that more than one route entry is necessary.

## 3. Capacity blossom cuts

Let $N_P$ and $N_D$ denote the pickup and delivery nodes. Vehicle capacity is $Q=2$. Emptying a full skip at a treatment facility changes its state but does not free a vehicle slot; only a delivery frees a slot. Consequently, a route can visit at most two pickups without an intervening delivery, regardless of treatment actions embedded in the connecting edges. The symmetric statement holds for deliveries.

**Proposition 2.** For every $S\subseteq N_P$ and every $S\subseteq N_D$,

$$
y(\delta^-(S))\ge \left\lceil\frac{|S|}{2}\right\rceil.
\tag{7}
$$

**Proof.** Partition each route's visits to $S$ into maximal consecutive blocks. Every block contains at most two nodes of $S$ and has one entering edge in $\delta^-(S)$. Covering all $|S|$ nodes therefore requires at least $\lceil |S|/2\rceil$ entering edges. Summing over routes proves (7). $\square$

### 3.1 Matching reformulation

For pickup requests $i$ and $j$, aggregate all directed parallel edges in both directions as

$$
z_{ij}=\sum_{e:P_i\to P_j\text{ or }P_j\to P_i}y_e.
$$

For $S\subseteq N_P$, define $z(E(S)):=\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}z_{ij}$.

**Proposition 3 (matching reformulation).** For every $S\subseteq N_P$, the capacity inequality (7) is equivalent to

$$
z(E(S))
\le
\left\lfloor\frac{|S|}{2}\right\rfloor.
\tag{8}
$$

**Proof.** Summing the unit in-degree equations over all nodes in $S$ counts every edge whose head belongs to $S$. If its tail lies outside $S$, it contributes to $y(\delta^-(S))$. If its tail also lies in $S$, it connects one unordered pair $\{i,j\}\subseteq S$ and is counted exactly once in $z(E(S))$, regardless of its direction or the number of parallel edges. These two disjoint classes exhaust all edges entering nodes in $S$, so

$$
|S|=y(\delta^-(S))+z(E(S)).
$$

Substituting $y(\delta^-(S))=|S|-z(E(S))$ into (7) gives

$$
z(E(S))
\le
|S|-\left\lceil\frac{|S|}{2}\right\rceil
=
\left\lfloor\frac{|S|}{2}\right\rfloor,
$$

which proves (8). $\square$

### 3.2 Why only odd sets require separation

For each pickup node $P_i$, let $\Sigma_i^{(1)}$ and $\Sigma_i^{(2)}$ denote the post-service states with one and two occupied vehicle slots, respectively. Node-state balance gives

$$
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^+(P_i)\\ \sigma_e^-=\sigma}}y_e
=
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^-(P_i)\\ \sigma_e^+=\sigma}}y_e
\qquad \forall \sigma\in\Sigma_i^+.
$$

In the projected $z$-graph,

$$
z(\delta(i))
=
\sum_{j\ne i}\sum_{e:P_j\to P_i}y_e
+
\sum_{j\ne i}\sum_{e:P_i\to P_j}y_e.
$$

Every edge $P_j\to P_i$ ends at $P_i$ in a two-slot state: one slot was already occupied before visiting $P_i$, and the pickup at $P_i$ occupies the other. Every edge $P_i\to P_j$ must leave $P_i$ in a one-slot state so that one slot remains available at $P_j$. Treatment on the connecting edge may change a skip from full to empty but does not free its slot. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
z(\delta(i))
&\le
\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_i^{(2)}}
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^-(P_i)\\ \sigma_e^+=\sigma}}y_e
+
\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_i^{(1)}}
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^+(P_i)\\ \sigma_e^-=\sigma}}y_e\\
&=
\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_i^{(2)}}
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^-(P_i)\\ \sigma_e^+=\sigma}}y_e
+
\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_i^{(1)}}
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^-(P_i)\\ \sigma_e^+=\sigma}}y_e\\
&\le
\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_i^+}
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^-(P_i)\\ \sigma_e^+=\sigma}}y_e
=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

The equality in the second line follows from node-state balance, and the last equality is the unit in-degree equation at $P_i$. Hence $z(\delta(i))\le1$ for every pickup node. The delivery projection satisfies the analogous inequality by the symmetric state argument.

For any $S\subseteq N_P$,

$$
\sum_{i\in S}z(\delta(i))
=2z(E(S))+z(\delta(S)),
$$

where $\delta(S)$ denotes the undirected boundary in the projected $z$-graph. Internal edges are counted at both endpoints, while boundary edges are counted once. Summing $z(\delta(i))\le1$ over $i\in S$ therefore gives

$$
2z(E(S))+z(\delta(S))\le |S|,
$$

and hence $z(E(S))\le |S|/2$. If $|S|$ is even, this is exactly (8), so the existing formulation already implies the corresponding inequality. If $|S|$ is odd, (8) requires the strictly stronger bound

$$
z(E(S))\le\frac{|S|-1}{2},
\qquad |S|\text{ odd}.
\tag{9}
$$

Fractional odd cycles can satisfy every degree inequality but violate (9): assigning $z_{ij}=1/2$ to every edge of an odd cycle gives $z(\delta(i))=1$ at every node and $z(E(S))=|S|/2>(|S|-1)/2$. Thus only odd-cardinality sets can generate additional violated cuts once the degree inequalities are satisfied.

### 3.3 Exact separation as a minimum odd cut

A connectivity family $y(\delta^-(S))\ge C$ with constant $C$ can be separated by ordinary directed minimum cuts. Assign capacity $y_e$ to every directed edge, use the depot as source, and solve one minimum-cut problem for each service node as sink. If any set $S$ violates the inequality, choosing a sink in $S$ makes $S$ a feasible source--sink cut of capacity below $C$, so one of these minimum-cut problems also returns a violated shore. Conversely, if every such minimum-cut value is at least $C$, no nonempty service-node set violates the family.

This ordinary min-cut construction cannot be applied directly to (7), because its right-hand side $\lceil |S|/2\rceil$ depends on the unknown shore. Ordinary minimum cut minimizes only $y(\delta^-(S))$, rather than the violation

$$
y(\delta^-(S))-\left\lceil\frac{|S|}{2}\right\rceil.
$$

Nevertheless, the equivalent matching form (8) needs separation only for odd sets. Define the nonnegative degree slack

$$
s_i=1-z(\delta(i))\ge0.
$$

Using $\sum_{i\in S}z(\delta(i))=2z(E(S))+z(\delta(S))$ gives

$$
z(\delta(S))+\sum_{i\in S}s_i
=|S|-2z(E(S)).
$$

For odd $|S|$, the equivalence is therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
z(E(S))\le\frac{|S|-1}{2}
&\iff |S|-2z(E(S))\ge1\\
&\iff z(\delta(S))+\sum_{i\in S}s_i\ge1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

Construct an undirected augmented graph $G^+$ with the pickup requests as its original vertices. Give every original edge $\{i,j\}$ capacity $z_{ij}$, add a dummy root $r$, and give each root edge $\{r,i\}$ capacity $s_i$. For a root-free shore $S$, let $c_{G^+}(\delta(S))$ denote the cut capacity, that is, the sum of the capacities of all augmented-graph edges with exactly one endpoint in $S$. By construction,

$$
c_{G^+}(\delta(S))
=z(\delta(S))+\sum_{i\in S}s_i
=|S|-2z(E(S)).
$$

Hence an odd set $S$ violates (9) if and only if $c_{G^+}(\delta(S))<1$, with violation

$$
z(E(S))-\frac{|S|-1}{2}
=\frac{1-c_{G^+}(\delta(S))}{2}.
$$

The separation problem is therefore a minimum odd-cut problem on $G^+$. Assign odd parity to every request vertex and also to $r$ when the number of request vertices is odd, so the total number of odd-parity vertices is even. **(Need Study)** The Padberg--Rao procedure constructs a Gomory--Hu tree and inspects the root-free shore induced by each relevant tree edge. A shore containing an odd number of request vertices and having cut capacity below one yields a violated candidate. Re-evaluating $z(E(S))-(|S|-1)/2$ with the original $y$ values verifies the cut before it is added. Gusfield's construction of the tree uses $m-1$ maximum-flow computations on an augmented graph with $m$ vertices, giving polynomial-time separation instead of enumerating all subsets in (7).

Vertices with $z(\delta(i))=0$ may be omitted: then $s_i=1$, so every root-free shore containing $i$ already has cut capacity at least one. Delivery sets are separated by the same construction.

## 4. Type-closed travel-time cover cuts

The right-hand side of the basic connectivity inequality (6) can be strengthened when $S$ is closed under skip type. Let $\mathcal H$ be the set of skip types. For a nonempty type set $H\subseteq\mathcal H$, define

$$
S_H=\{P_i,D_i:h_i\in H\}.
$$

Equivalently, first form one closed service-node set $S_h=\{P_i,D_i:h_i=h\}$ for each skip type $h$, and then take

$$
S_H=\bigcup_{h\in H}S_h.
$$

Thus the candidate sets are individual type blocks or arbitrary unions of such blocks; no candidate contains only part of a selected type.

This closure is essential. If only part of one type is retained, a delivery may consume an empty skip produced by an omitted pickup of the same type, so projecting a global route onto the retained requests need not be feasible. When all requests of every type in $H$ are retained, removing requests of other types preserves the supply and consumption of each retained empty skip and can only relax vehicle capacity.

The original travel-time matrix need not satisfy the triangle inequality. Deleting an intermediate request could therefore increase route duration. Define the metric closure

$$
\widehat t_{ab}=\text{shortest directed travel time from location }a\text{ to }b,
$$

computed independently of the request subset. The restricted instance for $H$ contains only requests in $S_H$, retains every legal treatment facility, and uses $\widehat t$. Under this metric, deleting actions cannot lengthen a route.

Let $\eta(H)$ be the minimum total duration of a feasible solution to the restricted instance and define

$$
\rho(H)=\left\lceil\frac{\eta(H)}{T}\right\rceil.
\tag{11}
$$

**Proposition 4.** Every integer solution satisfies

$$
y(\delta^-(S_H))\ge\rho(H).
\tag{12}
$$

**Proof.** Project every global route onto the requests in $S_H$, retaining the treatment actions needed by those requests and shortcutting deleted actions with $\widehat t$. Every nonempty projection is a feasible restricted route of duration at most $T$. If $r_H$ global routes touch $S_H$, their projections give a restricted solution with total duration at most $r_HT$. Hence $\eta(H)\le r_HT$ and $r_H\ge\rho(H)$. Each such global route enters $S_H$ at least once, proving (12). $\square$

Exact optimization of $\eta(H)$ is unnecessary. Let $\eta_{LB}(H)$ and $\eta_{UB}(H)$ be certified lower and upper bounds on the restricted duration IP. The computation may stop as soon as

$$
\left\lceil\frac{\eta_{LB}(H)}{T}\right\rceil
=
\left\lceil\frac{\eta_{UB}(H)}{T}\right\rceil.
\tag{13}
$$

The common integer is then the certified value of $\rho(H)$. An incumbent route count or an uncertified upper bound must not be used as the right-hand side of (12).

## 5. Time-flow formulations

### 5.1 The original node-state big-$M$ formulation

Let $0$ and $d$ denote the start and end depots. Exclude the dummy edge $(0,d)$ and partition the remaining edge set $E^*$ into depot-departure edges $E^0:=\{e\in E^*:u_e=0\}$, depot-return edges $E^d:=\{e\in E^*:v_e=d\}$, and internal edges $E^I:=E^*\setminus(E^0\cup E^d)$. Edge $e$ has duration $t_e$, tail state $\sigma_e^-$, and head state $\sigma_e^+$. The state attached to an edge is the onboard state after completing the action at the corresponding endpoint.

The original duration formulation assigns one continuous time label $B_{v,\sigma}$ to every reachable internal node-state $(v,\sigma)$. It imposes

$$
B_{v_e,\sigma_e^+}\ge t_ey_e
\qquad \forall e\in E^0,
\tag{14a}
$$

$$
B_{v_e,\sigma_e^+}
\ge
B_{u_e,\sigma_e^-}+t_e-M_e(1-y_e)
\qquad \forall e\in E^I,
\tag{14b}
$$

and

$$
B_{u_e,\sigma_e^-}+t_ey_e\le T
\qquad \forall e\in E^d,
\tag{14c}
$$

together with $0\le B_{v,\sigma}\le T$. The implementation uses the valid coefficient $M_e=T+t_e$ in (14b).

When $y_e=1$, (14a)--(14c) propagate a lower bound on cumulative route time and enforce return by time $T$. Their LP behavior is weaker. First, (14b) receives the additive slack $M_e(1-y_e)$ on every fractional edge. This slack accumulates along a fractional walk, as discussed in Section 2. Second, all fractional flows that reach the same node-state share the single label $B_{v,\sigma}$, even if those flows represent different elapsed times. The formulation therefore cannot conserve elapsed time separately for the fractional flow carried by each edge.

### 5.2 Arc time-flow variables and shortest-path bounds

Introduce one nonnegative continuous variable $g_e$ for each $e\in E^*$. For an integer route, define $g_e$ as the cumulative elapsed time immediately after traversing edge $e$, including the travel and service represented by $e$. 

To bound $g_e$, consider the directed node-state graph in which each multigraph edge $e$ is an arc from $(u_e,\sigma_e^-)$ to $(v_e,\sigma_e^+)$ with length $t_e$, including service time for $v_e$. Let

$$
\underline h^0_{u,\sigma}
=
\text{shortest duration from }0\text{ to }(u,\sigma),
$$

and

$$
\underline h^d_{v,\sigma}
=
\text{shortest duration from }(v,\sigma)\text{ to }d.
$$

Both quantities are computed by Dijkstra's algorithm because edge durations are nonnegative. The shortest paths may underestimate the duration of every feasible elementary route. They are therefore valid lower bounds for tightening the formulation.

For every non-departure edge $e\in E^*\setminus E^0$, define

$$
L_e:=\underline h^0_{u_e,\sigma_e^-}+t_e,
\qquad
U_e:=T-\underline h^d_{v_e,\sigma_e^+}.
$$

The arc time-flow linking constraints are

$$
g_e=t_ey_e
\qquad \forall e\in E^0,
\tag{15a}
$$

and

$$
L_ey_e\le g_e\le U_ey_e
\qquad \forall e\in E^*\setminus E^0.
\tag{15b}
$$

The multiplication by $y_e$ scales the entire feasible time interval. In particular, at a fractional solution, (15b) confines $g_e$ to the proportionally scaled interval $[L_ey_e,U_ey_e]$, which contracts linearly toward zero as $y_e$ decreases. Hence $g_e$ remains tightly linked to the amount of flow on edge $e$, whereas the additive term $M_e(1-y_e)$ in (14b) increasingly relaxes the corresponding time relation as $y_e$ decreases. Also, $y_e=0$ forces $g_e=0$, without the additive slack of (14b). If $L_e>U_e$, no route of duration at most $T$ can contain $e$, so $y_e$ may be fixed to zero. For a return edge $e\in E^d$, $\underline h^d_{d,\sigma_e^+}=0$ and hence $U_e=T$; its upper bound in (15b) is precisely the route-duration restriction $g_e\le Ty_e$.

Constraints (15a)--(15b) bound the time carried by each edge but do not yet connect the time of consecutive edges. That connection is supplied by a conservation equation.

### 5.3 TF1: node-only time conservation

For every physical service node $v$, TF1 imposes

$$
\sum_{e\in\delta^+(v)}(g_e-t_ey_e)
=
\sum_{e\in\delta^-(v)}g_e.
\tag{16}
$$

The right-hand side is the time arriving at $v$. For an outgoing edge $e$, $g_e$ includes the duration $t_e$ incurred after leaving the state at $v$, so $g_e-t_ey_e$ is the time at the tail of that edge. Equation (16) therefore conserves both route flow and its elapsed time at $v$.

To verify validity, take any feasible integer route and set $g_e$ to its cumulative elapsed time on every used edge and to zero on every unused edge. A used departure edge satisfies (15a). For every other used edge, the route prefix is no shorter than $\underline h^0_{u_e,\sigma_e^-}$ and the remaining route is no shorter than $\underline h^d_{v_e,\sigma_e^+}$, so (15b) holds. If $e^-$ and $e^+$ are the unique used in-edge and out-edge at $v$, respectively, then (16) reduces to

$$
g_{e^+}-t_{e^+}=g_{e^-},
$$

which is exactly cumulative-time propagation along the route.

Conversely, for an integer routing solution, (15a) and (16) recursively determine cumulative time from each depot departure. A positive-duration directed cycle disconnected from the depot would contradict (16), because summing (16) around the cycle gives $\sum_{e\in C}t_e=0$. Finally, (15b) on the return edge enforces total route duration at most $T$. Hence TF1 represents the same duration-feasible integer routes as (14a)--(14c), without using big-$M$ coefficients.

TF1 is nevertheless aggregated in the LP relaxation: time entering a physical node in one onboard state may leave through an edge associated with another state, provided aggregate equality (16) is preserved.

### 5.4 TF2: node-state time conservation

Let $\Sigma_v^+$ be the set of reachable post-service states at physical service node $v$. TF2 disaggregates (16) by state:

$$
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^+(v)\\ \sigma_e^-=\sigma}}
(g_e-t_ey_e)
=
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^-(v)\\ \sigma_e^+=\sigma}}g_e
\qquad
\forall v,\ \forall \sigma\in\Sigma_v^+.
\tag{17}
$$

Every edge incident to $v$ is associated with exactly one canonical post-service state at that endpoint. Summing (17) over $\sigma\in\Sigma_v^+$ therefore recovers (16). Consequently, every TF2-feasible LP solution is TF1-feasible after aggregation. The converse need not hold: TF1 may transfer time between different states at the same physical node, whereas (17) prohibits that transfer. TF2 thus gives an LP relaxation at least as strong as TF1 and can be strictly stronger. The two formulations coincide on integer routes because state-continuity constraints already force the selected in-edge and out-edge to use the same state.

### 5.5 Relation to the big-$M$ formulation and model size

The main model admits the following five duration formulations:

| Time flow | Big-$M$ | Duration formulation |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0 | 1 | Node-state labels $B$ and (14a)--(14c) |
| 1 | 0 | TF1 only |
| 1 | 1 | TF1 and the big-$M$ formulation |
| 2 | 0 | TF2 only |
| 2 | 1 | TF2 and the big-$M$ formulation |

If time flow is disabled, (14a)--(14c) are necessary to enforce the duration limit. TF1 and TF2 are each sufficient without those rows. Retaining both formulations takes the intersection of two valid LP relaxations and therefore cannot weaken the LP bound. No general dominance relation between their fractional feasible regions is required here; the possible strengthening from their intersection must be weighed against the larger LP.

The size increase can be stated more precisely. Let

$$
m:=|E^*|,\qquad m_0:=|E^0|,
$$

let $p$ be the number of physical service nodes, and let

$$
q:=\sum_v|\Sigma_v^+|
$$

be both the number of reachable physical node-state pairs carrying a $B$ variable and the number of node-state conservation equations in TF2. Ignoring all routing rows and variables common to the alternatives, their added sizes are

| Formulation | Additional continuous variables | Additional matrix rows |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Big-$M$ only | $q$ | $m$ |
| TF1 only | $m$ | $(2m-m_0)+p$ |
| TF2 only | $m$ | $(2m-m_0)+q$ |
| TF1 + big-$M$ | $m+q$ | $(3m-m_0)+p$ |
| TF2 + big-$M$ | $m+q$ | $(3m-m_0)+q$ |

There is one linking equality (15a) for each of the $m_0$ departure edges and two linking inequalities (15b) for each of the other $m-m_0$ edges, giving exactly $2m-m_0$ time-flow linking rows. Variable bounds such as $0\le B_{v,\sigma}\le T$ and $g_e\ge0$ are not counted as matrix rows. TF1 and TF2 introduce the same $m$ arc variables; TF2 replaces the $p$ node balances with $q$ node-state balances.

In the action-based multigraph, $m$ can be much larger than both $p$ and $q$. The arc variables $g_e$ and their linking rows can therefore enlarge the root LP substantially, and TF2 adds a further $q-p$ rows over TF1. This explains the computational tradeoff observed in the experiments: state-disaggregated time propagation can sharply reduce the search tree on difficult fixed-$K$ instances, while the larger LP may slow instances that the original formulation already solves easily.

### TF2 - Capacity cut - travel time cut

실험 설정은 TF2, big-$M$ 시간제약 비활성화, capacity blossom cut과 screened type-closed travel-time cover cut 활성화이며 두 cut 모두 adaptive-tree scope를 사용했다. Root cut은 pre-MIP barrier/no-crossover로 처리하고 main MIP은 automatic/automatic으로 풀었다. Main MIP 제한시간은 3600초, instance 전체 제한시간은 5400초, initial-incumbent 제한시간은 1200초이다.

두 실험의 차이에서 확인되는 cut의 순수한 효과는 다음과 같다.

- Cut을 추가하면 optimal 증명이 56개에서 57개로 한 개 증가하며, 추가로 닫힌 인스턴스는 C17이다. C17은 TF2 no-cut에서 $UB=7059$, $LB=7014$, gap 0.64%였지만 cut을 사용하면 $UB=LB=7043$으로 optimal이 된다.
- 전체 main MIP time은 52,782.1초에서 49,043.7초로 7.1% 감소했다. 67개 중 43개에서 시간이 감소하고 17개에서 증가했으며 7개는 사실상 동일했다. A14는 420.0초에서 133.4초, B17은 743.1초에서 241.7초, C14는 1419.8초에서 486.5초로 크게 빨라졌다.
- 전체 nodes는 916,348개에서 1,083,375개로 18.2% 증가하지만 이 수치는 C6이 지배한다. C6을 제외하면 189,638개에서 73,514개로 61.2% 감소한다. 즉 대부분의 인스턴스에서는 tree가 작아졌지만 C6은 726,710 nodes에서 1,009,861 nodes로 증가했다.
- Final gap은 5개에서 개선되고 56개에서 동일하며 6개에서 악화되었다. 개선 폭이 큰 인스턴스는 D5의 1.51%에서 0.47%, D7의 1.29%에서 0.55%, C17의 0.64%에서 0%이다. C12도 12.32%에서 11.86%로 소폭 개선된다.
- 반대로 C13, C15, C16, C18, C20, D6은 gap이 악화되었다. 특히 D6은 0.33%에서 1.61%로 커졌고, C16은 7.93%에서 8.52%로 커졌다. 큰 C 인스턴스에서는 root LP 처리 비중이 커서 cut 추가가 항상 더 좋은 incumbent나 bound로 이어지지 않는다.
- 결론적으로 TF2 자체가 기존 bottleneck의 대부분을 해소하며, 두 cut의 추가 효과는 평균 gap보다 계산시간 감소와 C17 optimal 증명에서 분명하다. 다만 C6과 D6처럼 악화되는 사례도 있으므로 현재 cut 설정이 모든 인스턴스를 지배한다고 볼 수는 없다.
