# Load-State Disaggregation and Time-Flow Strengthening in the State-Expanded Multigraph

## 1. Purpose and central thesis

The state-expanded multigraph strengthens transition consistency through **load-state disaggregation**. Flow is conserved separately at each node-state, preventing flow that arrives in one load state from departing through a transition requiring an incompatible state.

This strengthening is accompanied by two distinct weaknesses in the LP relaxation of the route-duration constraints:

1. **parallel-arc splitting and activation dilution:** when multiple Pareto edges connect the same pair of node-states, their common transition flow may be divided among several edge variables, weakening the big-$M$ implication activated separately by each variable; and
2. **path reconvergence and elapsed-time information loss:** distinct path prefixes may reach the same node-state after accumulating different elapsed times, while the node-time big-$M$ formulation represents them with a single time label and does not conserve their time-weighted arrivals.

We distinguish three route-duration formulations throughout the note:

1. the **node-time big-$M$ formulation**, used in the original model;
2. the **state-aggregated time-flow formulation**, which conserves elapsed-time flow after aggregating all load states associated with a physical node; and
3. the **state-disaggregated time-flow formulation**, which conserves elapsed-time flow separately at every node-state.

The intended central claim is:

> Load-state disaggregation enforces transition consistency by preventing flow from passing between incompatible load states. In the resulting state-expanded multigraph, however, fractional flow may be split across parallel arcs, and distinct path prefixes may reconverge at the same node-state after accumulating different elapsed times. These structures lead respectively to activation dilution and elapsed-time information loss in the node-time big-$M$ formulation. The state-disaggregated time-flow formulation addresses both effects through edge-wise time-flow linking and node-state-wise time-flow conservation.

## 2. Notation

Let a node-state be

$$
s=(i,\sigma),
$$

where $i$ is a physical service node and $\sigma$ is its post-service load state. Each multigraph edge $e$ represents

$$
e:
(u_e,\sigma_e^-)
\longrightarrow
(v_e,\sigma_e^+),
$$

and has travel-and-service duration $t_e$, variable cost $c_e$, and selection variable $y_e$.

Let $E^0:=\{e\in E:u_e=0\}$ denote the set of depot-departure edges.

The node-time big-$M$ formulation uses variables $B_{i,\sigma}$ and the edge-wise implication

$$
B_{v_e,\sigma_e^+}
\ge
B_{u_e,\sigma_e^-}+t_e-M_e(1-y_e).
\tag{1}
$$

The state-disaggregated time-flow formulation introduces an edge time-flow variable $g_e$, departure-edge initialization

$$
g_e=t_e y_e
\qquad \forall e\in E^0,
\tag{2a}
$$

non-departure linking bounds

$$
L_e y_e\le g_e\le U_e y_e
\qquad \forall e\in E\setminus E^0,
\tag{2b}
$$

and node-state conservation

$$
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^+(i)\\ \sigma_e^-=\sigma}}
(g_e-t_e y_e)
=
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^-(i)\\ \sigma_e^+=\sigma}}
g_e.
\tag{3}
$$

# Part I. Transition-consistency benefit and duration-relaxation costs of the multigraph

## 3. Load-state disaggregation and transition consistency

For every physical node $i$ and feasible post-service state $\sigma$, the state-expanded formulation imposes

$$
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^-(i)\\ \sigma_e^+=\sigma}}y_e
=
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^+(i)\\ \sigma_e^-=\sigma}}y_e.
\tag{4}
$$

### Lemma 1 — State-wise balance implies physical-node balance, but not conversely

The endpoint states partition the incoming and outgoing edges incident to $i$. Therefore, summing the state-wise balance equations over all $\sigma\in\Sigma_i^+$ yields the physical-node balance equation. Conversely, flow entering $i$ in state $\sigma$ and leaving in a different state $\tau\ne\sigma$ may satisfy physical-node balance while violating the state-wise equations for both states. $\square$

## 4. Parallel-arc splitting and activation dilution

For two node-states

$$
s=(u,\sigma^-),
\qquad
t=(v,\sigma^+),
$$

define the parallel-arc bucket

$$
E_{st}
=
\left\{
e:
(u_e,\sigma_e^-)=s,
\ (v_e,\sigma_e^+)=t
\right\}.
$$

The associated aggregate transition flow is

$$
z_{st}=\sum_{e\in E_{st}}y_e.
\tag{5}
$$

This aggregation is valid for the state-flow contribution of these edges because they have identical endpoint states. Their costs, durations, and load-state transitions remain edge-specific and are not aggregated.

### Proposition 1 — Splitting a fixed transition flow among parallel arcs weakens each positively used edge-wise big-$M$ implication

Suppose that at least two edges in $E_{st}$ carry positive flow. Then, for every $e\in E_{st}$ with $y_e>0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_e(1-y_e)
&=
M_e(1-z_{st})
+
M_e(z_{st}-y_e)\\
&=
M_e(1-z_{st})
+
M_e\sum_{f\in E_{st}\setminus\{e\}}y_f\\
&>
M_e(1-z_{st}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

Therefore, the right-hand side of the edge-wise time implication satisfies

$$
B_s+t_e-M_e(1-y_e)
=
B_s+t_e-M_e(1-z_{st})
-
M_e(z_{st}-y_e),
\tag{7}
$$

and is strictly smaller than the corresponding bound obtained by activating the same implication with the aggregate transition flow $z_{st}$. Thus, distributing a fixed transition flow among multiple parallel edges dilutes the activation of every positively used edge-wise big-$M$ constraint. $\square$

This proposition concerns only edges with identical physical and state endpoints. It establishes a per-edge activation effect and does not by itself imply a dominance relation between the complete simple-graph and multigraph LP relaxations.

## 5. Pathwise accumulation of big-$M$ deactivation

### Proposition 2 — Edge-wise big-$M$ deactivation accumulates additively along a fractional path prefix

For a path prefix or directed walk

$$
p=(e_1,\ldots,e_m)
$$

ending at node-state $s$, summing (1) yields

$$
B_s
\ge
B_{\mathrm{start}(p)}
+\sum_{e\in p}t_e
-\sum_{e\in p}M_e(1-y_e).
\tag{8}
$$

Thus,

$$
\sum_{e\in p}M_e(1-y_e)
$$

is the cumulative big-$M$ deactivation along $p$, obtained by adding the deactivation term of every edge in the fractional path prefix. $\square$

### Corollary 1 — At reconvergence, node-time big-$M$ constraints retain only the strongest discounted pathwise lower bound

Consider any path decomposition of a fractional routing flow. Each path $r$ in the decomposition has a coefficient $\alpha_r>0$, meaning that it carries $\alpha_r$ units of flow and

$$
y_e=\sum_{r:e\in r}\alpha_r.
$$

For a prefix $p$ of a decomposed path $r$, write $\alpha_p:=\alpha_r$. Let $\mathcal P_s$ be a collection of such prefixes that start at the same node-state $o$ and reconverge at the same node-state $s$.

By Proposition 2, the node-time big-$M$ constraints associated with these prefixes imply

$$
B_s
\ge
\max_{p\in\mathcal P_s}
\left\{
B_o
+\sum_{e\in p}t_e
-\sum_{e\in p}M_e(1-y_e)
\right\}.
$$

Hence the common time label $B_s$ retains the strongest of the discounted pathwise lower bounds. It does not preserve the flow-weighted elapsed-time contribution

$$
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_s}
\alpha_p\sum_{e\in p}t_e,
$$

where $\alpha_p$ is precisely the amount of decomposed fractional flow carried by prefix $p$. The former is a maximum of individually discounted lower bounds, whereas the latter is the flow-weighted sum of the elapsed times of the path prefixes. $\square$

## 6. Path-prefix compression and elapsed-time information loss

Consider edges sharing only the physical endpoints $u$ and $v$:

$$
(u,\sigma_1^-)
\longrightarrow
(v,\sigma_1^+),
\qquad
(u,\sigma_2^-)
\longrightarrow
(v,\sigma_2^+).
$$

These edges belong to different state-balance rows whenever their endpoint states differ. Consequently, they must not be aggregated as in (5).

### Remark 1 — State expansion can intensify reconvergence-induced elapsed-time information loss

The state-expanded graph lifts each physical transition to a family of state-specific edges, one for each admissible load-state transition. Load-state histories that are implicit at the physical-node level are therefore represented explicitly as distinct state-consistent path prefixes, yielding a much richer path structure. This richer structure can contain many prefixes that follow different physical paths or intermediate state sequences yet reconverge at the same node-state. Proposition 2 and Corollary 1 show why this richer path structure matters for duration propagation: big-$M$ deactivation accumulates along each fractional path prefix, while the common node-time label retains only the strongest discounted pathwise lower bound after reconvergence. Consequently, a common node-state may represent many distinct elapsed-time histories without preserving their flow-weighted elapsed times. In this way, state expansion can intensify reconvergence-induced elapsed-time information loss under the node-time big-$M$ formulation.

### Concluding remark — The state-expanded multigraph is particularly vulnerable to node-time big-$M$ relaxation

Proposition 1 identifies activation dilution when fractional transition flow is split among parallel edges with identical endpoint states. Proposition 2 and Corollary 1 identify pathwise big-$M$ deactivation and elapsed-time information loss at reconvergence, while Remark 1 explains how the richer state-consistent path structure of the state-expanded multigraph can intensify this mechanism. The node-time big-$M$ formulation is particularly susceptible to activation dilution and reconvergence-induced elapsed-time information loss.

# Part II. State-disaggregated time-flow strengthening

## 7. State-disaggregated time-flow formulation

Adapting the standard resource-flow construction to the state-expanded SPDP multigraph, we impose time-flow conservation separately for every node-state.

For every edge $e\in E$, introduce a nonnegative time-flow variable $g_e$. For each depot-departure edge, impose the initialization constraint (2a):

$$
g_e=t_e y_e
\qquad \forall e\in E^0.
$$

For every non-departure edge, impose the linking bounds (2b):

$$
L_e y_e\le g_e\le U_e y_e
\qquad \forall e\in E\setminus E^0.
$$

At every node-state $(i,\sigma)$, impose the state-disaggregated time-flow conservation equation (3):

$$
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^+(i)\\ \sigma_e^-=\sigma}}
(g_e-t_e y_e)
=
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^-(i)\\ \sigma_e^+=\sigma}}
g_e.
$$

The edge variable $y_e$ represents the amount of routing flow using edge $e$, while $g_e$ represents the total elapsed time carried by that flow immediately after traversing $e$. To make this interpretation precise, let $x_r\ge0$ be the weight of a duration-feasible route $r$, and let $\tau_{re}$ be its cumulative elapsed time immediately after traversing $e$. Then

$$
y_e=\sum_{r:e\in r}x_r,
\qquad
g_e=\sum_{r:e\in r}\tau_{re}x_r.
\tag{9}
$$

Whenever $y_e>0$,

$$
\frac{g_e}{y_e}
=
\frac{\sum_{r:e\in r}\tau_{re}x_r}
       {\sum_{r:e\in r}x_r}
\tag{10}
$$

is the average elapsed time of the flow using edge $e$. Thus, $g_e$ is simply the amount of flow on $e$ multiplied by its average elapsed time.

The departure-edge initialization constraint (2a) fixes the carried time to $g_e=t_e y_e$, since elapsed time is zero at the start depot. For each non-departure edge, the linking bounds (2b) restrict the average elapsed time to the valid interval $[L_e,U_e]$. In particular, a return edge has $U_e=T$, so its upper linking bound is $g_e\le T y_e$, enforcing the route-duration limit. The node-state conservation equation (3) states that, at each node-state, the total elapsed time carried by the incoming flow equals the total elapsed time carried by the outgoing flow before the durations of the outgoing edges are added.

For an integer route, assigning its cumulative elapsed time to every selected edge satisfies these constraints directly. Conversely, the conservation equations propagate cumulative time along every selected route, and the upper bound on a return edge enforces the route-duration limit. The formulation therefore represents the same duration-feasible integer routes as the node-time big-$M$ formulation.

## 8. Comparison with state-aggregated time-flow conservation

The state-disaggregated formulation in Section 7 is designed to exploit the load-state structure of the SPDP by conserving elapsed-time flow separately for every node-state. To isolate the strengthening obtained from state disaggregation, consider its state-aggregated counterpart. It uses the same routing variables, time-flow variables, and edge-linking constraints (2a)--(2b), but imposes time-flow conservation only at each physical node:

$$
\sum_{e\in\delta^+(i)}(g_e-t_e y_e)
=
\sum_{e\in\delta^-(i)}g_e.
\tag{11}
$$

Equation (11) permits time-flow imbalances associated with different load states to cancel at the same physical node, whereas (3) requires conservation separately for every node-state. The following proposition formalizes this dominance relationship.

### Proposition 4 — State-disaggregated conservation dominates state-aggregated conservation

Let $P_{\mathrm{agg}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{dis}}$ denote the feasible sets of the state-aggregated and state-disaggregated formulations, respectively, in the common $(y,g)$-space under the same routing and edge-linking constraints. Then

$$
P_{\mathrm{dis}}\subseteq P_{\mathrm{agg}},
$$

and consequently,

$$
\operatorname{proj}_y(P_{\mathrm{dis}})
\subseteq
\operatorname{proj}_y(P_{\mathrm{agg}}),
$$

so state-disaggregated conservation gives an LP relaxation that is no weaker in the routing variables.

For a fixed physical node $i$, summing (3) over all $\sigma\in\Sigma_i^+$ yields

$$
\sum_{e\in\delta^+(i)}(g_e-t_e y_e)
=
\sum_{e\in\delta^-(i)}g_e,
$$

which is (11).
Therefore, every solution in $P_{\mathrm{dis}}$ belongs to $P_{\mathrm{agg}}$. Projecting both feasible sets onto the routing variables preserves this inclusion. $\square$

Physical nodes in the state-expanded SPDP commonly admit multiple load states. The state-aggregated formulation therefore provides many opportunities for nonzero state-wise time-flow imbalances to cancel at the physical-node level. Although multiple states do not guarantee strict inclusion for every instance, they create substantial scope for the state-disaggregated formulation to exclude fractional routing solutions admitted by the state-aggregated formulation.

## 9. Connection to the two weaknesses identified in Part I

### Corollary 2 — Linear time flow avoids activation dilution and preserves flow-weighted elapsed time at reconvergence

The state-disaggregated time-flow formulation removes the two loss mechanisms identified for the node-time big-$M$ formulation through the combined effect of time-flow conservation and edge-flow linking.

First, elapsed-time propagation is enforced by the node-state conservation equation (3), rather than by an edge-wise implication containing $M_e(1-y_e)$. For a fractional solution, (3) preserves the total flow-weighted elapsed time across all incoming and outgoing edges as an equality. Together with (2a)--(2b), which anchor and bound $g_e$, it propagates valid elapsed-time flow without any additive deactivation term. Hence splitting flow among parallel edges cannot create the activation dilution identified in Proposition 1.

Second, consider the collection $\mathcal P_s$ from Corollary 1 of state-consistent path prefixes that reconverge at a node-state $s=(i,\sigma)$. Prefix $p\in\mathcal P_s$ carries $\alpha_p$ units of flow and accumulates elapsed time $\sum_{e\in p}t_e$. Equation (3) requires

$$
\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^+(i)\\ \sigma_e^-=\sigma}}
(g_e-t_e y_e)
=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_s}
\alpha_p\sum_{e\in p}t_e.
$$

Thus, reconvergence may merge the path histories, but it does not discard their flow-weighted elapsed time. The formulation preserves this weighted sum rather than retaining only the strongest discounted pathwise lower bound. $\square$

# Part III. Computational diagnosis of temporal-relaxation collapse

## 10. Experimental design and time-row-free reference

The experiment directly measures the LP contribution of the time formulations and tests whether the node-time big-$M$ plateau aligns with a time-row-free integer reference. All runs use the original-cost objective, and use the same graph generated with the same solver settings, and time limits. Only the same capacity cuts are included or excluded in every model.

Let

$$
Z(y):=C(y)+fK(y),
\qquad
K(y):=\sum_{e\in\delta^+(0)}y_e,
$$

where $K(y)$ is the depot-departure flow. It is the number of vehicles in an integer solution and may be fractional in an LP solution. Let $P^0\subseteq[0,1]^{|E|}$ denote the $y$-projection of the continuous relaxation obtained by removing all elapsed-time variables and constraints while retaining the routing, load-state balance, and capacity constraints. Its binary restriction is

$$
\mathcal Y_I^0
=
P^0\cap\{0,1\}^{|E|}.
$$

Define the time-row-free LP and IP reference values as

$$
z_{LP}^{0}
=
\min\{Z(y):y\in P^0\},
$$

and

$$
z_{IP}^{0}
=
\min\{Z(y):y\in\mathcal Y_I^0\}.
$$

The set $\mathcal Y_I^0$ is an auxiliary reference rather than the feasible set of the SPDP without the duration limit: after all time rows are removed, a binary solution may contain depot-disconnected subtours. Its optimum identifies the least-cost integer routing and load-state pattern before either route-duration feasibility or time-based subtour elimination is imposed.

The following three formulations are compared:

1. $\mathcal M^0$: all elapsed-time variables and constraints are removed;
2. $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{BM}}^T$: node-time big-$M$ constraints are imposed; and
3. $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{dis}}^T$: state-disaggregated time-flow constraints are imposed.

Let $z_{LP}^{\mathrm{BM}}$ and $z_{LP}^{\mathrm{dis}}$ denote the LP values of $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{BM}}^T$ and $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{dis}}^T$, respectively. Since the node-time big-$M$ formulation adds constraints to $P^0$,

$$
z_{LP}^{0}
\le
z_{LP}^{\mathrm{BM}}.
$$

Hence

$$
z_{LP}^{\mathrm{BM}}
-
z_{LP}^{0}
$$

measures the lower-bound improvement contributed by the big-$M$ time rows.

Let $\mathcal Y_I^T$ denote the common integer feasible set represented by the two duration formulations, and define

$$
z_{IP}^{T}
=
\min\{Z(y):y\in\mathcal Y_I^T\}.
$$

Solve both the LP relaxation and binary model of $\mathcal M^0$ to obtain $z_{LP}^{0}$ and $z_{IP}^{0}$. 
Record the optimal depot-departure flow in the LP solution and the vehicle count in the integer solution. The selected edges of an optimal time-row-free integer solution are inspected to determine whether it contains depot-disconnected subtours, depot-connected routes longer than $T$, or both. 
For $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{BM}}^T$ and $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{dis}}^T$, record the LP value and depot-departure flow, together with the final lower bound, optimality status, solution time, explored nodes, and final gap of the integer run. 
A proven optimum of either duration formulation determines $z_{IP}^{T}$ and its vehicle count. If an LP relaxation or integer model is not solved to optimality, report its valid bound without labeling it as an optimum.

All comparisons use the original objective $Z(y)$ directly. The analysis covers the full instance set used in the matched formulation comparison and reports separately the instances solved only by $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{dis}}^T$ and those solved by both duration formulations.

### Observation 1 — The node-time big-$M$ relaxation collapses toward the time-row-free reference

For the identified instances, the node-time big-$M$ constraints provide negligible improvement over the time-row-free LP relaxation, and the resulting LP bound remains near the time-row-free integer optimum. The state-disaggregated time-flow formulation yields a stronger lower bound and moves the relaxation toward the duration-feasible integer optimum. Together with the structure of the time-row-free integer solutions, this provides computational evidence that activation dilution and reconvergence-induced elapsed-time information loss substantially weaken temporal consistency in the node-time big-$M$ relaxation of the state-expanded multigraph.

If the required relations are not observed, this statement must be weakened to report only the measured formulation differences; the results should not be described as temporal-relaxation collapse.
