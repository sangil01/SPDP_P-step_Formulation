## Motivation

Note 13 established that, under the fixed fleet size $K(y)=k_{\min}$, the LP relaxation of the two-index multigraph model attains a travel cost below the unrestricted integer optimum $c_{\min}$, whereas every $k_{\min}$-route integer solution must pay $\phi_{IP}(k_{\min})>c_{\min}$. Branch-and-bound then stalls at the plateau $c_{\min}+f\,k_{\min}$. Both cut families tried in note 13 (costed path-segment cuts, request-partition inequality) left the plateau untouched. This note records the diagnosis of the plateau on A8 and the two model components that removed it: exact connectivity cuts and a single-commodity time-flow formulation that replaces the big-$M$ duration constraints.

All experiments use the P=0 direct two-index IP with the baseline configuration (original-cost objective, $K=k_{\min}$ fixed, VI36 with location subsets up to size 7, VI44, duration-MILP initial incumbent, Gurobi 120 s). The six test instances all have $k_{\min}=2$ and $f=500$.

The final bound equals $c_{\min}+1000$ on A8, A9, A13, B5, C4 and $c_{\min}+1001$ on B1. On A13 the baseline also failed to find the optimum itself: its best incumbent after 120 s was 1379 against $z^*=1378$.

## Diagnosis on A8

### Step 1. Disconnected fractional cycles

The fixed-$K$ travel-cost LP ($\phi_{LP}(2)=298.11$) was dumped edge by edge and analysed with a maximum-flow separation from the depot to every service node. Two node sets receive less than one unit of depot flow:

- $S_1=\{4,5,6,19,20,21\}$ (requests 3, 4, 5): entering flow $0.219$. The LP runs the cycle $4\to21\to6\to20\to5\to19\to4$ with $y=0.945$ on every edge. The onboard state is $E(4)$ throughout: the cycle carries an empty skip of type 4 that was never produced.
- A 16-node superset with entering flow $0.740$.

The big-$M$ duration rows do not exclude such cycles: with $y=0.945$ and $M\approx500$ every row has slack $\approx27$, and six rows absorb the cycle duration of $146$. State-balance rows are satisfied because a cycle conserves every token.

### Step 2. Duration is the only remaining source of the conditional gap

Removing the duration rows from the fixed-$K$ travel-cost **IP** (connectivity cuts kept) gives the optimum $301=c_{\min}$ in $0.56$ s, one branch-and-bound node, total duration $631\le 2T$. Hence

$$
\min\{C(y):y\in X,\ K(y)=2\}=301
\quad\text{without route duration},
\qquad
\phi_{IP}(2)=314
\quad\text{with route duration}.
$$

The whole conditional gap $314-301$ is created by the per-route limit $\sum_{e\in r}t_e\le T$, and the aggregate inequality $\sum_e t_e y_e\le T\,K(y)$ is not violated ($636.9\le960$). The LP therefore has to be told, without big-$M$, that a fractional route piece cannot be prolonged indefinitely.

### Step 3. Connectivity alone is not sufficient

Adding connectivity cuts until none is violated moves the LP from $298.11$ to $298.50$; the remaining solution is a mixture of many depot-connected fractional paths with weights $0.75,0.25,0.21,0.125,0.11,\dots$, several of which exceed $T$ (durations 465 and 589 appear in a greedy flow decomposition). In the IP, connectivity cuts alone (21,600 cuts in 120 s) leave the final bound at 1301.

## Connectivity cuts

For a service-node set $S\subseteq V\setminus\{0,2n+1\}$ every integer solution satisfies

$$
\sum_{e\in\delta^-(S)}y_e\ \ge\ 1,
\tag{1}
$$

because every route is an open path that starts at the depot $0\notin S$. A duration-strengthened right-hand side is

$$
\sum_{e\in\delta^-(S)}y_e\ \ge\ \max\Bigl\{1,\ \Bigl\lceil\frac{\underline H(S)}{T}\Bigr\rceil\Bigr\},
\qquad
\underline H(S)=\sum_{v\in S}\min_{e\in\delta^-(v)}t_e,
\tag{2}
$$

since each node of $S$ is entered exactly once, every route spends at most $T$ inside $S$, and a route entering $S$ uses at least one edge of $\delta^-(S)$. On the current instances $\underline H(S)$ never exceeded $T$ for a violated set, so (2) reduced to (1) in practice; the strengthening is kept as an option.

Separation is exact on the fractional support: for every service node $v$ a maximum flow from the depot with capacities $\bar y$ is computed (Edmonds-Karp on the dense $(2n+2)\times(2n+2)$ matrix); if the flow is below the right-hand side, the sink side of the minimum cut is the violated set. Cuts are added as Gurobi user cuts at every node (or root only), at most 32 per callback, sorted by violation. In the code this is $\mathtt{ConnectivityCuts.\{h,cpp\}}$ with the flags $\mathtt{--add\text{-}connectivity\text{-}cuts}$, $\mathtt{--connectivity\text{-}cut\text{-}scope}$, $\mathtt{--connectivity\text{-}cut\text{-}rhs}$, $\mathtt{--connectivity\text{-}cut\text{-}max\text{-}per\text{-}round}$. For LP runs the same separator is applied in a cutting-plane loop.

## Time-flow formulation

### Variables and constraints

For every non-dummy edge $e=(u,v)$ introduce

$$
g_e\ =\ y_e\cdot(\text{route time on arrival at }v\text{, including the service at }v),
$$

so that $g_e=0$ whenever $y_e=0$. Let $\sigma^-_e,\sigma^+_e$ be the post-service states at the tail and head of $e$. Let $\underline h^0_{u,\sigma}$ be the shortest duration from the depot to the node-state $(u,\sigma)$ and $\underline h^d_{v,\sigma}$ the shortest duration from $(v,\sigma)$ to the end depot, both computed by Dijkstra on the node-state graph in which every multigraph edge is an arc of length $t_e$ and node uniqueness is relaxed. The constraints are

$$
g_e = t_e\,y_e \qquad (u=0),
\tag{3}
$$

$$
\bigl(\underline h^0_{u,\sigma^-_e}+t_e\bigr)\,y_e\ \le\ g_e\ \le\ \bigl(T-\underline h^d_{v,\sigma^+_e}\bigr)\,y_e \qquad (u\ne0),
\tag{4}
$$

$$
\sum_{e\in\delta^+(v)}\bigl(g_e-t_e\,y_e\bigr)\ =\ \sum_{e\in\delta^-(v)}g_e
\qquad \forall v\in V\setminus\{0,2n+1\}.
\tag{5}
$$

Equation (5) is the node form; the option value 2 of $\mathtt{--add\text{-}time\text{-}flow\text{-}formulation}$ writes it separately for every $(v,\sigma)$ with $\sigma$ ranging over the post-service states of $v$, which is valid because the used in-edge and out-edge of $v$ share the same post-service state.

### Validity

Take a feasible integer solution and a route $r$. For every used edge $e$, $g_e$ equals the arrival time at head$(e)$; the arrival time is at least $\underline h^0_{u,\sigma^-_e}+t_e$ and at most $T-\underline h^d_{v,\sigma^+_e}$, since the remaining part of the route needs at least $\underline h^d$. This gives (3)-(4). At a service node $v$ exactly one in-edge $e^-$ and one out-edge $e^+$ are used, $g_{e^+}=g_{e^-}+t_{e^+}$, and all other terms vanish, which is (5). Edges with $\underline h^0+t_e>T-\underline h^d$ cannot appear in any duration-feasible route and are fixed to zero (none on the six instances).

Edges into the end depot have $t_e=0$, so (4) enforces $g_e\le T\,y_e$, i.e. the route duration limit, without any big-$M$ coefficient. The big-$M$ rows become redundant for the integer program and can be dropped.

### Effect on the relaxation

The root LP moves only slightly, and the movement comes from the node-state form and the removal of the big-$M$ rows rather than from the time-flow rows alone. Root relaxation values of the original-cost model (Gurobi "Root relaxation" line, before its own cut loop):

| Instance | BASE | TF1 | TF2 | TF2-S1 | BASE plateau after 120 s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A8 | 1298.11 | 1299.07 | 1301.14 | 1301.14 | 1301 |
| A9 | 1241.50 | 1241.50 | 1241.50 | 1240.00 | 1243 |
| A13 | 1370.00 | 1370.10 | 1370.10 | 1370.10 | 1375 |
| B1 | 1281.00 | 1281.17 | 1282.09 | 1282.09 | 1284 |
| B5 | 1135.50 | 1135.50 | 1135.50 | 1135.50 | 1141 |
| C4 | 1463.00 | 1463.00 | 1467.04 | 1463.39 | 1474 |

The largest gains are $+3.03$ on A8 and $+4.04$ on C4; on B5 the root is unchanged and on A9 the TF2-S1 value even decreases, because restricting VI36 to singleton subsets removes rows that were active at the root. In the same comparison the fixed-$K$ travel-cost LP on A8 goes from $298.11$ to $298.50$ with connectivity cuts and to $299.25$ with the node-form time-flow rows. So the relaxation value is not where the method pays off, with the single exception that the A8 TF2 root $1301.14$ already exceeds the bound $1301$ that the baseline could not pass in 120 s.

The reason the root stays low is the aggregated conservation (5): where two fractional route pieces meet at a node, the accumulated time may be transferred to whichever out-edge has the shorter continuation, so a long fractional chain can be "reset". A single commodity cannot distinguish which route a unit of time belongs to.

The formulation nevertheless changes branch-and-bound qualitatively. Once branching fixes edges to one, (5) propagates exact arrival times through the fixed part of a route and (4) becomes a hard duration bound; with big-$M$ rows the same information is only obtained after essentially all edges of the route are fixed, because a row with $y_e=0.95$ still carries slack $0.05M$. This is why the plateau disappears in the tree although the root bound is almost unchanged.

Reading of the table:

1. The time-flow formulation is the component that removes the plateau: with it, A8, A9, A13 and B1 are solved to optimality in seconds to a minute, whereas the baseline could not move the bound above $c_{\min}+f k_{\min}$ in 120 s.
2. The node-state form (TF2) and the removal of the redundant big-$M$ rows are what make C4 solvable (114 s), and they halve the node counts on A13 and B1.
3. B5 is limited by LP size: with VI36 subsets up to 7 the model has $2.1\cdot10^6$ nonzeros and Gurobi spends more than 100 s in the root cut loop. Restricting VI36 to the original singleton rows brings B5 to 55 s and C4 to 59 s without hurting the small instances. The control run shows that this restriction alone (without time-flow) does not improve the bound.
4. Connectivity cuts are complementary but not decisive: they help on B1 and B5, are neutral on A8/A9, and on C4 the combination with small VI36 rows lost optimality within the limit (variance across runs is substantial at this scale, and the root cutting loop can become expensive when many cuts are added).

### Recommended configuration

$\mathtt{--add\text{-}time\text{-}flow\text{-}formulation\ 2}$, $\mathtt{--vi\text{-}44\text{-}subproblem\text{-}add\text{-}time\text{-}constraints\ 0}$ (drops the big-$M$ rows from the main model), VI36 subsets $\le 1$ ($\mathtt{--vi\text{-}36\text{-}subset\text{-}max\text{-}size\ 1}$); connectivity cuts optional. In $\mathtt{run\_release\_many.sh}$ the corresponding variables are $\mathtt{ADD\_TIME\_FLOW\_FORMULATION}$, $\mathtt{VI\_44\_SUBPROBLEM\_ADD\_TIME\_CONSTRAINTS}$, $\mathtt{VI\_36\_SUBSET\_MAX\_SIZE}$, $\mathtt{ADD\_CONNECTIVITY\_CUTS}$.

### Open points

- The root LP value is essentially unchanged by (3)-(5); the gain is in the tree. A relaxation that also moves the root would need per-route time tracking (multi-commodity time flow indexed by the first pickup, or route columns), which is where the p-step / column formulations re-enter.
- For $k_{\min}>2$ the same mechanism applies (per-route duration with a fixed fleet); the connectivity RHS (2) and $k$-path cuts with $k\ge3$ become relevant only when sets that need three or more routes are entered by fewer than three units of flow.
- The costed path-segment cut, request-partition inequality and 2-path cuts were not violated at the relevant LP solutions; the negative results are recorded above so they are not retried in the same form.


## What did not work

- **Costed path-segment cuts and request-partition inequality** (note 13): no bound improvement.
- **Connectivity cuts alone** in the IP: 21,600 cuts, bound 1301 on A8.
- **Aggregate duration cut** $\sum_e t_e y_e\le T\,K(y)$: not violated by the plateau LP (636.9 versus 960 on A8).
- **2-path (k-path) cuts** $\sum_{\delta^-(S)}y\ge2$ for sets $S$ that no single duration-feasible route can cover, with an exact subset-DP oracle over node-states: on the A8 LP after connectivity and time-flow constraints no violated set was found among greedily grown sets of up to 16 nodes (all sets with entering flow below 2 admitted a single route of duration at most 300). The fractional solution spreads flow so evenly that large sets are entered by almost two units.
- **Root-only versus full-tree connectivity separation**: on B5 the root cutting loop with connectivity cuts consumed the whole 120 s (1 node), so root separation should be capped when the node LP is expensive.

## Notes on model size

B5 has 27,248 rows and 2.0 M nonzeros already in the baseline; the VI36 rows with subsets up to size 7 are dense. The time-flow variables add $|E|$ columns and about $2|E|+|V|$ rows, which raised the B5 root LP time from 1.6 s to 9.4 s and cut the node throughput by two orders of magnitude within 120 s. For the larger instances the model reduction (dropping the redundant big-$M$ rows, capping VI36 subset size) matters as much as the strength of the relaxation.

## Status of this note

The time-flow formulation described here is still in the code and is selected by '--add-time-flow-formulation' together with '--add-big-m-time-constraints', which replaced the older '--vi-44-subproblem-add-time-constraints 0' way of dropping the big-$M$ rows from the main model. The two switches are now independent, so TF1 and TF2 can each be run with or without the big-$M$ rows.

The connectivity cuts of this note were removed from the code. Note 16 measured them as a net loss once the capacity blossom and type-closed travel-time cover families were available: they competed for the same root budget without cutting off much that the two families miss. The sections above are kept as the record of the diagnosis that led to the time-flow formulation, not as a description of the current build.

The auxiliary models also changed. The $k_{\min}$ duration IP and the fixed-$K$ incumbent MILP always use the big-$M$ formulation now; the time-flow choice applies to the main MILP only.
