# 16. Capacity blossom cuts and type-closed travel-time cover cuts

Follow-up to note 15. The time-flow formulation removed the fixed-$K$ plateau on the six small instances, but the root relaxation barely moved and the larger instances (A15, A17, B6, B8, C6, C8, C9, C12, C13, C14, D5, D6, D7) still show the same conditional gap. This note develops the two cut families proposed in the discussion after note 15: a **capacity** family that generalises COR Eq. (36) to arbitrary pickup or delivery sets and is separated exactly as odd-set (blossom) inequalities, and a **travel-time** family that strengthens the connectivity right-hand side for skip-type-closed node sets with a certified duration bound. VI35 is no longer generated: its rows are the full-set members of the capacity family.

All notation follows note 15: $y_e$ edge variables of the action-based multigraph, $N_P$ / $N_D$ pickup and delivery nodes, $Q=2$, route duration limit $T$, $\delta^-(S)$ the edges entering $S$, $E(S)$ the edges with both endpoints in $S$.

## 1. Capacity blossom cuts

### 1.1 Validity on the multigraph

A pickup occupies one of the two slots, emptying at a treatment facility only changes the slot content from $F(h,l)$ to $E(h)$, and only a delivery frees a slot ([RouteState.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/RouteState.cpp)). Hence a route visits at most two pickups without an intermediate delivery, **whatever emptying happens on the connecting arcs**, and symmetrically at most two deliveries without an intermediate pickup. For every $S\subseteq N_P$ (and every $S\subseteq N_D$) all integer solutions satisfy

$$
\sum_{e\in\delta^-(S)} y_e \;\ge\; \Bigl\lceil \tfrac{|S|}{2}\Bigr\rceil
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\sum_{e\in E(S)} y_e \;\le\; \Bigl\lfloor \tfrac{|S|}{2}\Bigr\rfloor ,
\tag{1}
$$

the equivalence following from the unit in-degree rows ($\sum_{v\in S}\text{indeg}(v)=|S|=\sum_{\delta^-(S)}y+\sum_{E(S)}y$). COR's Eq. (36) is the special case $S=N_{P|l}$ (all pickups at one location); VI35 is the case $S=N_P$.

The counterexample discussed for the treatment-embedded graph ($0\to p_1\to[\text{empty}]\to p_2\to[\text{empty}]\to p_3$) is **not** a feasible route: after $p_2$ both slots are occupied (by an $E$ and an $F$ skip), so $p_3$ cannot be picked up. The earlier implementation of Eq. (36) ([PstepValidInequality.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/PstepValidInequality.cpp), function 'pickup_or_delivery_coefficients') followed the COR node model and counted an edge $p_1\to[\text{empty}]\to p_2$ with $p_1,p_2\in S$ as leaving and re-entering $S$. On the multigraph that edge is internal to $S$; the option '--vi-36-treatment-boundary 0' now counts only the real graph boundary, which removes coefficients from the left-hand side of a $\ge$ row and therefore strengthens it. Treatment-location rows keep the old coefficient rule (they count treatment visits inside 'sequence_pi').

### 1.2 Matching form and exact separation

Aggregate the parallel edges between two pickups, in both directions and including edges with embedded treatment,

$$
z_{ij}=\sum_{e:\,P_i\to P_j\ \text{or}\ P_j\to P_i} y_e .
$$

Then (1) reads $z(E(S))\le\lfloor |S|/2\rfloor$: the pickup adjacency of any integer solution is a matching on $N_P$. The degree inequality $z(\delta(i))\le 1$ is implied by the LP (in-edges from pickups deliver a two-skip post-state, out-edges to pickups need a one-skip post-state, and the state-balance rows separate the two classes), so even sets are implied by degree rows and only **odd** sets matter:

$$
z(E(S))\le\frac{|S|-1}{2}\quad(|S|\text{ odd})
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
z(\delta(S))+\sum_{v\in S}s_v\ \ge 1,\quad s_v = 1-z(\delta(v)).
$$

This is the blossom inequality of the fractional matching polytope, separated exactly by Padberg--Rao: add a root $r$ joined to every $v$ by an edge of capacity $s_v$, label every original node odd (and $r$ too when their number is odd), build a Gomory--Hu tree (Gusfield's algorithm, $m-1$ maximum flows on a graph with $m\le |N_P|+1$ nodes) and read off the odd shores of every tree edge with weight below $1$. Each candidate is re-evaluated on the original $y$ (violation $=z(E(S))-(|S|-1)/2$). Deliveries are handled identically. Nodes with $z$-degree zero are dropped from the separation graph (they have slack $1$ and can never belong to a violated set).

Implementation: [CapacityBlossomCuts.h](../SPDP_codes/include/CapacityBlossomCuts.h), [CapacityBlossomCuts.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/CapacityBlossomCuts.cpp). Rows are emitted in the sparse internal form $\sum_{E(S)}y\le\lfloor |S|/2\rfloor$ by default ('--capacity-blossom-row-form inbound' gives the other form). The test 'spdp_cut_module_tests' compares the separator with exhaustive enumeration of all odd subsets on random fractional points of the A8 graph (25 points per family, 13 with planted violated odd cycles) and checks that an integer solution violates no cut.

### 1.3 Cut management (CVRP-style)

The separator runs in the Gurobi user-cut callback shared by all families ([TwoIndexSolver.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/TwoIndexSolver.cpp)). Root: every root relaxation, at most 32 cuts per round, 256 per root, 20 rounds, stop after two consecutive rounds without a cut or with best violation below $0.02$. Tree ('adaptive-tree'): every node while the node count is below 50, afterwards every 100th node, at most 8 cuts per call, a global cap of 2000 cuts, and separation stops when its accumulated time exceeds 15% of the solver runtime. Emitted sets are kept in a pool so the same set is never added twice; candidates of one round whose Jaccard overlap with an accepted candidate exceeds $0.9$ are skipped. All limits are CLI options ('--capacity-blossom-*').

## 2. Type-closed travel-time cover cuts

### 2.1 Projection argument and why it needs the metric closure

For a set $H$ of skip types let $S_H=\{P_i,D_i: h_i\in H\}$. Take a feasible route and delete every action of a request whose type is not in $H$, keeping the treatment stops of the retained pickups. The result is a feasible route of the **restricted instance** that contains only the $H$ requests: the onboard set is a subset of the original one, every empty skip consumed by a retained delivery was produced by a retained pickup (same type $\Rightarrow$ same $H$), and the retained treatments still empty the retained skips. Arbitrary request subsets do not have this property (a delivery may use a skip picked up by another request of the same type), which is why $S$ must be type-closed.

Route duration is the one place where the argument can fail: the instances are non-metric (A8: 162 ordered location pairs with a shorter two-leg path, up to 11 minutes shorter). Deleting an intermediate stop $x$ replaces $t_{ax}+t_{xb}$ by $t_{ab}$, which can be *longer*. The restricted instance therefore uses the **metric closure** $\hat t_{ab}=$ shortest directed travel time (Dijkstra from every physical location, shared module [ShortestTravelTimes.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/ShortestTravelTimes.cpp); the virtual depot row/column is excluded because its zeros would connect every pair at cost $0$). With $\hat t\le t$ along any path, the projected route is no longer than the original one. Service times are unchanged.

### 2.2 The certified bound

Let $\eta(H)$ be the minimum total route duration of the restricted instance and $\rho(H)=\lceil \eta(H)/T\rceil$. If an integer solution uses $r_H$ routes that touch $S_H$, their projections form a restricted solution of total duration at most $r_H T$, hence $r_H\ge\rho(H)$, and every such route enters $S_H$ at least once:

$$
\sum_{e\in\delta^-(S_H)} y_e\ \ge\ \rho(H)
\qquad\forall\,\emptyset\ne H\subsetneq\{\text{types}\}.
\tag{2}
$$

$\eta(H)$ is **not** solved to optimality. The restricted duration IP (TF2 time flow, no big-$M$, DurationBound graph with the min-duration parallel-edge and empty-connector pruning) runs with the rounded-bound callback already used for $k_{\min}$: with $H_{LB}$ the tolerance-corrected solver bound and $H_{UB}$ the exact duration of the incumbent, it stops as soon as $\lceil H_{LB}/T\rceil=\lceil H_{UB}/T\rceil$, which certifies $\rho(H)$. $\lceil H_{LB}/T\rceil$ is valid on its own, but only masks that reached this certificate are turned into rows; masks that hit the time limit without it are logged and dropped. The full type set is skipped (its row is the departure bound of VI44). Certified values are cached per instance (key: requests, $T$, service times, closure fingerprint, formulation) so that configuration comparisons do not repeat the sub-IPs; the preprocessing time is reported separately from the 120 s main solve.

Implementation: [TypeTravelTimeCoverCuts.h](../SPDP_codes/include/TypeTravelTimeCoverCuts.h), [TypeTravelTimeCoverCuts.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/TypeTravelTimeCoverCuts.cpp). Rows are used either statically or **screened**: at every node relaxation the not-yet-added rows are evaluated and the violated ones (at most 8 per call) are added as user cuts. The duration IP wrapper 'solve_duration_rounded_bound' lives in [KMinComputation.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/KMinComputation.cpp); the same TF2 option is now available for the $k_{\min}$ duration IP ('--vi-44-subproblem-add-time-flow') and for the fixed-$K$ incumbent MILP ('--initial-incumbent-time-flow', '--initial-incumbent-big-m-time-constraints').

On A8 the preprocessing takes $1.1$ s for the 14 proper type unions; two of them have $\rho=2$ (types $\{0,3,4\}$ with 14 requests, $\eta\in[567,567]$; types $\{0,4,7\}$ with 12 requests, $\eta\ge 503$), the remaining twelve have $\rho=1$.

### 3.1 The separation timing problem and the root LP cut phase

The first round of experiments showed that enabling the two families inside the branch-and-cut callback changed almost nothing on the large instances. The reason is timing, not validity. On instances such as C13 and D7 the root node alone consumes the whole time limit, so Gurobi issues very few `MIPNODE` callbacks and the separators are never asked for a violated row while the bound is still moving.

The fix is to separate before the MIP starts. The new `--root-lp-cut-phase` option solves the LP relaxation of the same model, runs every enabled separator to closure on the fractional point, and adds the resulting rows to the MIP as ordinary constraints. The phase is bounded by `--root-lp-cut-phase-time-limit` and its runtime is subtracted from the main solve budget, so the comparison stays time-fair.

This is where the families actually act. The LP value before the phase reproduces the plateau reported in note 13: on C13 it is exactly $5230$, on D7 it is $6278.33$, on D5 it is $4908.85$ and on D6 it is $4876.25$, all equal to the bound the previous configuration converged to. The separated rows move the model above that plateau.

### 3.4 What the numbers support

P5 is the best of the three. It never loses more than $2$ units of lower bound against R0 and gains where the conditional gap was the binding difficulty: $+8$ on D7, $+7$ on C13, $+6$ on C14, $+5$ on D6, $+3$ on C9, $+1$ on C8. The upper bound also improves on the same instances, which is consistent with a tighter relaxation guiding the search rather than with luck in the heuristics.

Adding connectivity cuts on top, as in P6, is not worth it. It raises the bound slightly further on C9 and C13 but costs a large amount of search time on C14 and D5, where the upper bound degrades enough to push the mean gap above the baseline. The connectivity separation competes with the two new families for the same root LP budget without adding much beyond what they already cut off.

The small instances A8 through B8 are closed by every configuration, so they carry no information about the cuts. The one regression worth naming is A17, where all three configurations reach $2094$--$2096$ and the cuts do not help; this instance is limited by something other than the fixed-$K$ conditional gap.

The headline result is that the plateau described in note 13 is no longer where the solver stops. On the six instances where the bound used to sit exactly at the travel-only optimum, the separated rows lift it by $3$ to $8$ units before branching begins, and the remaining gap is ordinary branch-and-bound work rather than a structural blind spot in the formulation.
