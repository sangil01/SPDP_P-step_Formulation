# 16. Capacity blossom cuts and type-closed travel-time cover cuts

Follow-up to note 15. The time-flow formulation removed the fixed-$K$ plateau on the six small instances, but the root relaxation barely moved and the larger instances (A15, A17, B6, B8, C6, C8, C9, C12, C13, C14, D5, D6, D7) still show the same conditional gap. This note develops the two cut families proposed in the discussion after note 15: a **capacity** family that generalises COR Eq. (36) to arbitrary pickup or delivery sets and is separated exactly as odd-set (blossom) inequalities, and a **travel-time** family that strengthens the connectivity right-hand side for skip-type-closed node sets with a certified duration bound. VI35 is no longer generated: its rows are the full-set members of the capacity family.

All notation follows note 15: $y_e$ edge variables of the action-based multigraph, $N_P$ / $N_D$ pickup and delivery nodes, $Q=2$, route duration limit $T$, $\delta^-(S)$ the edges entering $S$, $E(S)$ the edges with both endpoints in $S$.

## 1. Capacity blossom cuts

### 1.1 Validity on the multigraph

A pickup occupies one of the two slots, emptying at a treatment facility only changes the slot content from $F(h,l)$ to $E(h)$, and only a delivery frees a slot ([RouteState.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/RouteState.cpp)). Hence a route visits at most two pickups without an intermediate delivery, **whatever emptying happens on the connecting arcs**, and symmetrically at most two deliveries without an intermediate pickup. For every $S\subseteq N_P$ (and every $S\subseteq N_D$) all integer solutions satisfy

$$
\sum_{e\in\delta^-(S)} y_e \;\ge\; \Bigl\lceil \tfrac{|S|}{2}\Bigr\rceil
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\sum_{e\in E(S)} y_e \;\le\; \Bigl\lfloor \tfrac{|S|}{2}\Bigr\rfloor ,
\tag{1}
$$

the equivalence following from the unit in-degree rows ($\sum_{v\in S}\text{indeg}(v)=|S|=\sum_{\delta^-(S)}y+\sum_{E(S)}y$). COR's Eq. (36) is the special case $S=N_{P|l}$ (all pickups at one location); VI35 is the case $S=N_P$.

The counterexample discussed for the treatment-embedded graph ($0\to p_1\to[\text{empty}]\to p_2\to[\text{empty}]\to p_3$) is **not** a feasible route: after $p_2$ both slots are occupied (by an $E$ and an $F$ skip), so $p_3$ cannot be picked up. The earlier implementation of Eq. (36) ([PstepValidInequality.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/PstepValidInequality.cpp), function 'pickup_or_delivery_coefficients') followed the COR node model and counted an edge $p_1\to[\text{empty}]\to p_2$ with $p_1,p_2\in S$ as leaving and re-entering $S$. On the multigraph that edge is internal to $S$; counting only the real graph boundary removes coefficients from the left-hand side of a $\ge$ row and therefore strengthens it. Since the boundary rule is dominated for every $y\ge 0$, it is now fixed in the code and the '--vi-36-treatment-boundary' option was removed. Treatment-location rows keep the old coefficient rule (they count treatment visits inside 'sequence_pi').

### 1.2 Matching form and exact separation

Aggregate the parallel edges between two pickups, in both directions and including edges with embedded treatment,

$$
z_{ij}=\sum_{e:\,P_i\to P_j\ \text{or}\ P_j\to P_i} y_e .
$$

Then (1) reads $z(E(S))\le\lfloor |S|/2\rfloor$: the pickup adjacency of any integer solution is a matching on $N_P$. The degree inequality $z(\delta(i))\le 1$ is implied by the LP (in-edges from pickups deliver a two-skip post-state, out-edges to pickups need a one-skip post-state, and the state-balance rows separate the two classes), so even sets are implied by degree rows and only **odd** sets matter:

$$
z(E(S))\le\frac{|S|-1}{2}\quad(|S|\text{ odd})
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
z(\delta(S))+\sum_{v\in S}s_v\ \ge 1,\quad s_v = 1-z(\delta(v)).
$$

This is the blossom inequality of the fractional matching polytope, separated exactly by Padberg--Rao: add a root $r$ joined to every $v$ by an edge of capacity $s_v$, label every original node odd (and $r$ too when their number is odd), build a Gomory--Hu tree (Gusfield's algorithm, $m-1$ maximum flows on a graph with $m\le |N_P|+1$ nodes) and read off the odd shores of every tree edge with weight below $1$. Each candidate is re-evaluated on the original $y$ (violation $=z(E(S))-(|S|-1)/2$). Deliveries are handled identically. Nodes with $z$-degree zero are dropped from the separation graph (they have slack $1$ and can never belong to a violated set).

Implementation: [CapacityBlossomCuts.h](../SPDP_codes/include/CapacityBlossomCuts.h), [CapacityBlossomCuts.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/CapacityBlossomCuts.cpp). Rows are emitted in the sparse internal form $\sum_{E(S)}y\le\lfloor |S|/2\rfloor$ by default ('--capacity-blossom-row-form inbound' gives the other form). The separator was validated against exhaustive enumeration of all odd subsets on random fractional points of the A8 graph (25 points per family, 13 with planted violated odd cycles), and an integer solution violated no returned cut. That check ran as a temporary 'spdp_cut_module_tests' target, which has since been removed from the tree together with its CTest registration.

### 1.3 Cut management (CVRP-style)

The separator runs in the Gurobi user-cut callback shared by all families ([TwoIndexSolver.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/TwoIndexSolver.cpp)). Root: every root relaxation, at most 32 cuts per round, 256 per root, 20 rounds, stop after two consecutive rounds without a cut or with best violation below $0.02$. Tree ('adaptive-tree'): every node while the node count is below 50, afterwards every 100th node, at most 8 cuts per call, a global cap of 2000 cuts, and separation stops when its accumulated time exceeds 15% of the solver runtime. Emitted sets are kept in a pool so the same set is never added twice; candidates of one round whose Jaccard overlap with an accepted candidate exceeds $0.9$ are skipped.

These rules are not specific to the blossom family. Only candidate generation differs between the two families: the blossom family solves a fresh odd-cut problem, the travel-time family screens the certified rows built during preprocessing. Everything after that is identical, so scope, caps, violation threshold, overlap filter, tailing-off streaks and the separation time budget were factored into a shared 'CutManagementOptions' block ([CutManagement.h](../SPDP_codes/include/CutManagement.h)). Each family owns an independent instance of that block and an independent stop state, so one family closing its root does not stop the other. Every field is a CLI option ('--capacity-blossom-*' and '--type-travel-time-cover-*') and every option is exposed in [run_release_many.sh](../run_release_many.sh).

## 2. Type-closed travel-time cover cuts

### 2.1 Projection argument and why it needs the metric closure

For a set $H$ of skip types let $S_H=\{P_i,D_i: h_i\in H\}$. Take a feasible route and delete every action of a request whose type is not in $H$, keeping the treatment stops of the retained pickups. The result is a feasible route of the **restricted instance** that contains only the $H$ requests: the onboard set is a subset of the original one, every empty skip consumed by a retained delivery was produced by a retained pickup (same type $\Rightarrow$ same $H$), and the retained treatments still empty the retained skips. Arbitrary request subsets do not have this property (a delivery may use a skip picked up by another request of the same type), which is why $S$ must be type-closed.

Route duration is the one place where the argument can fail: the instances are non-metric (A8: 162 ordered location pairs with a shorter two-leg path, up to 11 minutes shorter). Deleting an intermediate stop $x$ replaces $t_{ax}+t_{xb}$ by $t_{ab}$, which can be *longer*. The restricted instance therefore uses the **metric closure** $\hat t_{ab}=$ shortest directed travel time (Dijkstra from every physical location, shared module [ShortestTravelTimes.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/ShortestTravelTimes.cpp); the virtual depot row/column is excluded because its zeros would connect every pair at cost $0$). With $\hat t\le t$ along any path, the projected route is no longer than the original one. Service times are unchanged.

### 2.2 The certified bound

Let $\eta(H)$ be the minimum total route duration of the restricted instance and $\rho(H)=\lceil \eta(H)/T\rceil$. If an integer solution uses $r_H$ routes that touch $S_H$, their projections form a restricted solution of total duration at most $r_H T$, hence $r_H\ge\rho(H)$, and every such route enters $S_H$ at least once:

$$
\sum_{e\in\delta^-(S_H)} y_e\ \ge\ \rho(H)
\qquad\forall\,\emptyset\ne H\subsetneq\{\text{types}\}.
\tag{2}
$$

$\eta(H)$ is **not** solved to optimality. The restricted duration IP (big-$M$ duration rows, DurationBound graph with the min-duration parallel-edge and empty-connector pruning) runs with the rounded-bound callback already used for $k_{\min}$: with $H_{LB}$ the tolerance-corrected solver bound and $H_{UB}$ the exact duration of the incumbent, it stops as soon as $\lceil H_{LB}/T\rceil=\lceil H_{UB}/T\rceil$, which certifies $\rho(H)$. $\lceil H_{LB}/T\rceil$ is valid on its own, but only masks that reached this certificate are turned into rows; masks that hit the time limit without it are logged and dropped. The full type set is skipped (its row is the departure bound of VI44). Every run recomputes the certificates from scratch: the certificate cache used during the experiments below was removed so that no run depends on a value produced by an earlier one. The preprocessing time is charged to the instance budget and reported separately.

Implementation: [TypeTravelTimeCoverCuts.h](../SPDP_codes/include/TypeTravelTimeCoverCuts.h), [TypeTravelTimeCoverCuts.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/TypeTravelTimeCoverCuts.cpp). Rows are used either statically or **screened**: at every node relaxation the not-yet-added rows are evaluated and the violated ones (at most 8 per call) are added as user cuts. The duration IP wrapper 'solve_duration_rounded_bound' lives in [KMinComputation.cpp](../SPDP_codes/src/KMinComputation.cpp). This wrapper, the $k_{\min}$ duration IP and the fixed-$K$ incumbent MILP all use the big-$M$ formulation unconditionally: it is the smaller model, it solves faster on every instance tested, and fixing it keeps the certified bound independent of the main model choice. The time-flow selection remains an option of the main MILP only.

On A8 the preprocessing takes $1.1$ s for the 14 proper type unions; two of them have $\rho=2$ (types $\{0,3,4\}$ with 14 requests, $\eta\in[567,567]$; types $\{0,4,7\}$ with 12 requests, $\eta\ge 503$), the remaining twelve have $\rho=1$.

### 3.1 The separation timing problem and the root LP cut phase

The first round of experiments showed that enabling the two families inside the branch-and-cut callback changed almost nothing on the large instances. The reason is timing, not validity. On instances such as C13 and D7 the root node alone consumes the whole time limit, so Gurobi issues very few `MIPNODE` callbacks and the separators are never asked for a violated row while the bound is still moving.

The fix is to separate before the MIP starts. The new `--root-lp-cut-phase` option solves the LP relaxation of the same model, runs every enabled separator to closure on the fractional point, and adds the resulting rows to the MIP as ordinary constraints. The phase is bounded by `--root-lp-cut-phase-time-limit` and its runtime is subtracted from the main solve budget, so the comparison stays time-fair.

This is where the families actually act. The LP value before the phase reproduces the plateau reported in note 13: on C13 it is exactly $5230$, on D7 it is $6278.33$, on D5 it is $4908.85$ and on D6 it is $4876.25$, all equal to the bound the previous configuration converged to. The separated rows move the model above that plateau.

### 3.4 Which setting to use

P5 is the recommended configuration. It has the lowest mean gap and the largest total lower-bound gain, and it is best or tied on 12 of the 18 instances. On the instances where the fixed-$K$ conditional gap was the binding difficulty the bound rises by $+8$ on D7, $+7$ on C13, $+6$ on C14, $+5$ on D6, $+3$ on C9 and $+1$ on C8, and the upper bound improves on the same instances.

The ablation separates the two families cleanly. Capacity blossom alone (P2) supplies most of the bound strength, $+22$ of the $+26$ total. The travel-time cover alone (PT) is a net loss on the bound, $-11$, because it spends root LP time on rows that the blossom closure would have produced anyway; its low mean gap comes almost entirely from one lucky incumbent on A17, where the upper bound drops from $2144$ to $2102$ while the lower bound moves by $-1$. Running both together is what produces the best result, so the cover is worth keeping as a complement rather than as a replacement.

Adding connectivity cuts, as in P6, is a net loss. It gives the best bound on C9 and C13 but costs enough search time on C14 and D5 to push the mean gap above the baseline and to lose one proved optimum on B5. The connectivity separation competes with the two new families for the same root LP budget without cutting off much that they miss.

The small instances A8 through B8 and C4 are closed by every configuration, so they carry no information about the cuts. A17 is the one instance where none of the relaxation work helps: all configurations reach $2094$--$2096$, so its difficulty lies somewhere other than the fixed-$K$ conditional gap.

For reference, the first round at a 120 s budget without the root LP phase showed why the phase was needed. There R0 proved 6 optima with a mean gap of $1.274\%$, and the callback-only variants R0b, R2, R5 and T5 all failed to improve the total bound, scoring $-22$, $-29$, $-17$ and $-11$ respectively against R0. The families only became useful once separation moved ahead of the MIP.

## 4. Final state of the code

The implementation was consolidated after the experiments above, so the option surface no longer matches the configuration names used in section 3.

Connectivity cuts were removed entirely, along with 'ConnectivityCuts.{h,cpp}' and the four '--connectivity-cut-*' options. Section 3.4 already showed them to be a net loss, and they competed with the two remaining families for the same root budget.

Root cut processing is now described by two independent choices instead of a single flag. '--root-cut-mode' selects where the root cuts are separated: 'pre-mip' runs the cut loop on a standalone continuous LP and copies the resulting rows into the MIP as ordinary constraints, 'callback' separates at the root node of the MIP. The LP algorithm of each model is chosen separately through '--pre-mip-method', '--pre-mip-crossover', '--mip-method', '--mip-node-method' and '--mip-crossover', using the Gurobi values directly ($-1$ automatic, $0$ primal simplex, $1$ dual simplex, $2$ barrier, $4$ deterministic concurrent; 'NodeMethod' accepts $-1,0,1,2$). Crossover may only be switched off together with barrier, which the CLI enforces. In 'pre-mip' mode the standalone LP writes its own Gurobi log next to the main one, so the LP algorithm comparison can be read without the MIP log in the way. The pre-MIP time is always subtracted from the main solve budget.

The main model's duration formulation is now two independent switches, '--add-time-flow-formulation' (0 off, 1 node conservation, 2 node-state conservation) and '--add-big-m-time-constraints', so TF1 and TF2 can each be run with or without the big-$M$ rows. Turning both off is rejected, since the route-duration limit would then be unenforced.

A single '--instance-time-limit' bounds the whole instance. Preprocessing, the VI44 duration IP, the travel-time cover duration IPs, the incumbent MILP, the pre-MIP LP and the main MIP all draw from it, and each phase receives the smaller of its own limit and the remaining budget. An exhausted budget stops the next phase immediately rather than granting it a final second, which the earlier implementation did.

## 5. Choosing the root cut processing options

Section 4 introduced seven options that decide where the root cuts are separated and which LP algorithm solves each model. This section measures them on the consolidated build. The cut configuration is fixed to P5 throughout, so only '--root-cut-mode', '--pre-mip-root-lp-time-limit', '--pre-mip-method', '--pre-mip-crossover', '--mip-method', '--mip-node-method' and '--mip-crossover' vary.

### 5.1 Protocol

Fixed base: $p=0$ direct two-index IP, original cost, $K=k_{\min}$ fixed, VI44 on, VI35 and the legacy VI36 rows off, TF2 with the big-$M$ rows removed, capacity blossom cuts on the adaptive tree, screened type-closed travel-time cover cuts over all type unions at the root, duration-MILP incumbent with a 60 s limit, 120 s main solve, 6 Gurobi threads, four runs in parallel on a 28-core machine. '--instance-time-limit' is 0, so preprocessing, the $k_{\min}$ duration IP, the cover certificates and the incumbent are charged outside the solve budget and every configuration receives the same 120 s for root processing plus branch-and-bound.

Six instances were selected after a probe run. B1 and A13 are proved within the limit and therefore measure proof speed; B5 and C4 reach the tree and end with a small gap; C8 and C9 spend the entire limit at the root and therefore isolate the root LP algorithm. A8 and A9 were dropped because every configuration closes them in under 6 s, and A17 was dropped because no relaxation work moves its bound.

Three stages were run, 300 solver runs in total. Stage 1 compared the 21 admissible main-MIP algorithm combinations under '--root-cut-mode callback'. Stage 2 crossed the six pre-MIP LP profiles with the four main-MIP profiles that stage 1 selected. Stage 3 varied '--pre-mip-root-lp-time-limit' over $15$, $30$ and $60$ s and repeated the three leading configurations to estimate run-to-run variance.

Because the configurations do not all find the same incumbent, the ranking uses a common reference. With $\widehat z_i=\min_c UB_{c,i}$ the best upper bound observed on instance $i$ across every run, the reported dual gap is

$$
g^{LB}_{c,i}=100\,\frac{\widehat z_i-LB_{c,i}}{\widehat z_i}.
$$

Primal simplex at the root is the only outright failure. On C9 it does not finish the root relaxation inside the limit and the bound stays at $2500$ against $3890$ for every other method, a $36.5\%$ dual gap. It should never be selected.

The remaining methods split by instance type rather than dominating one another. Barrier at the root produces the strongest bound on the two root-dominated instances, $2997$ on C8 and $3906$ on C9 against $2993$ and $3901$ for the automatic setting, but it fails to prove B1 and A13, which the automatic setting proves in 26 s and 86 s. Barrier at the tree nodes is consistently harmful once branching starts: on C4 it processes $378$ to $1606$ nodes where simplex processes $7500$ to $12000$, and the bound drops accordingly. The best overall callback configuration is automatic root with dual simplex nodes, at a mean dual gap of $0.588\%$.

Stage 1 therefore carried four main-MIP profiles into stage 2: automatic/automatic, automatic/dual, barrier/dual and barrier/barrier without crossover.

It has the lowest mean dual gap, is best or tied on every instance except the proof time of B1, and is the only configuration that proved B5 optimal in one of its two runs. Replacing '--mip-node-method 1' by $-1$ changes the mean gap by $0.014$ percentage points, so the node method is a weak preference rather than a requirement.

Stage 3 shows the pre-MIP budget is not binding for this profile. Barrier without crossover converges in 1--16 s on all six instances, and $15$, $30$ and $60$ s produce the same bounds. The $30$ s default is kept because it leaves room for the larger instances that were not part of this comparison. A budget only matters for the automatic and primal profiles, which is another reason not to use them.

### 5.5 Limits of this comparison

The repeated runs put the noise floor at $\pm 1$ to $\pm 2$ units of lower bound. B5 changes between $1140$, $1141$ and a proved $1143$ across runs of the same configuration, and C4 between $1478$ and $1479$. Only the C8 difference, $2993$ for callback against $2995$--$2996$ for pre-mip in every run, is larger than this floor and reproducible. The conclusion is therefore that pre-mip with barrier and no crossover is the better default, not that it is better on every instance.

Two further caveats apply. The comparison uses six instances of at most 50 requests at a 120 s limit, so it does not establish the same ranking for the 100-request instances, where the root LP is far more expensive and the pre-MIP budget could become binding. And C8 is an unusual case in this set: its incumbent MILP found no solution within 60 s, so 14 of the 21 callback configurations ended without any feasible solution. Its lower bounds are comparable across configurations, but its upper bounds are not.
