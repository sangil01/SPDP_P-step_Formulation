## Problem description

- Homogeneous Service (ER): empty-and-return tasks only
- Multiple landfills
- Total of $n$ requests
- Each request $r$ is defined by

$$
\{p_r, e_r, d_r, \phi_r\},
$$

$\quad$ where $p_r$, $e_r$, and $d_r$ denote the pick-up, emptying, and delivery locations, and $\phi_r$ is the container type. ($p_r = d_r$)

- Requests may share identical attributes, for example the **same emptying location.**
- Homogeneous fleet with capacity $Q = 2$, maximum duration $T$, and open routes
- Service times are included:

$$
s_{\text{pick-up}}, \quad s_{\text{emptying}}, \quad s_{\text{delivery}}
$$

- Objective: minimize total travel distance plus a fixed cost per vehicle

## Fixed return vs. flexible return

### Fixed return

- Pick-up location must equal delivery location.
- Sequence is strictly maintained:

$$
\text{Pick-up} \rightarrow \text{Emptying} \rightarrow \text{Delivery}
$$

### Flexible return

- The number of pick-up requests equals the number of delivery requests for each $\{\text{location}, \text{type}\}$ pair.
- This allows sequences such as

$$
\text{Delivery} \rightarrow \text{Pick-up} \rightarrow \text{Emptying},
$$

$\quad$  which corresponds to a switch-out (SO) operation using empty containers from different requests of the same type.

## Flow-based MILP with valid inequalities (COR (2022) version)

### Problem setting

- Requests: $R = \{1, \ldots, n\}$.
- Locations: $L$.
- Tractors: sufficiently large set $K$ with $|K| \ge n$.
- Route duration limit: $T$.
- Service times:
  - $s^p$: pick-up service time
  - $s^e$: emptying service time at treatment facility
  - $s^d$: delivery service time
- For each request $r \in R$:
  - $\alpha(r) \in L$: pick-up location
  - $\beta(r) \in L$: treatment location
  - $\omega(r) \in L$: delivery location
  - $\phi(r) \in F$: skip type
- Flexible return balance condition:

$$
\forall l \in L,\ \forall f \in F,\quad
|\{r \in R : \alpha(r)=l,\ \phi(r)=f\}|
=
|\{r \in R : \omega(r)=l,\ \phi(r)=f\}|.
$$

- 즉, type/location별로 pick-up한 full container 수와 delivery해야 하는 empty container 수가 같아야 한다.
- Objective: transport cost + tractor fixed cost $\bar c$ 최소화.

### Notation

- Directed graph:

$$
G(N,A)
$$

- Dummy origin and destination:

$$
\{0,d\} = \{0,3n+1\}
$$

- Service node sets:

$$
N_P = \{1,\ldots,n\},\quad
N_T = \{n+1,\ldots,2n\},\quad
N_D = \{2n+1,\ldots,3n\}.
$$

- Total node set:

$$
N = \{0,d\} \cup N_P \cup N_T \cup N_D.
$$

- For each request $i$:
  - $i \in N_P$: pick-up of request $i$
  - $n+i \in N_T$: emptying/treatment of request $i$
  - $2n+i \in N_D$: delivery of an empty skip for request $i$
- Arc set $A$:
- ![[Pasted image 20260702154259.png|465]]
  - $0 \rightarrow N_P$: route starts with a pick-up.
  - $0 \rightarrow d$: unused tractor.
  - $N_D \rightarrow d$: open route ends after a delivery.
  - Internal arcs allow transitions among $N_P,N_T,N_D$ except arcs that immediately contradict the same request's operation order.

- Arc attributes:
  - $c_{ij}$: transport cost on arc $(i,j)$.
  - $t_{ij}$: travel time on arc $(i,j)$.
  - Open route, unused tractor

$$
c_{id}=t_{id}=0\quad i \in N_D,\qquad c_{0d}=t_{0d}=0.
$$

- Service time at node $i$:

$$
s_i =
\begin{cases}
s^p, & i \in N_P,\\
s^e, & i \in N_T,\\
s^d, & i \in N_D,\\
0, & i \in \{0,d\}.
\end{cases}
$$

- Minimum remaining total service time for treat a request from node $i$:

$$
\bar{s}_i =
\begin{cases}
s_i+s_{n+i}+s_{2n+i}, & i \in N_P,\\
s_i+s_{n+i}, & i \in N_T,\\
s_i, & i \in N_D,\\
0, & i \in \{0,d\}.
\end{cases}
$$

### Load-state parameters

To keep track of skips on the tractor, the flexible-return model separates full skips by request and empty skips by type.

- Full skip state parameter:

$$
q_i^r =
\begin{cases}
1, & i \in N_P,\ r=i,\\
-1, & i \in N_T,\ r=i-n,\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Interpretation:
- at pick-up node $i=r$, request $r$'s full skip count increases by 1.
- at treatment node $i=r+n$, request $r$'s full skip count decreases by 1.

- Empty skip state parameter:

$$
u_i^f =
\begin{cases}
1, & i \in N_T,\ \phi(i-n)=f,\\
-1, & i \in N_D,\ \phi(i-2n)=f,\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Interpretation:
- after emptying at $N_T$, one empty skip of that type is created.
- after delivery at $N_D$, one empty skip of that type is removed.

### Variables

- Arc-use variable:

$$
x_{ij} =
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{arc }(i,j)\text{ is used},\\
0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\quad (i,j) \in A \setminus \{(0,d)\}.
$$

$x_{0d}$ is the number of unused tractors and is integer.

- Arrival time:

$$
B_i = \text{time when a tractor arrives at node } i,\quad i \in N,
$$

with $B_0 = 0$.

- Full skip state:

$$
Q_i^r = \text{fraction of full skip of request } r
\text{ on the tractor when it leaves node } i.
$$

- Empty skip state:

$$
U_i^f = \text{fraction of empty skips of type } f
\text{ on the tractor when it leaves node } i.
$$

Although $Q_i^r$ and $U_i^f$ are continuous, the constraints force them to behave like binary/integer state variables.

Fixed values:

$$
Q_0^r = Q_d^r = 0\quad r \in R,
$$

$$
U_0^f = U_d^f = 0\quad f \in F,
$$

$$
Q_i^r = 1\quad i \in N_P,\ i=r,
$$

$$
Q_i^r = 0\quad i \in N_T,\ i=r+n.
$$

### MILP: flexible return model

Objective:

$$
\min
\sum_{(i,j)\in A} c_{ij}x_{ij}
+
\sum_{j\in N_P} \bar c x_{0j}
\tag{19}
$$

Flow constraints:

$$
\sum_{j\in N_P \cup \{d\}} x_{0j} = |K|
\tag{2}
$$

$$
\sum_{i\in N_D \cup \{0\}} x_{id} = |K|
\tag{3}
$$

$$
\sum_{j:(i,j)\in A} x_{ij} = 1
\quad i \in N \setminus \{0,d\}
\tag{4}
$$

$$
\sum_{j:(j,i)\in A} x_{ji} = 1
\quad i \in N \setminus \{0,d\}
\tag{5}
$$

$$
x_{ij}+x_{ji}\le 1
\quad i,j\in N:\ (i,j),(j,i)\in A
\tag{6}
$$

Time constraints:

$$
B_0 = 0
\tag{7}
$$

$$
B_i+s_i+t_{ij}-B_j \le (1-x_{ij})M
\quad (i,j)\in A,\ j\ne d
\tag{9}
$$

$$
0 \le B_i \le T-\bar{s}_i
\quad i \in N \setminus \{0,d\}
\tag{16}
$$

For flexible return, pick-up must occur before emptying:

$$
B_i \le B_{n+i}
\quad i \in N_P.
\tag{22}
$$

Capacity:

$$
0 \le
\sum_{r\in R} Q_i^r + \sum_{f\in F} U_i^f
\le 2
\quad i \in N \setminus \{0,d\}.
\tag{23}
$$

Full skip state:

$$
Q_j^r \ge Q_i^r + q_j^r - (1-x_{ij})
\quad (i,j)\in A\setminus\{(0,d)\},\ r\in R
\tag{24}
$$

$$
Q_j^r \le Q_i^r + q_j^r + (1-x_{ij})
\quad (i,j)\in A\setminus\{(0,d)\},\ r\in R
\tag{25}
$$

$$
Q_0^r = 0\quad r\in R
\tag{26}
$$

$$
Q_d^r = 0\quad r\in R
\tag{27}
$$

$$
0 \le Q_i^r \le 1
\quad i\in N,\ r\in R.
\tag{28}
$$

Empty skip state:

$$
U_j^f \ge U_i^f + u_j^f - M(1-x_{ij})
\quad (i,j)\in A\setminus\{(0,d)\},\ f\in F
\tag{29}
$$

$$
U_j^f \le U_i^f + u_j^f + M(1-x_{ij})
\quad (i,j)\in A\setminus\{(0,d)\},\ f\in F
\tag{30}
$$

$$
U_0^f = 0\quad f\in F
\tag{31}
$$

$$
U_d^f = 0\quad f\in F
\tag{32}
$$

$$
0 \le U_i^f \le 2
\quad i\in N,\ f\in F.
\tag{33}
$$

Domains:

$$
x_{ij}\in\{0,1\}
\quad (i,j)\in A\setminus\{(0,d)\}
\tag{17}
$$

$$
x_{0d}\ge 0,\quad x_{0d}\in \mathbb{Z}.
\tag{18}
$$


### Valid inequalities

#### Pattern elimination

Because tractor capacity is 2, the model can eliminate impossible local action patterns:
- more than two pick-ups in a row.
- pick-up, pick-up, delivery.
- emptying immediately before two pick-ups.
- more than two treatment/emptying actions in a row.
- more than two deliveries in a row.
- corresponding impossible before/after patterns for $N_T$ and $N_D$.

Representative inequalities:

$$
\sum_{\substack{l\in N_P\\l\ne i}} x_{li}
+
\sum_{\substack{j\in N_P\cup N_D\\j\ne i}} x_{ij}
\le 1
\quad i\in N_P
\tag{34a}
$$

$$
\sum_{\substack{l\in N_P\cup N_T\\l\ne i}} x_{li}
+
\sum_{\substack{j\in N_P\\j\ne i}} x_{ij}
\le 1
\quad i\in N_P
\tag{34b}
$$

$$
\sum_{\substack{l\in N_T\\l\ne i}} x_{li}
+
\sum_{\substack{j\in N_P\cup N_T\\j\ne i}} x_{ij}
\le 1
\quad i\in N_T
\tag{34c}
$$

$$
\sum_{\substack{l\in N_T\cup N_D\\l\ne i}} x_{li}
+
\sum_{\substack{j\in N_T\\j\ne i}} x_{ij}
\le 1
\quad i\in N_T
\tag{34d}
$$

$$
\sum_{\substack{l\in N_D\\l\ne i}} x_{li}
+
\sum_{\substack{j\in N_T\cup N_D\\j\ne i}} x_{ij}
\le 1
\quad i\in N_D
\tag{34e}
$$

$$
\sum_{\substack{l\in N_D\cup N_P\\l\ne i}} x_{li}
+
\sum_{\substack{j\in N_D\\j\ne i}} x_{ij}
\le 1
\quad i\in N_D.
\tag{34f}
$$

#### Route-count lower bound from capacity

Since each tractor can carry at most two skips, at least $\lceil n/2\rceil$ transitions of each action type are needed:

$$
\sum_{i\in N_P}\sum_{j\in N_T\cup N_D\cup\{0\}} x_{ji}
\ge \lceil n/2\rceil
\tag{35a}
$$

$$
\sum_{i\in N_P}\sum_{j\in N_T\cup N_D} x_{ij}
\ge \lceil n/2\rceil
\tag{35b}
$$

$$
\sum_{i\in N_T}\sum_{j\in N_P\cup N_D} x_{ji}
\ge \lceil n/2\rceil
\tag{35c}
$$

$$
\sum_{i\in N_T}\sum_{j\in N_P\cup N_D} x_{ij}
\ge \lceil n/2\rceil
\tag{35d}
$$

$$
\sum_{i\in N_D}\sum_{j\in N_P\cup N_T} x_{ji}
\ge \lceil n/2\rceil
\tag{35e}
$$

$$
\sum_{i\in N_D}\sum_{j\in N_P\cup N_T\cup\{d\}} x_{ij}
\ge \lceil n/2\rceil.
\tag{35f}
$$

For each physical location $l$, the same lower-bound idea is also applied to the subset of action nodes at that location:

$$
N_{P|l}=\{i\in N_P:\alpha(i)=l\},\quad
N_{T|l}=\{i\in N_T:\beta(i-n)=l\},\quad
N_{D|l}=\{i\in N_D:\omega(i-2n)=l\}.
$$

$$
\sum_{i\in N_{P|l}}
\sum_{j\in \{0\}\cup (N_P\setminus N_{P|l})\cup N_T\cup N_D}
x_{ji}
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{|N_{P|l}|}{2}\right\rceil
\quad l\in L
\tag{36a}
$$

$$
\sum_{i\in N_{T|l}}
\sum_{j\in N_P\cup (N_T\setminus N_{T|l})\cup N_D}
x_{ji}
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{|N_{T|l}|}{2}\right\rceil
\quad l\in L
\tag{36b}
$$

$$
\sum_{i\in N_{D|l}}
\sum_{j\in N_P\cup N_T\cup (N_D\setminus N_{D|l})}
x_{ji}
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{|N_{D|l}|}{2}\right\rceil
\quad l\in L.
\tag{36c}
$$

These prevent the relaxation from serving many same-location actions with too few route entries.

#### Lifted subtour elimination

For $i,j\in N_T$, $i\ne j$:

$$
x_{ij}+x_{i,j-n}+x_{ji}+x_{j,i-n}\le 1.
\tag{37a}
$$

$$
x_{ij}+x_{i+n,j}+x_{ji}+x_{j+n,i}\le 1
\quad i,j\in N_P,\ i\ne j.
\tag{39}
$$

#### Symmetry breaking

When consecutive actions are equivalent, impose an index order:

$$
x_{ij}=0
\quad i,j\in N_P,\ \alpha(i)=\alpha(j),\ i>j
\tag{40a}
$$

$$
x_{ij}=0
\quad i,j\in N_T,\ \beta(i)=\beta(j),\ i>j
\tag{40b}
$$

$$
x_{ij}=0
\quad i,j\in N_D,\ \omega(i)=\omega(j),\ i>j.
\tag{40c}
$$

Also forbid action transitions at the same physical location that are clearly unnecessary:

$$
x_{ij}=0
\quad i\in N_P,\ j\in N_D,\ \alpha(i)=\omega(j)
\tag{41a}
$$

$$
x_{ij}=0
\quad i\in N_T,\ j\in N_P,\ \beta(i)=\alpha(j)
\tag{41b}
$$

$$
x_{ij}=0
\quad i\in N_D,\ j\in N_T,\ \omega(i)=\beta(j).
\tag{41c}
$$

For flexible return, if two pick-up requests are identical in pick-up location, treatment location, and type, order them by arrival time:

$$
B_i\le B_j
\quad
\forall i,j\in N_P,\ i<j,\ \alpha(i)=\alpha(j),\ \beta(i)=\beta(j),\ \phi(i)=\phi(j).
\tag{43}
$$

#### Tractor lower bound from duration

For flexible return:

$$
k_{\min}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{
\left(
\sum_{r\in R} t_{\alpha(r),\beta(r)}
+
\sum_{r\in R}
\min_{\substack{r'\in R\\ \phi(r')=\phi(r)}}
t_{\beta(r),\omega(r')}
\right)/2
+
\sum_{i\in N}s_i
}{T}
\right\rceil.
$$

Then add:

$$
\sum_{j\in N_P} x_{0j} \ge k_{\min}.
\tag{44}
$$

## Action based multi graph modeling (Our version)

### Node set

For the flexible-return version, landfill visits are embedded into arcs, so the node set becomes

$$
V = \{0, d\} \cup C_P \cup C_D,
$$

where

$$
\{0, d\} = \{0, 2n+1\},
$$

$$
C_P = \{1, \ldots, n\},
$$

$$
C_D = \{n+1, \ldots, 2n\}.
$$

### Arc set

$$
A = \text{set of all feasible arcs based on container state}.
$$

- By incorporating landfill visits into arcs, multiple edges may exist between the same pair of nodes.
- These edges are determined by the vehicle state and by the landfill sequence used on the move.
- Example:
  - $c_{p1} \rightarrow c_{p2}$ can be infeasible if the vehicle already carries a container before visiting $c_{p1}$.

## State representation

The vehicle state is

$$
\sigma = \{z_1, z_2\},
$$

where each slot satisfies

$$
z \in \{N\} \cup \{E(h)\} \cup \{F(h,l)\}.
$$

- $N$: empty slot
- $E(h)$: empty container of type $h$
- $F(h,l)$: full container of type $h$ that must be emptied at landfill $l$

## State transition

### Pick-up node $c_p$

- Before service: $N \in \sigma$
- After service:

$$
\sigma \leftarrow \sigma \setminus \{N\} \cup \{F(h_p, l_p)\}
$$

### Delivery node $c_d$

- Before service: $E(h^{c_{d}}) \in \sigma$
- After service:

$$
\sigma \leftarrow \sigma \setminus \{E(h^{c_{d}})\} \cup \{N\}
$$

###  Landfill $l$

A visit to landfill $l$ must empty at least one onboard full container assigned to $l$.

- If exactly one such full container is onboard, it is emptied.
- If two such full containers are onboard, either one of them or both may be emptied.

For each emptied container, the state transition is

$$
F(h,l)\rightarrow E(h).
$$

The emptying service time is $s_{\text{emptying}}$ when one container is emptied and $2s_{\text{emptying}}$ when two containers are emptied.

## Node attributes

Each node stores:

- `id`: unique identifier
- `type`: virtual start/end, pick-up, or delivery
- `location`: physical location where the action occurs, starting from 0
- `request_id`: associated request ID, starting from 0
- `container type`: type of container handled by the action
- `landfill_location`: designated landfill for the corresponding request (only pick-up node)

## Arc attributes

Each arc is represented by

$$
e = \{u, v, \pi, \sigma_u, \sigma_v, t_e, c_e\}.
$$

- $(u,v)$: origin and destination node IDs
- $\pi$: sequence of physical landfill locations visited on the move; it does not identify the emptied requests
- $\sigma_u, \sigma_v$: state **after servicing** $u$ and $v$
- $t_e$: travel time along $u\to\pi\to v$, plus the emptying service time inside the arc, plus the service time of $v$; it does not include the service time of $u$
- $c_e$: travel cost along $u\to\pi\to v$ (if $u=0$, the vehicle fixed cost is also included)
- key: identifier for multiple edges sharing the same $(u,v)$ pair (for implementation)

The emptying service time inside an arc is $s_{\text{emptying}}$ when one container is emptied and $2s_{\text{emptying}}$ when two containers are emptied.

If two containers are emptied at the same landfill $l$, the physical visit is recorded only once:

$$
\pi=(l).
$$

Therefore, $\pi$ records physical landfill visits, not the number or identities of the emptied containers. The same $(u,v,\pi,\sigma_u)$ may generate parallel edges with different end states $\sigma_v$.

The landfill sequence satisfies

$$
\pi \in \{(), (l_1), (l_2), (l_1,l_2), (l_2,l_1)\}.
$$

## Feasible arc generation algorithm

1. Select a node pair $(u,v)$.
2. Identify all feasible post-service states $\sigma_u$ of node $u$.
3. For each state, generate the direct move without emptying.
4. If one full container is onboard, generate the outcome that empties it.
5. If two full containers are onboard, generate the outcomes that empty either one of them or both.
6. For each emptying outcome, enumerate the possible orders of the distinct physical landfill locations.
7. Apply the service at $v$. If the resulting end state $\sigma_v$ is feasible, compute $t_e$ and $c_e$ and generate the edge.
8. Remove duplicate outcomes and apply the infeasibility, symmetry, and dominance rules.
## Prune infeasible arcs

An arc is pruned if one of the following holds:

1. $t_e > T$, where $T$ is the maximum vehicle work time
2. $\sigma_u$ or $\sigma_v$ contains two containers of some type $h'$ although only one request of that type exists
3. $\sigma_u$ or $\sigma_v$ contains two full containers of some $(h', l')$ although only one corresponding request exists
4. There is only one request requiring type $h'$, but an arc of the form $(u,v) = (c_d, c_p)$ for that request is generated

If $u$ is a delivery node, $prev(σ_u)$ should be checked for the second and third conditions.

## Prune dominated arcs

For any two arcs $e_1$ and $e_2$, if they have the same state transition,

$$
(u, v, \sigma_u, \sigma_v)^{e_1} = (u, v, \sigma_u, \sigma_v)^{e_2},
$$

and

$$
t_{e_1} \le t_{e_2}, \qquad c_{e_1} \le c_{e_2},
$$

with at least one strict inequality, then $e_1$ dominates $e_2$ and $e_2$ is pruned.

- If both are identical, keep only one representative edge.

## Why using physical emptying locations inside arcs is more compact than using emptying-action identities inside arcs

Consider the following two compressed settings.

### Setting A: physical-location-based compressed setting (current setting)

- full-container token:

$$
F(h,l),
$$

where $h$ is the container type and $l$ is the physical emptying location;

- arc-internal emptying sequence:

$$
\pi = (l_1,\ldots,l_q),
$$

that is, a sequence of physical emptying locations.

### Setting B: emptying-action-based compressed setting

- full-container token:

$$
F(r),
$$

where $r$ identifies the request, or equivalently the emptying action associated with that request;

- arc-internal emptying sequence:

$$
\pi^E = (E_{r_1},\ldots,E_{r_q}),
$$

that is, a sequence of emptying actions.

### Theorem

Here:

- $R$ = request set,
- $h_r$ = container type of request $r$,
- $l_r$ = physical emptying location of request $r$,
- $V^{A},V^{B}$ = service-node sets of Settings A and B,
- $\mathcal F^{A},\mathcal F^{B}$ = full-container token sets of Settings A and B,
- $\mathcal A^{A},\mathcal A^{B}$ = atomic token sets of Settings A and B,
- $\Sigma^{A},\Sigma^{B}$ = corresponding two-slot state spaces,
- $\Pi^{A}(u,\sigma_u,v)$ = feasible physical-location emptying sequences considered when generating an arc from node-state pair $(u,\sigma_u)$ to service node $v$ in Setting A,
- $\Pi^{B}(u,\hat\sigma_u,v)$ = feasible emptying-action sequences considered when generating an arc from node-state pair $(u,\hat\sigma_u)$ to service node $v$ in Setting B,
- $P^{A},P^{B}$ = the maximum number of such feasible sequences over all admissible triples $(u,\sigma_u,v)$ or $(u,\hat\sigma_u,v)$,
- $E^{A},E^{B}$ = sets of candidate edge descriptions before feasibility filtering and pruning.

Assume that the two compressed settings use the same pick-up and delivery node set, so that their service-node sets satisfy

$$
|V^{A}| = |V^{B}|.
$$

Let

$$
\mathcal F^{A} = \{F(h_r,l_r): r\in R\},
\qquad
\mathcal F^{B} = \{F(r): r\in R\},
$$

and define the atomic token sets

$$
\mathcal A^{A}
=
\{N\}
\cup
\{E(h)\}
\cup
\mathcal F^{A},
$$

$$
\mathcal A^{B}
=
\{N\}
\cup
\{E(h)\}
\cup
\mathcal F^{B}.
$$

Let $\Sigma^{A}$ and $\Sigma^{B}$ be the corresponding two-slot state spaces, and let

$$
P^{A} = \max_{u,\sigma_u,v} |\Pi^{A}(u,\sigma_u,v)|,
\qquad
P^{B} = \max_{u,\hat\sigma_u,v} |\Pi^{B}(u,\hat\sigma_u,v)|.
$$

Then:

$$
|\mathcal A^{A}| \le |\mathcal A^{B}|,
\qquad
|\Sigma^{A}| \le |\Sigma^{B}|,
\qquad
P^{A} \le P^{B},
$$

and therefore the number of candidate edge descriptions admits the coarse upper-bound comparison

$$
|E^{A}| \le |V^{A}|^2 |\Sigma^{A}|^2 P^{A}
\le
|V^{B}|^2 |\Sigma^{B}|^2 P^{B}.
$$

In particular, the physical-location-based compressed setting has a no-larger structural upper bound on graph size than the emptying-action-based compressed setting.

#### Proof

First, define the mapping

$$
\varphi : R \to \{(h,l)\},
\qquad
\varphi(r) = (h_r,l_r).
$$

Then $\mathcal F^{A}$ is exactly the image of $R$ under $\varphi$, so

$$
|\mathcal F^{A}| = |\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)| \le |R| = |\mathcal F^{B}|.
$$

Since the sets $\{N\}$ and $\{E(h)\}$ are identical in both settings, this immediately gives

$$
|\mathcal A^{A}| \le |\mathcal A^{B}|.
$$

Now let a token set have size $m$. Because vehicle capacity is $2$, a state is an unordered pair of tokens with repetition allowed. Hence the number of possible states is at most

$$
\frac{m(m+1)}{2}.
$$

Applying this bound to $\mathcal A^{A}$ and $\mathcal A^{B}$ yields

$$
|\Sigma^{A}| \le |\Sigma^{B}|.
$$

Next, fix

- an origin service node $u$,
- a destination service node $v$,
- and any onboard request-level configuration before the internal emptying sequence begins.

Suppose that the onboard full requests in this configuration are

$$
r_1,\ldots,r_q.
$$

Define the projection

$$
\psi(E_r)=l_r,
$$

and extend it componentwise to sequences:

$$
\psi(E_{r_1},\ldots,E_{r_q}) = (l_{r_1},\ldots,l_{r_q}).
$$

Let $\hat\sigma_u$ be the corresponding start state in Setting B, and let $\sigma_u$ be its projection to Setting A obtained by replacing each full token $F(r)$ with $F(h_r,l_r)$.

Then every emptying-action sequence in

$$
\Pi^{B}(u,\hat\sigma_u,v)
$$

induces exactly one physical-location sequence in

$$
\Pi^{A}(u,\sigma_u,v)
$$

via $\psi$. Moreover, different emptying-action sequences may collapse to the same physical-location sequence if different requests share the same emptying location. Thus $\psi$ is many-to-one but never one-to-many, so

$$
|\Pi^{A}(u,\sigma_u,v)|
\le
|\Pi^{B}(u,\hat\sigma_u,v)|.
$$

Taking the maximum over admissible triples $(u,\sigma_u,v)$ and $(u,\hat\sigma_u,v)$ gives

$$
P^{A} \le P^{B}.
$$

Finally, before feasibility filtering, a candidate edge description is determined by:

1. an origin node $u$,
2. a destination node $v$,
3. a start state $\sigma_u$,
4. an end state $\sigma_v$,
5. an embedded emptying sequence.

Therefore, in either setting,

$$
|E| \le |V|^2 |\Sigma|^2 P.
$$

Applying this to Settings A and B gives

$$
|E^{A}| \le |V^{A}|^2 |\Sigma^{A}|^2 P^{A},
\qquad
|E^{B}| \le |V^{B}|^2 |\Sigma^{B}|^2 P^{B}.
$$

Combining

$$
|V^{A}| = |V^{B}|, \qquad
|\Sigma^{A}| \le |\Sigma^{B}|, \qquad
P^{A} \le P^{B}
$$

proves

$$
|E^{A}| \le |V^{A}|^2 |\Sigma^{A}|^2 P^{A}
\le
|V^{B}|^2 |\Sigma^{B}|^2 P^{B}.
$$

This proves the theorem. $\square$

### Interpretation

The theorem does **not** claim that the final feasible graph after all pruning steps must always be strictly smaller in practice.
What it proves is narrower and cleaner:

- the physical-location-based compressed setting has a no-larger atomic token set,
- a no-larger state-space upper bound,
- a no-larger embedded-sequence upper bound,
- and therefore a no-larger coarse upper bound on candidate graph size,

relative to the compressed setting that keeps emptying-action identities inside arcs.

### Stronger statement for the raw candidate graph

The previous theorem only compares coarse upper bounds.  
For the **raw candidate graph before feasibility filtering and pruning**, one can go one step further.

Let

$$
E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{A}, \qquad E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{B}
$$

denote the sets of raw candidate edge descriptions in Settings A and B, respectively, under the following convention:

- states are generated purely from the token alphabet with capacity $2$,
- embedded emptying sequences are generated purely from the currently onboard full tokens $= \sigma(u)$,
- no feasibility filtering, multiplicity filtering, symmetry pruning, or dominance pruning is applied yet.

### Proposition

Under the above raw-enumeration convention, there exists a surjective mapping

$$
\Gamma : E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{B} \twoheadrightarrow E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{A}.
$$

Consequently,

$$
|E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{A}| \le |E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{B}|.
$$

#### Proof

Define the token projection

$$
\rho(N)=N,
\qquad
\rho(E(h))=E(h),
\qquad
\rho(F(r))=F(h_r,l_r).
$$

Extend $\rho$ componentwise to two-slot states.

Also define the sequence projection

$$
\psi(E_r)=l_r,
$$

and extend it componentwise to emptying-action sequences.

Now take any raw candidate edge description in Setting B:

$$
e^{B}=(u,v,\hat\sigma_u,\pi^{E},\hat\sigma_v).
$$

Map it to

$$
\Gamma(e^{B})
=
\big(u,\; v,\; \rho(\hat\sigma_u),\; \psi(\pi^{E}),\; \rho(\hat\sigma_v)\big).
$$

This is a well-defined raw candidate edge description in Setting A, because Setting A is exactly the coarser representation obtained by forgetting request identities inside full tokens and inside emptying sequences.

So $\Gamma$ is a mapping from $E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{B}$ to $E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{A}$.

It remains to show surjectivity.

Take any raw candidate edge description in Setting A:

$$
e^{A}=(u,v,\sigma_u,\pi,\sigma_v).
$$

By construction of the token set in Setting A, every full token

$$
F(h,l)
$$

appearing in $\sigma_u$ or $\sigma_v$ comes from at least one request $r$ such that

$$
h_r=h,\qquad l_r=l.
$$

Likewise, every location entry $l$ appearing in $\pi$ comes from at least one emptying action $E_r$ with

$$
l_r=l.
$$

Since we are still in the raw, pre-filtered setting, we may choose such requests independently for each occurrence of a full token or a location in the sequence. This yields:

- a lifted request-level start state $\hat\sigma_u$ such that $\rho(\hat\sigma_u)=\sigma_u$,
- a lifted request-level end state $\hat\sigma_v$ such that $\rho(\hat\sigma_v)=\sigma_v$,
- a lifted emptying-action sequence $\pi^E$ such that $\psi(\pi^E)=\pi$.

Hence there exists

$$
e^{B}=(u,v,\hat\sigma_u,\pi^{E},\hat\sigma_v)
$$

with

$$
\Gamma(e^{B})=e^{A}.
$$

Therefore $\Gamma$ is surjective, and thus

$$
|E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{A}| \le |E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{B}|.
$$

This proves the claim. $\square$

### Consequence for the start-fixed forward pricing problem

The above compactness result can be extended to the **raw forward pricing search space** when the source node-state pair

$$
(s,\sigma_s)
$$

is fixed.

Let

$$
\mathcal{L}^{A}_{k}(s,\sigma_s), \qquad \mathcal{L}^{B}_{k}(s,\hat\sigma_s)
$$

denote the sets of raw forward partial paths (equivalently, raw labels before feasibility filtering, symmetry pruning, and dominance pruning) of length $k$ starting from the fixed source state in Settings A and B, respectively.

We compare a request-level source state $\hat\sigma_s$ in Setting B and its projected source state

$$
\sigma_s = \rho(\hat\sigma_s)
$$

in Setting A.

### Proposition

For every depth $k \in \{0,1,\dots,p\}$, there exists a surjective mapping

$$
\Gamma_k : \mathcal{L}^{B}_{k}(s,\hat\sigma_s) \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{L}^{A}_{k}(s,\sigma_s).
$$

Consequently,

$$
|\mathcal{L}^{A}_{k}(s,\sigma_s)| \le |\mathcal{L}^{B}_{k}(s,\hat\sigma_s)|
\qquad
\forall k=0,1,\dots,p.
$$

Hence the current physical-location-based model has a no-larger raw start-fixed forward pricing search space.

#### Proof

We prove the claim by induction on the depth $k$.

For $k=0$, each search space contains exactly one trivial label, namely the initial state itself. So

$$
|\mathcal{L}^{A}_{0}(s,\sigma_s)| = |\mathcal{L}^{B}_{0}(s,\hat\sigma_s)| = 1,
$$

and the surjective map is immediate.

Assume now that, for some $k < p$, there exists a surjective map

$$
\Gamma_k : \mathcal{L}^{B}_{k}(s,\hat\sigma_s) \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{L}^{A}_{k}(s,\sigma_s).
$$

Take any raw label in Setting A at depth $k+1$. It must be obtained by extending some raw label

$$
\ell_k^{A} \in \mathcal{L}^{A}_{k}(s,\sigma_s)
$$

with some raw candidate edge

$$
e^{A} \in E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{A}.
$$

By the induction hypothesis, $\ell_k^{A}$ has at least one preimage

$$
\ell_k^{B} \in \mathcal{L}^{B}_{k}(s,\hat\sigma_s)
$$

such that

$$
\Gamma_k(\ell_k^{B}) = \ell_k^{A}.
$$

By the previous proposition for raw candidate edges, the edge $e^{A}$ also has at least one preimage

$$
e^{B} \in E_{\mathrm{raw}}^{B}
$$

such that

$$
\Gamma(e^{B}) = e^{A}.
$$

Extending $\ell_k^{B}$ by $e^{B}$ gives a raw depth-$(k+1)$ label in Setting B whose projection is exactly the given raw depth-$(k+1)$ label in Setting A. Therefore every element of

$$
\mathcal{L}^{A}_{k+1}(s,\sigma_s)
$$

has a preimage in

$$
\mathcal{L}^{B}_{k+1}(s,\hat\sigma_s),
$$

which proves surjectivity of $\Gamma_{k+1}$.

By induction, the claim holds for all $k=0,1,\dots,p$. Hence

$$
|\mathcal{L}^{A}_{k}(s,\sigma_s)| \le |\mathcal{L}^{B}_{k}(s,\hat\sigma_s)|
\qquad
\forall k=0,1,\dots,p.
$$

This proves the proposition. $\square$

### Interpretation

This proposition does **not** say that the actual number of labels processed by the implemented pricing algorithm must always be smaller, because the implemented algorithm additionally applies:

- feasibility filtering,
- elementary and symmetry restrictions,
- dominance pruning,
- and reduced-cost-based screening.

What the proposition proves is the following narrower structural statement:

- for a fixed source node-state pair $(s,\sigma_s)$,
- the current physical-location-based compression yields a no-larger **raw** forward search space than the compressed model that keeps emptying-action identities inside arcs.

### Why we compress emptying but not pick-up / delivery

This compactness argument supports compressing **emptying** into arcs, but it does **not** suggest doing the same for pick-up and delivery.

Although emptying no longer has a single uniform transition, its possible transitions remain simple and local.

- If exactly one onboard full container can be emptied at the visited landfill, it is emptied.
- If two onboard full containers can be emptied there, either one of them or both may be emptied.

For each emptied container, the state transition is

$$
F(h,l)\rightarrow E(h),
$$

while every unselected container remains unchanged.

In contrast, if pick-up and delivery were also compressed to physical locations, then the transition at a location would be substantially more complicated. One would need to decide:

- whether the visit is used as a pick-up or a delivery,
- which request is being served,
- whether one request or two requests are processed,
- and how the request-level service choice updates the onboard state.

Therefore, compressing pick-up / delivery to physical locations would make the state transition rule much more complicated and much less local.

So the current modeling choice is:

- compress emptying, because its feasible transitions are few and can be enumerated locally from the current state;
- do not compress pick-up / delivery, because their request-level service decisions do not aggregate naturally into a simple physical-location transition.
