The phase-1 formulation is

$$

subject to

\sum_{r \in R^p} a_r^i x_r + z_i = 2
\qquad \forall i \in V \setminus \{0, 2n+1\}
$$

$$

$$

$$

$$
\sum_{r \in R^p} b_e^r x_r = \theta_e
\qquad \forall e \in E
$$

$$
x_r \ge 0
\qquad \forall r \in R^p
$$

$$
1 \ge \theta_e \ge 0
\qquad \forall e \in E

### heuristic-cg

#### Start/end columns

To let the master start and terminate a route, include depot seed steps of the form

$$
\text{start} \to C_i^p,
\qquad
C_i^d \to \text{end}.
$$

Then these boundary columns have the following states:

- $\text{start} \to C_i^p$: the state after servicing $C_i^p$ is $\{N,F(h_i,l_i)\}$.
- $C_i^d \to \text{end}$: the state after servicing $C_i^d$ is $\{N,N\}$.

These are heuristic phase-1 seed columns. They are shorter than the standard nondepot $p$-steps, but they provide the boundary pieces needed to connect the below 3-step windows into complete routes.

####  $C^p-C^p-C^d-C^d$ columns for fixed return

For the flexible-return SPDP with $p=3$, it seems reasonable to use a structured heuristic-CG pattern instead of solving the exact phase-1 pricing problem from the beginning. The main intuition is that, in SPDP, a route tends to be more efficient when two full containers are handled together and the number of landfill visits is reduced. In that sense, a pattern of the form

$$
C^p - C^p - l - C^d - C^d
$$

looks like a logical way to process two containers within one local segment.

Assume that, for any two requests, the minimum-time pattern that serves them in the order

$$
C^p - C^p - l_1 - l_2 - C^d - C^d
$$

is shorter than the route time limit $T$. (그냥 이 가정 없이, heuristic-cg 이후 exact-cg 한 번 돌려서 보장하면 됨) This is a practically reasonable assumption for constructing phase-1 seeds, and it implies that every contiguous 3-step subpath extracted from such a feasible local pattern is also time-feasible.

Let $\{i,j\}$ be request indices. Then the natural candidate patterns are

- $C^p_i \to C^p_j \to C^d_i \to C^d_j$,
- $C^p_i \to C^p_j \to C^d_j \to C^d_i$,
- $C^p_j \to C^p_i \to C^d_i \to C^d_j$,
- $C^p_j \to C^p_i \to C^d_j \to C^d_i$.

For each such pattern:

- only the transition from a pick-up node to a delivery node may contain a
  nonempty $\pi$;
- if the two loaded full containers require the same landfill, then
  $\pi=(l)$ is used;
- if they require different landfills, then $\pi=(l_1,l_2)$ or
  $\pi=(l_2,l_1)$ is used;
- on that nonempty-$\pi$ transition, the landfill service applies the state transition

$$
\{F(h_a,l_a),F(h_b,l_b)\}\to\{E(h_a),E(h_b)\},
$$

  where $a,b$ denote the two onboard full containers on that transition. Because the vehicle state is an unordered two-slot state, $\{a,b\}=\{b,a\}$.
- all other transitions use $\pi=\varnothing$.

However, in the p-step master, adjacent p-steps can be linked only when the last node of the front p-step is equal to the first node of the back p-step. Therefore, for each fixed-return pattern, generate the following five 3-step windows:

If the pattern is $C^p_i \to C^p_j \to C^d_i \to C^d_j$,
1. $C_i^p \to C_j^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d$,

   with node-state sequence. (The state written immediately after a customer node denotes the state **after servicing** that node.)

$$
\{N,F(h_i,l_i)\}
\to
\{F(h_i,l_i),F(h_j,l_j)\}
\to
\{N,E(h_j)\}
\to
\{N,N\}.
$$


2. $C_j^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d \to C_k^p$   with $k\notin\{i,j\}$,

   with node-state sequence

$$
\{F(h_i,l_i),F(h_j,l_j)\}
\to
\{N,E(h_j)\}
\to
\{N,N\}
\to
\{N,F(h_k,l_k)\}.
$$

3. $C_i^d \to C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p$  with $\{k,m\}\cap\{i,j\}=\varnothing$,

   with node-state sequence

$$
\{N,E(h_j)\}
\to
\{N,N\}
\to
\{N,F(h_k,l_k)\}
\to
\{F(h_k,l_k),F(h_m,l_m)\}.
$$

4. $C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p \to C_k ^d$  with $\{k,m\}\cap\{i,j\}=\varnothing$,

   with node-state sequence

$$
\{N,N\}
\to
\{N,F(h_k,l_k)\}
\to
\{F(h_k,l_k),F(h_m,l_m)\}
\to
\{N,E(h_m)\}.
$$

5. $C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p \to C_m^d$  with $\{k,m\}\cap\{i,j\}=\varnothing$,

   with node-state sequence

$$
\{N,N\}
\to
\{N,F(h_k,l_k)\}
\to
\{F(h_k,l_k),F(h_m,l_m)\}
\to
\{E(h_k),N\}.
$$

These five 3-step windows are intended to be used together as heuristic phase-1 p-steps because they allow the p-step master to reconstruct a long route that repeatedly follows the efficient two-container pattern $C^p-C^p-C^d-C^d$ through linkings of the form $1 \to (4 \text{ or } 5) \to 3 \to 2 \to 1$, where consecutive p-steps are connected through a common boundary node.

#### Flexible-return columns

Because the current model is the flexible-return version, a route does not need to return the same request identity after a pick-up. Once two full containers have been collected and emptied, the vehicle may hold two empty containers that can be delivered to other customers of the same corresponding types. In particular, after serving a delivery node $C_k^d$, it can be efficient to immediately serve the paired pick-up node $C_k^p$, because these two actions occur at the same customer location. This creates a local switch-out step

$$
C_k^d \to C_k^p,
$$

whose travel distance is zero.

This suggests that, in addition to the matched-pair $C^p-C^p-C^d-C^d$ family, phase 1 should also include a flexible-return column family based on alternating chains of the form

$$
C^p-C^p-l-C^d-C^p-C^d-C^p-l-C^d-C^p-C^d-C^p\cdots-C^d-C^p-C^d-C^d.
$$

Unlike the fixed-return case, this is no longer a repetition of a single $C^p-C^p-C^d-C^d$ block. Instead, the route evolves through repeated switch-out steps in which a delivery is immediately followed by a pick-up.

For the flexible-return state descriptions below, if a delivery node consumes the empty container originating from one of the two previously picked-up requests, then the remaining onboard empty is the empty container of the other request. When a window begins with a delivery node and one previously loaded full container is already carried into the window, that carried-over full container is denoted by $F(h_a,l_a)$, and the corresponding emptied token after landfill service is denoted by $E(h_a)$.

##### $C^p-C^p-C^d$ entry columns

The natural entry into such a flexible-return chain is a column of the form

$$
C_i^p \to C_j^p \to C_k^d.

with $k \notin \{i,j\}$.

Its node-state sequence is

$$
\{N,F(h_i,l_i)\}
\to
\{F(h_i,l_i),F(h_j,l_j)\}
\to
\{N,E(h_j)\}

$$

\to
\{F(h_i,l_i),F(h_j,l_j)\}
\to
\{E(h_i),N\}

### heuristic-cg implementation algorithm

The initial implementation can be organized as follows.

1. Independently generate depot seed columns of the form

$$
\text{start} \to C_i^p,
\qquad
C_i^d \to \text{end}
$$

for all requests $i$.

2. For every unordered request pair $\{i,j\}$, generate the four basic fixed-return patterns

$$
C_i^p \to C_j^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d,
\quad
C_i^p \to C_j^p \to C_j^d \to C_i^d,
\quad
C_j^p \to C_i^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d,
\quad
C_j^p \to C_i^p \to C_j^d \to C_i^d.
$$

3. For each candidate fixed-return pattern, generate the five 3-step windows

$$
C_i^p \to C_j^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d,
\qquad
C_j^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d \to C_k^p,
$$

$$
C_i^d \to C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p,
\qquad
C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p \to C_k^d,
$$

$$
C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p \to C_m^d,
$$

over all admissible index combinations satisfying

$$
k \notin \{i,j\}, \qquad \{k,m\}\cap\{i,j\}=\varnothing,
$$

and keep only feasible realizations whose node-state sequences match the corresponding fixed-return templates above.

4. Generate flexible-return entry columns of type

$$
C_i^p \to C_j^p \to C_k^d
$$

with $k \notin \{i,j\}$, over all admissible triples $(i,j,k)$.

Keep only realizations whose node-state sequences match the corresponding flexible-return entry templates above.

5. Generate the four flexible-return middle windows

$$
C_i^d \to C_i^p \to C_j^d \to C_j^p,
\qquad
C_i^p \to C_j^d \to C_j^p \to C_k^d,
$$

$$
C_j^d \to C_j^p \to C_k^d \to C_k^p,
\qquad
C_j^p \to C_k^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^d,
$$

over all admissible index combinations satisfying

$$
i \ne j, \qquad |\{i,j,k\}|=3, \qquad j \ne k, \qquad |\{j,k,m\}|=3,
$$

assign a nonempty $\pi$ only on the designated pick-up to delivery transition for each window, and keep only realizations whose node-state sequences match the corresponding flexible-return state templates above.

6. Generate flexible-return exit columns of type

$$
C_k^p \to C_u^d \to C_v^d
$$

with $k \notin \{u,v\}$ and $u \ne v$, over all admissible triples $(k,u,v)$.

Keep only realizations whose node-state sequences match the corresponding flexible-return exit templates above.

7. Store generated columns by a canonical p-step key so that identical p-steps are inserted only once.

8. Add the resulting heuristic columns to the phase-1 restricted master problem and solve phase 1.

- **If the number of generated heuristic columns is too large in practice, introduce a score for each candidate family and retain only the top-ranked candidates before generating the corresponding windows or boundary columns.**

### Why short boundary columns are used

In the heuristic-cg construction, some columns connected to the front or back of a route are intentionally kept shorter than the nominal 3-step length. This choice is used for the depot boundary columns

$$
\text{start}\to C_i^p,
\qquad
C_i^d\to \text{end},
$$

and also for the flexible-return entry and exit columns

$$
C_i^p\to C_j^p\to C_k^d,
\qquad
C_k^p\to C_u^d\to C_v^d.
$$

The reason is structural. The local route patterns that the heuristic-cg procedure is designed to promote are, for fixed return,

$$
\text{start}/(C^p-C^p-C^d-C^d)/\text{end},
$$

and, for flexible return, alternating chains entered through a short $C^p-C^p-C^d$ piece and terminated through a short $C^p-C^d-C^d$ piece. In both cases, the essential combinatorial structure lies in the internal repeated part of the route, not in the front or back boundary pieces. Hence, forcing the front and back parts to be represented by full 3-step columns would mainly introduce many additional columns without creating new route structures that are needed for the intended heuristic patterns.

Short boundary columns are therefore used as minimal connection pieces. They are sufficient for the master problem to concatenate the internal 3-step windows into complete routes, while avoiding the unnecessary proliferation of start- and end-related columns. In that sense, the shortening of the boundary columns is a deliberate reduction of the heuristic column pool that preserves the ability of the master to reconstruct the route families that motivated the heuristic-cg design.

#### Exactness consideration

If this point is discussed in the paper, the relevant exactness claim should not be stated at the level of the pure 3-step formulation alone, but at the level of the master problem that is actually solved in the branch-and-price algorithm. In particular, the algorithm may be viewed as using two families of columns:

1. the exact 3-step columns that are generated dynamically by pricing, and
2. a finite set of heuristic boundary columns that are inserted explicitly at the beginning.

Under this interpretation, the resulting branch-and-price algorithm remains exact as long as the following two properties hold:

1. every heuristic boundary column corresponds to a valid feasible subpath of a feasible SPDP route, and
2. every feasible SPDP route can still be represented in the master by concatenating valid columns from the available families.

The heuristic boundary columns then do not introduce artificial route structures; they only provide alternative decompositions of routes that are already feasible in the original problem. Their role is to improve the initial restricted master problem by supplying useful boundary pieces, while the exact pricing step still guarantees that all required 3-step columns with negative reduced cost can be generated when needed. Consequently, the exactness issue is not whether the heuristic columns belong to the smallest possible compact 3-step set, but whether their inclusion preserves the set of feasible complete route decompositions represented by the master problem.

## Heuristic pricing $\to$ exact pricing

The role of heuristic pricing is **not** to replace the exact pricing problem in
the branch-and-price algorithm. Its role is only to reduce the wall-clock time
spent in a pricing round **by finding a small number of improving columns quickly,**
**before resorting to a full exact pricing pass.**

This distinction is essential. In the current project, exactness of the root-node
LP relaxation must still be certified by the exact pricing problem. Therefore,
the heuristic layer should modify only:

- the **order** in which start-fixed pricing subproblems are explored,
- the **amount** of search performed before reoptimizing the restricted master,
- and the **number** of negative reduced-cost columns extracted in one round.

### Phase-dependent reduced cost

Let

$$
\varphi \in \{\mathrm{I},\mathrm{II}\}
$$

denote the current node-CG phase.

For a pricing edge $e=(i,j)\in E$, define the phase-dependent edge objective as

$$
\tilde c_e^{\varphi}
=
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } \varphi=\mathrm{I},\\
c_e & \text{if } \varphi=\mathrm{II}.
\end{cases}
$$

This matches the current implementation:

- in phase I, pricing is used only to remove the artificial variables, so the
  original travel cost is not charged in the pricing objective;
- in phase II, pricing uses the original compact $p$-step objective.

For a feasible $p$-step $r$ starting from

$$
u=(s,\sigma_s)
$$

and ending at

$$
v=(f,\sigma_f),
$$

its phase-dependent reduced cost is

$$
\bar c_r^{\varphi}
=
\sum_{e\in P_r}\big(\tilde c_e^{\varphi}-\delta_e-2\alpha_{v_e}\big)
+
t(r)\gamma_v
+
(\gamma_v-\gamma_u)\tau_r
- \alpha_s + \alpha_f - \beta_u + \beta_v,
$$

where, as before,

- $\alpha$ is the visit-row dual,
- $\beta$ is the state-balance-row dual,
- $\gamma$ is the time-row dual,
- $\delta$ is the edge-linking-row dual.

Equivalently,

$$
\bar c_r^{\varphi}
=
C^{\varphi}(P_r)+\Phi(u,v,t(r)),
$$

with

$$
C^{\varphi}(P_r)
=
\sum_{e\in P_r}\big(\tilde c_e^{\varphi}-\delta_e-2\alpha_{v_e}\big).
$$

The heuristic policy proposed below never changes this formula. Every completed
label that is accepted as a column is evaluated by the same exact reduced-cost
formula as in exact pricing.

### Start-fixed pricing decomposition

Let

$$
U = \{u=(s,\sigma_s)\}
$$

be the set of all feasible start node-state pairs in the current pricing
context.

For each

$$
u \in U,
$$

define the start-fixed pricing subproblem

$$
\mathrm{PP}^{\varphi}(u)
:
\qquad
\zeta^{\varphi}(u)
=
\min_{r\in R_u^p} \bar c_r^{\varphi},
$$

where

$$
R_u^p
=
\{r\in R^p : v_0(r)=s,\ \sigma(v_0(r))=\sigma_s\}.
$$

Then the full pricing problem solved in one CG round is

$$
\min_{u\in U}\zeta^{\varphi}(u).
$$

In the current forward-labeling implementation, this corresponds exactly to
looping over all feasible start classes $u$ and solving the labeling problem for
each of them.

For $p=3$, the main practical burden is not a long-horizon route completion but
the fact that a pricing round may examine **many** start classes. Therefore, **the**
**most natural heuristic layer is to reduce the amount of work spent across start**
**classes, rather than to change the exact within-label reduced-cost evaluation.**

### Heuristic pricing pass

#### Main principle

The heuristic pass solves only a **restricted** part of the full pricing round.
It is allowed to miss negative reduced-cost columns. Its only purpose is to
return some negative reduced-cost columns quickly.

Formally, let

$$
U^{H,\varphi}\subseteq U
$$

be the subset of start classes explored in the heuristic pass at phase
$\varphi$.

For each

$$
u\in U^{H,\varphi},
$$

let

$$
\widehat R_u^{p,\varphi}\subseteq R_u^p
$$

denote the subset of complete $p$-steps that are actually searched before the
heuristic pass stops. Then the heuristic pass returns columns only from

$$
\widehat R^{p,\varphi}
=
\bigcup_{u\in U^{H,\varphi}} \widehat R_u^{p,\varphi}.
$$

Because, in general,

$$
\widehat R^{p,\varphi}\subsetneq R^p,
$$

the heuristic pass does **not** certify the absence of negative reduced-cost
columns.

#### Start priority score

To determine which start classes should be explored first, define a cheap
phase-dependent start score.

For a start class

$$
u=(s,\sigma_s),
$$

let

$$
\delta^+(u)
=
\{e\in E : \text{$e$ is a feasible pricing edge outgoing from $u$}\}.
$$

Define the one-step optimistic score

$$
\eta^{\varphi}(u)
=
\min_{e\in \delta^+(u)}
\big(\tilde c_e^{\varphi}-\delta_e-2\alpha_{v_e}\big).
$$

If $\delta^+(u)=\varnothing$, set

$$
\eta^{\varphi}(u)=+\infty.
$$

The heuristic start order is obtained by sorting start classes in
nondecreasing order of $\eta^{\varphi}(u)$.

This score is deliberately simple:

- it is phase-consistent because it uses $\tilde c_e^{\varphi}$;
- it incorporates the current dual information through $\delta_e$ and
  $\alpha_{v_e}$;
- and it is cheap enough to compute at every pricing round.

The score is **not** required to be a valid lower bound on
$\zeta^{\varphi}(u)$. It is used only as a search-order heuristic.

#### Restricted search policy

Let

$$
K_{\mathrm{start}}^{\varphi}\in \mathbb Z_+,
\qquad
K_{\mathrm{col}}^{\varphi}\in \mathbb Z_+,
\qquad
K_{\mathrm{col,start}}^{\varphi}\in \mathbb Z_+,
$$

be heuristic control parameters.

The heuristic pass is defined as follows.

1. Compute $\eta^{\varphi}(u)$ for every start class $u\in U$.
2. Sort all start classes in nondecreasing order of $\eta^{\varphi}(u)$.
3. Explore only the first

$$
K_{\mathrm{start}}^{\varphi}
$$

   start classes in this order.
4. For each explored start class $u$, run the same forward labeling algorithm as
   in exact pricing, with the same:
   - feasibility rules,
   - elementarity restriction,
   - pick-up symmetry-breaking rule,
   - delivery symmetry-breaking rule,
   - and exact dominance rule.
5. As soon as

$$
K_{\mathrm{col,start}}^{\varphi}
$$

   negative reduced-cost columns have been found for the current start class,
   stop exploring that start class and move to the next one.
6. As soon as the heuristic pass has collected

$$
K_{\mathrm{col}}^{\varphi}
$$

   negative reduced-cost columns in total, terminate the heuristic pass and
   return them to the master.

For the present project with $p=3$, the most natural initial choice is

$$
K_{\mathrm{col,start}}^{\varphi}=1,
$$

that is, once a start class yields one negative reduced-cost column, the
heuristic pass immediately moves on. This is consistent with the intended role
of the heuristic layer: it is better to obtain a few improving columns from many
different promising start classes than to exhaustively enumerate many negative
columns from one start class before reoptimizing the restricted master.

#### Important exactness restriction

Within the heuristic pass, the following operations remain exact:

- every generated complete label is converted to a column using the same
  $\tau$-selection rule as exact pricing;
- every accepted column is evaluated with the same exact reduced-cost formula
  $\bar c_r^{\varphi}$;
- no relaxation of elementarity, state continuity, or symmetry validity is
  introduced.

Therefore, every column returned by the heuristic pass is a genuinely feasible
master column with its correct reduced cost. The heuristicity enters **only**
through incomplete search.

### Exact fallback pricing pass

If the heuristic pass returns at least one negative reduced-cost column, the
algorithm inserts those columns into the restricted master, reoptimizes the
master LP, and begins the next CG iteration without calling exact pricing in
that iteration.

If the heuristic pass returns no column, then the algorithm must solve the full
exact pricing problem:

$$
\min_{u\in U}\ \min_{r\in R_u^p}\ \bar c_r^{\varphi}.
$$

This exact pass uses:

- all start classes $u\in U$,
- no heuristic truncation on the number of explored starts,
- no early stopping after the first negative column of a start class,
- and the same exact labeling and reduced-cost evaluation as the current
  pricing algorithm.

Only this exact pass is allowed to certify that the current dual solution admits
no negative reduced-cost column.

### Hybrid pricing algorithm in one CG iteration

For a fixed phase $\varphi$, one pricing round of the hybrid procedure is:

1. Solve the current restricted master LP and extract its dual solution.
2. Run the heuristic pricing pass and obtain a column set

$$
\mathcal C_H^{\varphi}\subseteq R^p.
$$

3. If

$$
\mathcal C_H^{\varphi}\neq\varnothing,
$$

   add $\mathcal C_H^{\varphi}$ to the restricted master and terminate the
   pricing round.
4. Otherwise, run the exact pricing pass and obtain

$$
\mathcal C_E^{\varphi}\subseteq R^p.
$$

5. If

$$
\mathcal C_E^{\varphi}\neq\varnothing,
$$

   add $\mathcal C_E^{\varphi}$ to the restricted master and terminate the
   pricing round.
6. If

$$
\mathcal C_E^{\varphi}=\varnothing,
$$

   stop column generation for the current phase.

This procedure applies equally to:

- phase I exact pricing rounds, where the objective is to eliminate the
  artificial variables,
- and phase II pricing rounds, where the objective is to optimize the original
  compact $p$-step LP relaxation.

### Exactness proposition 굳이?

#### Proposition

Consider the node-CG algorithm obtained by replacing each exact pricing round by
the hybrid procedure above. Suppose that:

1. every column returned by the heuristic pass belongs to the true feasible
   column set $R^p$ and is evaluated with its exact reduced cost;
2. whenever the heuristic pass returns no column, the exact pricing pass is
   executed;
3. the column generation algorithm terminates a phase only when the exact
   pricing pass proves that no negative reduced-cost column exists.

Then the resulting hybrid node-CG algorithm is exact for the LP solved in the
corresponding phase.

#### Proof

Fix a phase $\varphi$ and consider any CG iteration with dual solution
$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)$ extracted from the current restricted master.

If the heuristic pass returns a nonempty set of columns, then the algorithm
simply adds feasible improving columns to the restricted master and continues.
This cannot invalidate correctness, because adding feasible columns only enlarges
the restricted master and does not alter the full master problem.

Now consider an iteration in which the algorithm terminates the phase. By
assumption 3, phase termination can occur only after the exact pricing pass has
been executed and has found no negative reduced-cost column. Therefore,

$$
\bar c_r^{\varphi}\ge 0
\qquad \forall r\in R^p.
$$

But this is exactly the standard optimality condition for column generation:
the dual solution of the current restricted master is dual-feasible for the full
master, and no omitted column has negative reduced cost. Hence the current
restricted master solution is optimal for the full master LP of phase
$\varphi$.

Therefore the hybrid pricing policy changes only the sequence of restricted
masters visited during column generation, but not the final optimal LP value of
the phase. This proves the proposition. $\square$

### Why this is well matched to the current project

The present pricing problem has the following practical features:

1. $p=3$, so each individual start-fixed pricing problem is short;
2. the reduced-cost evaluation of a complete label is already exact and cheap;
3. the main practical overhead comes from solving many start-fixed pricing
   problems in every round.

For this reason, heuristic pricing should focus on **inter-start selection**
rather than on approximate within-path reduced-cost computations.

In particular, the present project should **not** begin by:

- relaxing the reduced-cost formula,
- changing the dominance rule,
- or using approximate feasibility checks.

Those modifications would complicate correctness and are unnecessary for the
first acceleration layer. By contrast, the hybrid heuristic-to-exact policy
above leaves the mathematical pricing problem unchanged and only reduces the
number of exact start-fixed searches performed in the typical pricing round.

### Recommended initial setting for the current implementation

For the first implementation, the following conservative policy is appropriate.

1. Use the current exact forward labeling code without changing:
   - the state representation,
   - the complete-label test,
   - the $\tau$ optimization,
   - the exact reduced-cost computation,
   - or the dominance rule.
2. Before the pricing loop starts, compute $\eta^{\varphi}(u)$ for every start
   class.
3. Sort all start classes by $\eta^{\varphi}(u)$.
4. In the heuristic pass, explore only the first

$$
K_{\mathrm{start}}^{\varphi}
$$

   starts.
5. For each explored start, stop after the first negative reduced-cost column:

$$
K_{\mathrm{col,start}}^{\varphi}=1.
$$

6. Stop the heuristic pass after collecting a small total number of columns,
   for example

$$
K_{\mathrm{col}}^{\varphi}\in\{8,16\}.
$$

7. If the heuristic pass returns no column, immediately call the current exact
   pricing routine on the full start set $U$.

This initial design is intentionally conservative. It is expected to preserve
the final root-node LP bound while substantially reducing the number of
iterations in which the expensive full exact pricing pass must be invoked.

### Expected effect on root-node column generation

The heuristic-to-exact policy is expected to improve root-node performance in
the following sense.

- Early in phase II, many negative reduced-cost columns typically exist. In that
  regime, the heuristic pass should often find improving columns quickly, so the
  exact pricing pass is skipped in many rounds.
- Later in phase II, when negative reduced-cost columns become scarce, the
  heuristic pass will more often return no column, and the exact pass will be
  invoked to certify optimality.

Hence the hybrid strategy is designed so that:

- the expensive exact certification is paid only near the end of a pricing
  phase,
- while the earlier rounds use cheap heuristic discovery to move the dual
  solution quickly.

This is precisely the setting in which heuristic pricing is most useful for the
current project: we do not want to change the exact master-pricing framework,
but we do want to avoid paying the full exact pricing cost in every single
column-generation iteration.
