### heuristic-cg

Key idea: Since the vehicle capacity = 2, it is efficient if two customer are covered with one landfill. Therefore, making p-step covering two customer, i.e. two $C_d$ and two $C_p$ is expected to generate good solution.

#### Start/end columns

To let the master start and terminate a route, include depot seed steps of the form

$$
\text{start} \to C_i^p,
\qquad
C_i^d \to \text{end}.
$$

Then these boundary columns have the following states:

- $\text{start} \to C_i^p$: the state after servicing $C_i^p$ is $\{N,F(h_i,l_i)\}$.
- $C_i^d \to \text{end}$: the state after servicing $C_i^d$ is $\{N,N\}$.

These are heuristic phase-1 seed columns. They are shorter than the standard nondepot $p$-steps, but they provide the boundary pieces needed to connect the below 3-step windows into complete routes.

####  $C^p-C^p-C^d-C^d$ columns for fixed return

For the flexible-return SPDP with $p=3$, it seems reasonable to use a structured heuristic-CG pattern instead of solving the exact phase-1 pricing problem from the beginning. The main intuition is that, in SPDP, a route tends to be more efficient when two full containers are handled together and the number of landfill visits is reduced. In that sense, a pattern of the form

$$
C^p - C^p - l - C^d - C^d
$$

looks like a logical way to process two containers within one local segment.

Assume that, for any two requests, the minimum-time pattern that serves them in the order

$$
C^p - C^p - l_1 - l_2 - C^d - C^d
$$

is shorter than the route time limit $T$. (그냥 이 가정 없이, heuristic-cg 이후 exact-cg 한 번 돌려서 보장하면 됨) This is a practically reasonable assumption for constructing phase-1 seeds, and it implies that every contiguous 3-step subpath extracted from such a feasible local pattern is also time-feasible.

Let $\{i,j\}$ be request indices. Then the natural candidate patterns are

- $C^p_i \to C^p_j \to C^d_i \to C^d_j$,
- $C^p_i \to C^p_j \to C^d_j \to C^d_i$,
- $C^p_j \to C^p_i \to C^d_i \to C^d_j$,
- $C^p_j \to C^p_i \to C^d_j \to C^d_i$.

For each such pattern:

- only the transition from a pick-up node to a delivery node may contain a
  nonempty $\pi$;
- if the two loaded full containers require the same landfill, then
  $\pi=(l)$ is used;
- if they require different landfills, then $\pi=(l_1,l_2)$ or
  $\pi=(l_2,l_1)$ is used;
- on that nonempty-$\pi$ transition, the landfill service applies the state transition

$$
\{F(h_a,l_a),F(h_b,l_b)\}\to\{E(h_a),E(h_b)\},
$$

  where $a,b$ denote the two onboard full containers on that transition. Because the vehicle state is an unordered two-slot state, $\{a,b\}=\{b,a\}$.
- all other transitions use $\pi=\varnothing$.

However, in the p-step master, adjacent p-steps can be linked only when the last node of the front p-step is equal to the first node of the back p-step. Therefore, for each fixed-return pattern, generate the following five 3-step windows:

If the pattern is $C^p_i \to C^p_j \to C^d_i \to C^d_j$,
1. $C_i^p \to C_j^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d$,

   with node-state sequence. (The state written immediately after a customer node denotes the state **after servicing** that node.)

$$
\{N,F(h_i,l_i)\}
\to
\{F(h_i,l_i),F(h_j,l_j)\}
\to
\{N,E(h_j)\}
\to
\{N,N\}.
$$


2. $C_j^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d \to C_k^p$   with $k\notin\{i,j\}$,

   with node-state sequence

$$
\{F(h_i,l_i),F(h_j,l_j)\}
\to
\{N,E(h_j)\}
\to
\{N,N\}
\to
\{N,F(h_k,l_k)\}.
$$

3. $C_i^d \to C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p$  with $\{k,m\}\cap\{i,j\}=\varnothing$,

   with node-state sequence

$$
\{N,E(h_j)\}
\to
\{N,N\}
\to
\{N,F(h_k,l_k)\}
\to
\{F(h_k,l_k),F(h_m,l_m)\}.
$$

4. $C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p \to C_k ^d$  with $\{k,m\}\cap\{i,j\}=\varnothing$,

   with node-state sequence

$$
\{N,N\}
\to
\{N,F(h_k,l_k)\}
\to
\{F(h_k,l_k),F(h_m,l_m)\}
\to
\{N,E(h_m)\}.
$$

5. $C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p \to C_m^d$  with $\{k,m\}\cap\{i,j\}=\varnothing$,

   with node-state sequence

$$
\{N,N\}
\to
\{N,F(h_k,l_k)\}
\to
\{F(h_k,l_k),F(h_m,l_m)\}
\to
\{E(h_k),N\}.
$$

These five 3-step windows are intended to be used together as heuristic phase-1 p-steps because they allow the p-step master to reconstruct a long route that repeatedly follows the efficient two-container pattern $C^p-C^p-C^d-C^d$ through linkings of the form $1 \to (4 \text{ or } 5) \to 3 \to 2 \to 1$, where consecutive p-steps are connected through a common boundary node.

#### Flexible-return columns

Because the current model is the flexible-return version, a route does not need to return the same request identity after a pick-up. Once two full containers have been collected and emptied, the vehicle may hold two empty containers that can be delivered to other customers of the same corresponding types. In particular, after serving a delivery node $C_k^d$, it can be efficient to immediately serve the paired pick-up node $C_k^p$, because these two actions occur at the same customer location. This creates a local switch-out step

$$
C_k^d \to C_k^p,
$$

whose travel distance is zero.

This suggests that, in addition to the matched-pair $C^p-C^p-C^d-C^d$ family, phase 1 should also include a flexible-return column family based on alternating chains of the form

$$
C^p-C^p-l-C^d-C^p-C^d-C^p-l-C^d-C^p-C^d-C^p\cdots-C^d-C^p-C^d-C^d.
$$

Unlike the fixed-return case, this is no longer a repetition of a single $C^p-C^p-C^d-C^d$ block. Instead, the route evolves through repeated switch-out steps in which a delivery is immediately followed by a pick-up.

For the flexible-return state descriptions below, if a delivery node consumes the empty container originating from one of the two previously picked-up requests, then the remaining onboard empty is the empty container of the other request. When a window begins with a delivery node and one previously loaded full container is already carried into the window, that carried-over full container is denoted by $F(h_a,l_a)$, and the corresponding emptied token after landfill service is denoted by $E(h_a)$.

### heuristic-cg implementation algorithm

The initial implementation can be organized as follows.

1. Independently generate depot seed columns of the form

$$
\text{start} \to C_i^p,
\qquad
C_i^d \to \text{end}
$$

for all requests $i$.

2. For every unordered request pair $\{i,j\}$, generate the four basic fixed-return patterns

$$
C_i^p \to C_j^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d,
\quad
C_i^p \to C_j^p \to C_j^d \to C_i^d,
\quad
C_j^p \to C_i^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d,
\quad
C_j^p \to C_i^p \to C_j^d \to C_i^d.
$$

3. For each candidate fixed-return pattern, generate the five 3-step windows

$$
C_i^p \to C_j^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d,
\qquad
C_j^p \to C_i^d \to C_j^d \to C_k^p,
$$

$$
C_i^d \to C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p,
\qquad
C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p \to C_k^d,
$$

$$
C_j^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^p \to C_m^d,
$$

over all admissible index combinations satisfying

$$
k \notin \{i,j\}, \qquad \{k,m\}\cap\{i,j\}=\varnothing,
$$

and keep only feasible realizations whose node-state sequences match the corresponding fixed-return templates above.

4. Generate flexible-return entry columns of type

$$
C_i^p \to C_j^p \to C_k^d
$$

with $k \notin \{i,j\}$, over all admissible triples $(i,j,k)$.

Keep only realizations whose node-state sequences match the corresponding flexible-return entry templates above.

5. Generate the four flexible-return middle windows

$$
C_i^d \to C_i^p \to C_j^d \to C_j^p,
\qquad
C_i^p \to C_j^d \to C_j^p \to C_k^d,
$$

$$
C_j^d \to C_j^p \to C_k^d \to C_k^p,
\qquad
C_j^p \to C_k^d \to C_k^p \to C_m^d,
$$

over all admissible index combinations satisfying

$$
i \ne j, \qquad |\{i,j,k\}|=3, \qquad j \ne k, \qquad |\{j,k,m\}|=3,
$$

assign a nonempty $\pi$ only on the designated pick-up to delivery transition for each window, and keep only realizations whose node-state sequences match the corresponding flexible-return state templates above.

6. Generate flexible-return exit columns of type

$$
C_k^p \to C_u^d \to C_v^d
$$

with $k \notin \{u,v\}$ and $u \ne v$, over all admissible triples $(k,u,v)$.

Keep only realizations whose node-state sequences match the corresponding flexible-return exit templates above.

7. Store generated columns by a canonical p-step key so that identical p-steps are inserted only once.

8. Add the resulting heuristic columns to the phase-1 restricted master problem and solve phase 1.

- **If the number of generated heuristic columns is too large in practice, introduce a score for each candidate family and retain only the top-ranked candidates before generating the corresponding windows or boundary columns.**

### Why short boundary columns are used

In the heuristic-cg construction, some columns connected to the front or back of a route are intentionally kept shorter than the nominal 3-step length. This choice is used for the depot boundary columns

$$
\text{start}\to C_i^p,
\qquad
C_i^d\to \text{end},
$$

and also for the flexible-return entry and exit columns

$$
C_i^p\to C_j^p\to C_k^d,
\qquad
C_k^p\to C_u^d\to C_v^d.
$$

The reason is structural. The local route patterns that the heuristic-cg procedure is designed to promote are, for fixed return,

$$
\text{start}/(C^p-C^p-C^d-C^d)/\text{end},
$$

and, for flexible return, alternating chains entered through a short $C^p-C^p-C^d$ piece and terminated through a short $C^p-C^d-C^d$ piece. In both cases, the essential combinatorial structure lies in the internal repeated part of the route, not in the front or back boundary pieces. Hence, forcing the front and back parts to be represented by full 3-step columns would mainly introduce many additional columns without creating new route structures that are needed for the intended heuristic patterns.

Short boundary columns are therefore used as minimal connection pieces. They are sufficient for the master problem to concatenate the internal 3-step windows into complete routes, while avoiding the unnecessary proliferation of start- and end-related columns. In that sense, the shortening of the boundary columns is a deliberate reduction of the heuristic column pool that preserves the ability of the master to reconstruct the route families that motivated the heuristic-cg design.

#### Exactness consideration

If this point is discussed in the paper, the relevant exactness claim should not be stated at the level of the pure 3-step formulation alone, but at the level of the master problem that is actually solved in the branch-and-price algorithm. In particular, the algorithm may be viewed as using two families of columns:

1. the exact 3-step columns that are generated dynamically by pricing, and
2. a finite set of heuristic boundary columns that are inserted explicitly at the beginning.

Under this interpretation, the resulting branch-and-price algorithm remains exact as long as the following two properties hold:

1. every heuristic boundary column corresponds to a valid feasible subpath of a feasible SPDP route, and
2. every feasible SPDP route can still be represented in the master by concatenating valid columns from the available families.

The heuristic boundary columns then do not introduce artificial route structures; they only provide alternative decompositions of routes that are already feasible in the original problem. Their role is to improve the initial restricted master problem by supplying useful boundary pieces, while the exact pricing step still guarantees that all required 3-step columns with negative reduced cost can be generated when needed. Consequently, the exactness issue is not whether the heuristic columns belong to the smallest possible compact 3-step set, but whether their inclusion preserves the set of feasible complete route decompositions represented by the master problem.

## Heuristic pricing $\to$ exact pricing

The present idea is meant
for **phase 2** only. The purpose is to avoid solving the full exact pricing
problem at every root-node LP iteration when many negative reduced-cost columns
still exist.

The heuristic layer changes only the **search policy**, not the pricing model
itself.

### Start-fixed phase-2 pricing problem

Let

$$
u=(s,\sigma_s)
$$

be a feasible start node-state pair, and let

$$
R_u^p
=
\{r\in R^p : v_0(r)=s,\ \sigma(v_0(r))=\sigma_s\}.
$$

For phase 2, the reduced cost of a feasible $p$-step $r\in R_u^p$ ending at

$$
v=(f,\sigma_f)
$$

is

$$
\bar c_r
=
\sum_{e\in P_r}(c_e-\delta_e-2\alpha_{v_e})
+
t(r)\gamma_v
+
(\gamma_v-\gamma_u)\tau_r
- \alpha_s + \alpha_f - \beta_u + \beta_v.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\bar c_r = C(P_r)+\Phi(u,v,t(r)),
$$

with

$$
C(P_r)=\sum_{e\in P_r}(c_e-\delta_e-2\alpha_{v_e}).
$$

For each start class $u$, define

$$
\zeta(u)=\min_{r\in R_u^p}\bar c_r.
$$

Then one exact pricing round solves

$$
\min_{u\in U}\zeta(u),
$$

where $U$ is the set of all feasible start node-state pairs. In the present
$p=3$ setting, the main practical burden is that a pricing round may still have
to inspect many such start classes.

### Heuristic pass

The heuristic pass explores only a restricted part of the full phase-2 pricing
round. It may miss negative reduced-cost columns, so it is not an optimality
certificate; its only purpose is to return some negative columns quickly.

Let

$$
U^H \subseteq U
$$

be the subset of start classes explored in the heuristic pass, and let

$$
\widehat R_u^p \subseteq R_u^p
$$

be the subset of complete $p$-steps from $u$ that are actually searched before
the heuristic pass stops. Then the heuristic pass returns columns only from

$$
\widehat R^p
=
\bigcup_{u\in U^H}\widehat R_u^p.
$$

Because

$$
\widehat R^p \subsetneq R^p
$$

in general, the heuristic pass alone can never be used to terminate phase 2.

### Start ordering

For a start class

$$
u=(s,\sigma_s),
$$

let

$$
\delta^+(u)
=
\{e\in E : \text{$e$ is a feasible pricing edge outgoing from $u$}\}.
$$

Define the one-step score

$$
\eta(u)
=
\min_{e\in \delta^+(u)}(c_e-\delta_e-2\alpha_{v_e}),
$$

with

$$
\eta(u)=+\infty
\qquad \text{if } \delta^+(u)=\varnothing.
$$

The heuristic pass sorts start classes by nondecreasing $\eta(u)$ and explores
only the first

$$
min(r_{start}*|U|,K_{\mathrm{start}})
$$

classes in that order. This score is not intended to be a valid lower bound on
$\zeta(u)$; it is only a cheap dual-guided priority rule.

### Restricted search rule

Let

$$
K_{\mathrm{start}},\qquad
K_{\mathrm{col,start}},\qquad
K_{\mathrm{col}},\qquad
r_{start}
$$

be heuristic control parameters.

The heuristic phase-2 pricing pass is:

1. compute $\eta(u)$ for all $u\in U$;
2. sort $U$ by nondecreasing $\eta(u)$;
3. explore only the first $min(r_{start}*|node-state-pair-set|,K_{\mathrm{start}})$ start classes;
4. for each explored start class, run the same forward labeling algorithm as in
   exact pricing, with the same feasibility rules, symmetry-breaking rules, and
   exact dominance rule;
5. stop the search for the current start class once

$$
K_{\mathrm{col,start}}
$$

   negative reduced-cost columns have been found from that start;
6. stop the entire heuristic pass once

$$
K_{\mathrm{col}}
$$

   negative reduced-cost columns have been collected in total.

For the current $p=3$ setting, the most natural initial choice is

$$
K_{\mathrm{col,start}}=1,
$$

because the purpose is not to exhaust one start class, but to obtain a few
improving columns quickly from promising starts and then reoptimize the
restricted master.

### Exact fallback pass

At a phase-2 CG iteration, proceed as follows.

1. Solve the current restricted master LP and extract the dual solution.
2. Run the heuristic pricing pass and obtain a set

$$
\mathcal C_H \subseteq R^p.
$$

3. If

$$
\mathcal C_H \neq \varnothing,
$$

   add $\mathcal C_H$ to the restricted master and go to the next CG iteration.
4. If

$$
\mathcal C_H = \varnothing,
$$

   run the full exact pricing pass over all start classes $u\in U$.
5. Let

$$
\mathcal C_E \subseteq R^p
$$

   be the set of negative reduced-cost columns returned by exact pricing.
6. If

$$
\mathcal C_E \neq \varnothing,
$$

   add $\mathcal C_E$ to the restricted master and continue.
7. If

$$
\mathcal C_E = \varnothing,
$$

   terminate phase 2.

Phase 2 stops only after the exact pricing pass proves that no negative
reduced-cost column remains. The heuristic pass changes only the order and amount of search performed before that exact certificate is requested.

### 1st improvement

The first implementation will add only the following three phase-2 components:

1. a column pool;
2. an adaptive $K_{\mathrm{start}}$ ladder;
3. a 3-step shallow search before the current labeling-based heuristic.

A heuristic column is accepted as soon as its exact reduced cost satisfies

$$
\bar c_r < 0.
$$
#### 1. Column pool

Let

$$
\mathcal P
$$

be the phase-2 column pool. It stores previously generated feasible columns
that are not currently active in the restricted master. Each pooled column $r$
stores

$$
H_{\mathrm{pool}}(r)
=
\min\{\bar c_r^{(t)} : r \text{ was previously generated or repriced at iteration } t\}.
$$

At the beginning of each phase-2 iteration, sort $\mathcal P$ in
nondecreasing order of $H_{\mathrm{pool}}(r)$, reprice only the first

$$
K_{\mathrm{pool}}
$$

columns under the current dual solution, and add the repriced columns with

$$
\bar c_r < 0
$$

to the restricted master. In the implementation, the accepted pool columns are
still filtered by the same duplicate test and the same global per-round column
cap already used by the current pricing routine. For this first version, use

$$
N_{\mathrm{pool}} = 5000,
\qquad
K_{\mathrm{pool}} \in \{128,256\}.
$$

Pool pruning is performed in two stages. First, before repricing, remove every
pooled column that is already present in the current restricted master. Second,
if the remaining pool size exceeds

$$
N_{\mathrm{pool}},
$$

keep only the best

$$
N_{\mathrm{pool}}
$$

entries after sorting by nondecreasing $H_{\mathrm{pool}}(r)$ w.r.t best reduced cost in history, breaking ties by
the most recently stored reduced cost. Thus the pool always keeps the
historically strongest deferred columns.

If the pool yields at least one negative reduced-cost column, skip the
remaining heuristic and exact pricing steps of that iteration.

#### 2. Adaptive $K_{\mathrm{start}}$ ladder

Let

$$
U=\{u=(s,\sigma_s)\}
$$

be the set of feasible start classes in the current pricing context. Instead of
one fixed heuristic start budget, use levels

$$
K_{\mathrm{start}}^{(1)} < K_{\mathrm{start}}^{(2)} < \cdots < K_{\mathrm{start}}^{(L)}
$$

with

$$
K_{\mathrm{start}}^{(\ell)}
=
\min\!\left\{
\overline K_{\mathrm{start}},
\left\lceil \frac{l}{L}|U| \right\rceil
\right\},
\qquad \ell=1,\ldots,L,
$$

where $\overline K_{\mathrm{start}}=min(r_{start}*|U|,K_{\mathrm{start}})$ is a hard cap and
$r_{\mathrm{start}}^{(\ell)}$ is the level-dependent ratio. In this first
version, start classes are ordered only by the current one-step score

$$
\eta(u)=\min_{e\in\delta^+(u)}(c_e-\delta_e-2\alpha_{v_e}),
$$

and this order is fixed for the entire phase-2 iteration. Level $\ell$ explores
only the not-yet-explored starts in the ordered prefix

$$
\{1,\ldots,K_{\mathrm{start}}^{(\ell)}\},
$$

so the ladder is implemented as a prefix expansion of one ordered start list
rather than repeated re-exploration. Level $\ell+1$ is entered only if all
newly explored starts at level $\ell$ fail to produce any negative
reduced-cost column.

#### 3. 3-step shallow search

Because the current project uses

$$
p=3,
$$

the first heuristic attempted on a selected start class should be a shallow
enumeration rather than the full labeling algorithm. Let

$$
w_e = c_e-\delta_e-2\alpha_{v_e}
$$

be the local arc score used only for shallow ordering in phase 2.

For a nondepot start class

$$
u=(s,\sigma_s), \qquad s\neq 0,
$$

only complete 3-arc paths are admissible. Here, "feasible" means that the arc
or partial path satisfies the same local requirements already enforced in the
current pricing code: state continuity, time feasibility, elementarity, and the
pick-up / delivery ordering rules. The shallow search therefore keeps the best
$K_1$ feasible first arcs by $w_e$, then for each retained first arc keeps the
best $K_2$ feasible second arcs by $w_e$, and finally evaluates every feasible
third arc that completes a valid 3-step. Each completed 3-step is then
evaluated by the exact reduced-cost formula, and the shallow search of the
current start class is terminated as soon as $K_{\mathrm{col,start}}$ negative columns have been found, or the shallow candidate set is exhausted. The entire shallow-search phase of the current heuristic round is also terminated immediately once the cumulative number of accepted heuristic columns
reaches $K_{\mathrm{col}}.$

For a depot start class

$$
u=(0,\sigma_0),
$$

complete paths of lengths

$$
q\in\{1,2,3\}
$$

are admissible. The same shallow ordering is used, but every retained prefix of
length $q=1$ and $q=2$ is also tested immediately as a complete depot-start
column, and every feasible 3-arc completion is tested exactly as above. 

If the shallow search of a selected start finds between $1$ and
$K_{\mathrm{col,start}}$ negative columns, those columns are accepted and the
algorithm proceeds to the next CG iteration, unless the cumulative number of
accepted heuristic columns has already reached $K_{\mathrm{col}}$. If the
shallow search of a selected start finds no negative column, then that start is
declared unsuccessful at the current heuristic level and the algorithm moves to
the next selected start.

#### Updated phase-2 algorithm

For each phase-2 iteration:

1. Solve the restricted master LP and extract the dual solution.
2. Prune the column pool by removing columns already contained in the current
   restricted master, and if necessary truncate the remaining pool to
   $N_{\mathrm{pool}}$ by nondecreasing $H_{\mathrm{pool}}(r)$.
3. Run the column-pool repricing pass on the first $K_{\mathrm{pool}}$ pooled
   columns in that order. If the repriced pool yields a column with
   $\bar c_r < 0$, add the accepted pool columns to the master and start the
   next iteration.
4. Whenever a pricing pass produces deferred columns that are not inserted into
   the current master because of the duplicate filter or the global per-round
   column cap, append them to the pool, update their

$$
H_{\mathrm{pool}}(r),
$$

   and then prune again if the pool size exceeds $N_{\mathrm{pool}}$.
5. For levels $\ell=1,\ldots,L$, compute

$$
K_{\mathrm{start}}^{(\ell)}
=
\min\!\left\{
\overline K_{\mathrm{start}},
\left\lceil \frac{l}{L}|U| \right\rceil
\right\}
$$

   sort all start classes by $\eta(u)$, maintain this order for the whole
   phase-2 iteration, and at level $\ell$ explore only the starts in the prefix

$$
\{1,\ldots,K_{\mathrm{start}}^{(\ell)}\}
$$

   that have not already been explored at earlier levels.
6. For each selected start class, run the 3-step shallow search first. If it
   finds between $1$ and $K_{\mathrm{col,start}}$ columns with $\bar c_r < 0$,
   add the accepted columns to the master and start the next iteration. If the
   cumulative number of accepted heuristic columns reaches $K_{\mathrm{col}}$,
   terminate the current heuristic round immediately.
7. If all newly explored starts fail at the current heuristic level, extend the
   explored prefix to level $\ell+1$ and continue. Previously explored starts
   are not re-explored. "Accepted columns" in step 4 means feasible negative
   columns after duplicate filtering. The same heuristic round cap $K_{\mathrm{col}}$ is shared across all
   selected starts of the current heuristic level.
8. If all heuristic levels fail, run the exact pricing pass over the full start
   set $U$.
9. If exact pricing finds a negative reduced-cost column, add it to the master
   and continue.
10. If exact pricing proves that no negative reduced-cost column remains,
   terminate phase 2.

Thus the first implementation uses

$$
\text{column pool}
\to
\text{adaptive shallow search}
\to
\text{exact pricing}.
$$

The exactness condition is unchanged: phase 2 still terminates only after the
exact pricing pass certifies that no negative reduced-cost column exists.

### 2nd improvement

#### 1. Visited-node group pruning at forward extension

In the state-expanded pricing graph, many outgoing arcs from a fixed
node-state

$$
u=(x,\sigma_x)
$$

lead to the same physical head node $y$ with different head states. Since elementarity is enforced at the physical-node level, if

$$
y \in U(l)
$$

for the current label $l$, then every extension from $u$ to any $(y,\sigma(y)$ is infeasible. Hence the natural idea is to group outgoing pricing edges by head physical node and skip the whole group as soon as the head node is already visited.

The implementation was as follows.

- For each current pricing node-state $u=(x,\sigma_x)$, the outgoing extension set was partitioned by the head physical node. That is, it was decomposed as
$$
  \delta^+(u)=\bigcup_{y\in \Gamma(u)} \delta^+_y(u),
  $$
  where $\Gamma(u)$ is the set of physical head nodes reachable from $u$, and $\delta^+_y(u)$ contains all pricing edges from $u$ whose head physical node is $y$, regardless of the head state.

- Let $V(l)$ be the set of physical service nodes already visited by the current label $l$. During forward extension, before inspecting the state-specific edges inside $\delta^+_y(u)$, the algorithm first checks whether
  $$
  y\in U(l)
  $$
  If this holds, then the whole group $\delta^+_y(u)$ is skipped immediately, because every extension from $u$ to any state-expanded node $(y,\sigma_y)$ is infeasible.

- Therefore the forward extension rule becomes:
  $$
  \text{for each } y\in \Gamma(u):
  \qquad
  \text{if } y\in V_{\mathrm{vis}}(l)\text{, skip }\delta^+_y(u);
  \qquad
  \text{otherwise inspect the edges in }\delta^+_y(u).
  $$

To measure the actual impact, the heuristic pricing log was extended with

- `heuristic_outgoing_groups`: total number of outgoing groups considered
- `heuristic_visited_pruned_groups`: number of groups skipped because the head
  physical node was already visited

However, the result was weaker than expected. For the tested instances, the
fraction

$$
\frac{\texttt{heuristic\_visited\_pruned\_groups}}
{\texttt{heuristic\_outgoing\_groups}}
$$

was only about \(5\%\) in representative large iterations, and the total
pricing time did not noticeably decrease. Hence the hypothesis

> “many state-expanded head states will be eliminated at once by the visited set”

was not strongly supported empirically, at least for the current $p=3$ root
pricing setting.
