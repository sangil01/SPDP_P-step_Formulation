### New VI 1. location-subset capacity inequalities (subset version of Eq. (36))

This is the RCI variation for SPDP.

For nonempty location subsets $S \subseteq O_P$, $S \subseteq O_E$, and
$S \subseteq O_D$, respectively, define

$$
N_{P,S}=\bigcup_{o\in S}N_{P,o},
\qquad
N_{E,S}=\bigcup_{o\in S}N_{E,o},
\qquad
N_{D,S}=\bigcup_{o\in S}N_{D,o}.
$$

Thus, $N_{P,S}$, $N_{E,S}$, and $N_{D,S}$ are the pickup, treatment,
and delivery actions associated with locations in $S$.

The subset inbound indicators are defined as follows.

- $P_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}=1$ if $v_e\in N_{P,S}$ and the compressed edge enters a pickup block whose location belongs to $S$. Equivalently, $P_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}=1$ if $v_e\in N_{P,S}$ and either $\pi_e\ne\emptyset$ or $u_e\notin N_{P,S}$.
- $D_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}=1$ if $v_e\in N_{D,S}$ and the compressed edge enters a delivery block whose location belongs to $S$. Equivalently, $D_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}=1$ if $v_e\in N_{D,S}$ and either $\pi_e\ne\emptyset$ or $u_e\notin N_{D,S}$.
- $E_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}=1$ if  $\pi_e\cap S\ne\emptyset$

The inbound subset lower bounds are

$$
\sum_{e\in E}P_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}\theta_e
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{|N_{P,S}|}{2}\right\rceil
\qquad
\forall\,\emptyset\ne S\subseteq O_P,
$$

$$
\sum_{e\in E}E_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}\theta_e
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{|N_{E,S}|}{2}\right\rceil
\qquad
\forall\,\emptyset\ne S\subseteq O_E,
$$

$$
\sum_{e\in E}D_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}\theta_e
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{|N_{D,S}|}{2}\right\rceil
\qquad
\forall\,\emptyset\ne S\subseteq O_D.
$$

The symmetric outbound indicators are defined by

- $P_{e,S}^{\mathrm{out}}=1$ if $u_e\in N_{P,S}$ and either $\pi_e\ne\emptyset$ or $v_e\notin N_{P,S}$.
- $D_{e,S}^{\mathrm{out}}=1$ if $u_e\in N_{D,S}$ and either $\pi_e\ne\emptyset$ or $v_e\notin N_{D,S}$.
- $E_{e,S}^{\mathrm{out}}$ is the number of exits from maximal consecutive $S$-treatment blocks in $\pi_e$. Since $|\pi_e|\le2$, $E_{e,S}^{\mathrm{out}}=E_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}$.

The outbound subset lower bounds are

$$
\sum_{e\in E}P_{e,S}^{\mathrm{out}}\theta_e
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{|N_{P,S}|}{2}\right\rceil
\qquad
\forall\,\emptyset\ne S\subseteq O_P,
$$

$$
\sum_{e\in E}E_{e,S}^{\mathrm{out}}\theta_e
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{|N_{E,S}|}{2}\right\rceil
\qquad
\forall\,\emptyset\ne S\subseteq O_E,
$$

$$
\sum_{e\in E}D_{e,S}^{\mathrm{out}}\theta_e
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{|N_{D,S}|}{2}\right\rceil
\qquad
\forall\,\emptyset\ne S\subseteq O_D.
$$

A lighter combined version replaces each pair of inbound and outbound rows by

$$
\sum_{e\in E}
\left(P_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}+P_{e,S}^{\mathrm{out}}\right)\theta_e
\ge
2\left\lceil\frac{|N_{P,S}|}{2}\right\rceil
\qquad
\forall\,\emptyset\ne S\subseteq O_P,
$$

$$
\sum_{e\in E}
\left(E_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}+E_{e,S}^{\mathrm{out}}\right)\theta_e
\ge
2\left\lceil\frac{|N_{E,S}|}{2}\right\rceil
\qquad
\forall\,\emptyset\ne S\subseteq O_E,
$$

$$
\sum_{e\in E}
\left(D_{e,S}^{\mathrm{in}}+D_{e,S}^{\mathrm{out}}\right)\theta_e
\ge
2\left\lceil\frac{|N_{D,S}|}{2}\right\rceil
\qquad
\forall\,\emptyset\ne S\subseteq O_D.
$$


For $S=\{o\}$, the subset inequalities reduce to Eq. (36). For
$S=O_P$, $S=O_E$, or $S=O_D$, they reduce to the corresponding global
location-family lower bounds in Eq. (35). Therefore, the genuinely new rows
are associated with

$$
2\le |S|\le |O_X|-1,
\qquad
X\in\{P,E,D\}.
$$

The complete family is exponential in $|O_X|$, but the current instances have
$2\le |O_P|,|O_E|,|O_D|\le7$. Hence all intermediate subsets can be enumerated
with at most $2^7-7-2=118$ subsets per location family.

#### Proof for $\text{Inbound}\iff\text{Outbound}\iff\text{Combined}.$

The equivalence should follow directly from flow balance: once the inbound
inequality is imposed, the corresponding outbound inequality is automatically
satisfied and therefore cannot be violated.

### New VI 2. Request-block SEC

Let $N$ be the request set. For each request $i\in N$, let $P_i$ and $D_i$ denote its pickup and delivery service nodes. For a request subset $I\subseteq N$, define the associated request block by

$$
S(I)=\{P_i,D_i:i\in I\}.
$$

The request-block subtour elimination constraint (SEC) is

$$
\sum_{e\in E:u_e\in S(I),\ v_e\in S(I)}\theta_e
\le
|S(I)|-1
=
2|I|-1
\qquad
\forall I\subseteq N,\ |I|\ge2.
$$


### New VI 3. Modified VI 44

VI 44 in COR (2022) counts only actual routes and is written as follows:

$$
\sum_{e\in E:u_e=0,\ v_e\in C^p}\theta_e\ge k_{\min}.
$$

The previous implementation used only the condition `edge.u == 0`, thereby including the dummy edge $0\rightarrow\mathrm{end}$, which does not represent an actual route, on the left-hand side. Consequently, the zero-cost variable $\theta_{0,\mathrm{end}}$ could satisfy any deficit in the required number of routes, preventing VI 44 from enforcing its intended lower bound. The condition was changed to `edge.u == 0 && graph.node(edge.v).kind == Pickup`. The modified constraint therefore excludes $0\rightarrow\mathrm{end}$ and sums only the $0\rightarrow$pickup edges.

#### Sub-LP for tighter $k_{\min}$

Let

$$
\mathcal I=\{1,\ldots,n\},
\qquad
C^p=\{C_i^p:i\in\mathcal I\}=\{1,\ldots,n\},
\qquad
C^d=\{C_i^d:i\in\mathcal I\}=\{n+1,\ldots,2n\},
$$

where $C_i^p=i$ and $C_i^d=n+i$ are the pickup and delivery action nodes of request $i$. Consistently with the $p$-step formulation,

$$
V=\{0,2n+1\}\cup C^p\cup C^d,
\qquad
\bar V=V\setminus\{0,2n+1\}=C^p\cup C^d.
$$

Let $\Sigma_i^+$ be the set of feasible states after serving node $i\in\bar V$. For a fixed multigraph edge $e$, $\sigma(u_e)$ and $\sigma(v_e)$ denote the post-service states stored at its tail and head, respectively. These states are edge-specific: parallel edges incident to the same action node may carry different states. Let $t_e$ be the total duration of edge $e$, including travel through its embedded emptying-location sequence $\pi_e$, emptying operations, and service at $v_e$.

Introduce a continuous variable $y_e\in[0,1]$ for each edge, and let

$$
E_{0,2n+1}=\{e\in E:u_e=0,\ v_e=2n+1\}
$$

be the singleton set containing the dummy start-to-end edge. The following LP computes a lower bound $\underline H$ on the total duration of any integer solution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\underline H=\min_y\quad
&\sum_{e\in E}t_ey_e \\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&\sum_{e:v_e=i}y_e=1
&&\forall i\in\bar V,\\
&\sum_{e:u_e=i}y_e=1
&&\forall i\in\bar V,\\
&\sum_{\substack{e:v_e=i\\\sigma(v_e)=\sigma}}y_e
-\sum_{\substack{e:u_e=i\\\sigma(u_e)=\sigma}}y_e=0
&&\forall i\in\bar V,\ \sigma\in\Sigma_i^+,\\
&\sum_{\substack{e:u_e=0\\v_e\in C^p}}y_e
=\sum_{\substack{e:u_e\in C^d\\v_e=2n+1}}y_e,\\
&y_e=0
&&\forall e\in E_{0,2n+1},\\
&0\le y_e\le1
&&\forall e\in E\setminus E_{0,2n+1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first two constraint families visit every pickup and delivery node exactly once. The third conserves flow separately for every post-service state, and the fourth equates the numbers of actual departures and returns. The LP omits integrality, $p$-step variables and their time labels, global route connectivity, and individual route-duration constraints. It may therefore contain disconnected fractional cycles and is a relaxation of the integer route formulation.

An incomplete primal objective must not be used as $\underline H$. With an optimal objective value or a certified dual lower bound $L$ and numerical tolerance $\varepsilon$, the implementation may safely use

$$
\max\left\{
k_{\min}^{\mathrm{COR}},
\left\lceil\frac{L-\varepsilon}{T}\right\rceil
\right\}.
$$

##### Proposition 1. Validity of the duration-based route lower bound

Consider any feasible integer solution with $K$ routes and total duration $H$. Setting $y_e=1$ for its selected non-dummy edges and $y_e=0$ otherwise gives a feasible solution to the sub-LP. Hence

$$
\underline H\le H.
$$

Every route has duration at most $T$, so $H\le TK$. Since $K$ is integer,

$$
K\ge\left\lceil\frac{\underline H}{T}\right\rceil.
\tag{45}
$$

It follows that

$$
k_{\min}^{\mathrm{new}}
=
\left\lceil\frac{\underline H}{T}\right\rceil
$$

and the strengthened VI 44 is

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{e:u_e=0\\v_e\in C^p}}\theta_e
\ge k_{\min}^{\mathrm{new}}.
}
$$

##### Proposition 2. Dominance over the COR duration lower bound

For each request $i\in\mathcal I$, let $p_i$, $l_i$, and $h_i$ be its pickup location, emptying location, and container type, respectively. Its delivery action is performed at the same physical location $p_i$. Assume that the travel-time matrix is a directed metric. Define

$$
L_{\mathrm{COR}}
=n(s^p+s^e+s^d)
+\frac12\left(
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}t_{p_i,l_i}
+\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\min_{j:h_j=h_i}t_{l_i,p_j}
\right),
$$

so that $k_{\min}^{\mathrm{COR}}=\lceil L_{\mathrm{COR}}/T\rceil$. We prove that every feasible sub-LP solution $y$ satisfies

$$
\sum_{e\in E}t_ey_e\ge L_{\mathrm{COR}}.
\tag{46}
$$

For edge $e$, let $\tau_e$ be its pure travel time, $m_e$ the number of full containers emptied along $\pi_e$, and

$$
s(v_e)=
\begin{cases}
s^p,&v_e\in C^p,\\
s^d,&v_e\in C^d,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Then

$$
t_e=\tau_e+m_es^e+s(v_e).
\tag{47}
$$

The degree equations imply

$$
\sum_{e:v_e\in C^p}y_e=n,
\qquad
\sum_{e:v_e\in C^d}y_e=n.
\tag{48}
$$

It remains to count the emptying operations. Let $F(\sigma)$ be the number of full containers in state $\sigma$. Multiply each state-balance equation by $F(\sigma)$ and sum over all $(i,\sigma)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0
={}&\sum_{i\in\bar V}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_i^+}F(\sigma)
\left(
\sum_{\substack{e:v_e=i\\\sigma(v_e)=\sigma}}y_e
-\sum_{\substack{e:u_e=i\\\sigma(u_e)=\sigma}}y_e
\right)\\
={}&\sum_{\substack{e\in E\\v_e\in\bar V}}F(\sigma(v_e))y_e
-\sum_{\substack{e\in E\\u_e\in\bar V}}F(\sigma(u_e))y_e.
\end{aligned}
\tag{49}
$$

The only feasible state at either depot is $\{N,N\}$, so $F(\sigma(0))=F(\sigma(2n+1))=0$. Consequently, adding the missing depot boundary terms to (49) does not change its value, and

$$
\sum_{e\in E}
\left[F(\sigma(v_e))-F(\sigma(u_e))\right]y_e=0.
\tag{50}
$$

Equation (50) follows by reindexing the $F(\sigma)$-weighted state-balance equations: all terms at physical node-state pairs cancel, while the depot boundary terms vanish because both depot states are empty.

Along edge $e$, the emptying-location sequence converts $m_e$ full containers into empty containers, while service at $v_e$ adds one full container if and only if $v_e\in C^p$. Delivery service removes an empty container and does not change $F$. Hence

$$
F(\sigma(v_e))-F(\sigma(u_e))
=\mathbf 1_{\{v_e\in C^p\}}-m_e.
\tag{51}
$$

Substituting (51) into (50) and using (48) gives

$$
\sum_{e\in E}m_ey_e
=\sum_{e:v_e\in C^p}y_e
=n.
\tag{52}
$$

Combining (47), (48), and (52), the sub-LP objective can therefore be written as

$$
\sum_{e\in E}t_ey_e
=\sum_{e\in E}\tau_ey_e+n(s^p+s^e+s^d).
\tag{53}
$$

We next lower-bound the travel term. Let $\operatorname{loc}(v)$ denote the physical location of action node $v$. For each container type $h$, define the minimum travel time from physical location $x$ to a compatible delivery by

$$
\delta_h(x)=\min_{j:h_j=h}t_{x,p_j}.
$$

At location $x$, define the remaining container-travel potential of vehicle state $\sigma$ as

$$
\Phi(x,\sigma)
=\sum_{E(h)\in\sigma}\delta_h(x)
+\sum_{F(h,l)\in\sigma}\bigl(t_{x,l}+\delta_h(l)\bigr),
$$

For any physical movement $x\to x'$ within an edge, the directed triangle inequality implies

$$
\delta_h(x)\le t_{x,x'}+\delta_h(x'),
\qquad
t_{x,l}\le t_{x,x'}+t_{x',l}.
$$

Thus each carried container decreases $\Phi$ by at most the travel time of that movement. Moreover, the allowed state transitions have the following effects on $\Phi$:

$$
\begin{array}{rcll}
F(h,l)\to E(h) &:&\quad
t_{l,l}+\delta_h(l)=\delta_h(l),
&\text{no change at emptying location }l,\\
E(h_j)\to N &:&\quad
\delta_{h_j}(p_j)=0,
&\text{no change at delivery }C_j^d,\\
N\to F(h_i,l_i) &:&\quad
t_{p_i,l_i}+\delta_{h_i}(l_i),
&\text{increase at pickup }C_i^p.
\end{array}
$$

Since the vehicle carries at most two containers, summing along the physical path represented by edge $e$ gives the edge-wise inequality

$$
2\tau_e\ge
\Phi\bigl(\operatorname{loc}(u_e),\sigma(u_e)\bigr)
-\Phi\bigl(\operatorname{loc}(v_e),\sigma(v_e)\bigr)
+\sum_{\substack{i\in\mathcal I\\v_e=C_i^p}}
\left[t_{p_i,l_i}+\delta_{h_i}(l_i)\right].
\tag{54}
$$

The pickup term in (54) corrects for the fact that $\sigma(v_e)$ is the state after service at $v_e$. If $v_e=C_i^p$, pickup service increases the arrival potential by $t_{p_i,l_i}+\delta_{h_i}(l_i)$; hence

$$
-\Phi\bigl(\operatorname{loc}(v_e),\sigma(v_e)\bigr)
+t_{p_i,l_i}+\delta_{h_i}(l_i)
=-\Phi\bigl(\operatorname{loc}(v_e),\text{state before serving }C_i^p\bigr).
$$

Thus the right-hand side of (54) is exactly the potential decrease caused by travel on $e$, not including service for $v_e$ . 

Multiplying (54) by $y_e$ and summing over $e\in E$ yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\sum_{e\in E}\tau_ey_e
\ge{}&\sum_{e\in E}\left[
\Phi\bigl(\operatorname{loc}(u_e),\sigma(u_e)\bigr)
-\Phi\bigl(\operatorname{loc}(v_e),\sigma(v_e)\bigr)
\right]y_e\\
&+\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}
\left[t_{p_i,l_i}+\delta_{h_i}(l_i)\right]
\sum_{e:v_e=C_i^p}y_e.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first term is zero by the state-balance equations and $\Phi(\cdot,\{N,N\})=0$ at both depots. The pickup-degree equations make the final edge sum equal to one for every $i$. Therefore

$$
\sum_{e\in E}\tau_ey_e
\ge\frac12\left(
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}t_{p_i,l_i}
+\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\min_{j:h_j=h_i}t_{l_i,p_j}
\right).
\tag{55}
$$

Substitution of (55) into (53) proves (46). Since (46) holds for every feasible $y$,

$$
\underline H
=\min_y\sum_{e\in E}t_ey_e
\ge L_{\mathrm{COR}}.
$$

The monotonicity of the ceiling function therefore gives

$$
\boxed{
k_{\min}^{\mathrm{new}}
=\left\lceil\frac{\underline H}{T}\right\rceil
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{L_{\mathrm{COR}}}{T}\right\rceil
=k_{\min}^{\mathrm{COR}}.
}
$$
