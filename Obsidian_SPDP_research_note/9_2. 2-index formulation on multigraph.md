
## Multigraph

Let

$$
\mathcal I=\{1,\ldots,n\},
\qquad
C^p=\{C_i^p:i\in\mathcal I\},
\qquad
C^d=\{C_i^d:i\in\mathcal I\},
$$

and

$$
V=\{0,2n+1\}\cup C^p\cup C^d,
\qquad
\bar V=C^p\cup C^d.
$$

The state-expanded directed multigraph is $G=(V,E)$. An edge $e\in E$ stores

$$
e=\bigl(u_e,v_e,\pi_e,\sigma(u_e),\sigma(v_e),t_e,c_e\bigr),
$$

where $\pi_e$ is the embedded sequence of emptying locations, $\sigma(u_e)$ and $\sigma(v_e)$ are the post-service vehicle states at its endpoints, and $t_e$ and $c_e$ are its duration and original cost. Service feasibility, container capacity, emptying operations, and container-state transitions are therefore embedded in $E$. Let $\Sigma_i^+$ be the set of feasible post-service states at $i\in\bar V$, and define

$$
\delta^-(i)=\{e\in E:v_e=i\},
\qquad
\delta^+(i)=\{e\in E:u_e=i\}.
$$

The start-to-end dummy-edge set is

$$
E_{0,2n+1}=\{e\in E:u_e=0,\ v_e=2n+1\}.
$$

## Decision variables

For every multigraph edge, let

$$
y_e=
\begin{cases}
1,&\text{if edge }e\text{ is selected},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

For every physical node-state pair, let

$$
B_{i,\sigma}\in[0,T]
\qquad
(i\in\bar V,\ \sigma\in\Sigma_i^+)
$$

denote the cumulative duration immediately after serving $i$ in state $\sigma$.

## Formulation

Let $w_e=c_e$ for the original SPDP objective and $w_e=t_e$ for the minimum-total-duration objective. The direct 2-index formulation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_{y,B}\quad
&\sum_{e\in E}w_ey_e \\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&\sum_{e\in\delta^-(i)}y_e=1
&&\forall i\in\bar V,\\
&\sum_{e\in\delta^+(i)}y_e=1
&&\forall i\in\bar V,\\
&\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^-(i)\\\sigma(v_e)=\sigma}}y_e
-\sum_{\substack{e\in\delta^+(i)\\\sigma(u_e)=\sigma}}y_e=0
&&\forall i\in\bar V,\ \sigma\in\Sigma_i^+,\\
&\sum_{\substack{e:u_e=0\\v_e\in C^p}}y_e
=\sum_{\substack{e:u_e\in C^d\\v_e=2n+1}}y_e,\\
&B_{v_e,\sigma(v_e)}\ge t_ey_e
&&\forall e\in E:u_e=0,\ v_e\in C^p,\\
&B_{v_e,\sigma(v_e)}
\ge B_{u_e,\sigma(u_e)}+t_e-(T+t_e)(1-y_e)
&&\forall e\in E:u_e,v_e\in\bar V,\\
&B_{u_e,\sigma(u_e)}+t_ey_e\le T
&&\forall e\in E:u_e\in C^d,\ v_e=2n+1,\\
&y_e=0
&&\forall e\in E_{0,2n+1},\\
&y_e\in\{0,1\}
&&\forall e\in E\setminus E_{0,2n+1},\\
&0\le B_{i,\sigma}\le T
&&\forall i\in\bar V,\ \sigma\in\Sigma_i^+.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

The first two constraint families perform every pickup and delivery action exactly once. The state-balance equations join incoming and outgoing edges only when their post-service states agree. Depot balance equates the numbers of actual $0\rightarrow C^p$ departures and $C^d\rightarrow(2n+1)$ returns; the dummy edge is excluded because it does not represent a route.

When $y_e=1$, the three time-constraint families propagate cumulative duration along the selected edge and impose the limit $T$ on each depot-to-end route. Moreover, summing the internal-edge constraints around a disconnected directed cycle $C$ gives

$$
0\ge\sum_{e\in C}t_e,
$$

which is impossible for a non-dummy cycle of positive duration. Thus every selected component is a state-consistent depot-to-end route, and (1) represents the SPDP feasible set on the multigraph. If the $B$ variables and time constraints are omitted, disconnected integer cycles may remain and the resulting model is only a relaxation.

The LP relaxation is obtained by replacing $y_e\in\{0,1\}$ with $0\le y_e\le1$. Its optimal value is a valid root-node lower bound for the corresponding objective.

## Valid inequalities

All edge-based valid inequalities can be added directly in the form

$$
\sum_{e\in E}a_e^j y_e\ \{\ge,\le\}\ b_j.
$$

This includes VI 35, the location-subset VI 36, the request-block SEC, and the modified VI 44

$$
\sum_{\substack{e:u_e=0\\v_e\in C^p}}y_e\ge k_{\min}.
$$

The two formulations both prove optimality for 11 instances. The 2-step formulation additionally proves A8 and A9, whereas the 2-index formulation additionally proves A10 and C3; neither proves B1 or C4. Their incumbents are identical in 16 instances, while for C3 the 2-index formulation proves the optimum 2269 and the 2-step formulation returns 2279.

The Gurobi logs give the following interpretation.

- **Dual-bound stagnation in the 2-index formulation.** The final incumbents of A8, A9, and C4 are found within approximately 2, 1, and 4 seconds, respectively. **For A8, root cutting raises the bound from 1298.110 to 1301, after which 7.31 million branch-and-bound nodes produce no further global improvement. A9 similarly reaches 1243 at the root and remains there after 15.44 million nodes.** C4 improves from 1431 to 1474, mostly within the first 25 seconds, and then stalls. Thus these runs are limited by proving the lower bound rather than finding feasible solutions. Since VI 44 already enforces the correct two-route lower bound, the residual gaps arise from within-route sequencing, container matching, and time feasibility.

- **Why the 2-step formulation solves A8 and A9.** Its root bound is 3.082 higher for A8 but is exactly the same for A9. Nevertheless, it proves A8 and A9 using only 1,969 and 16,942 nodes. Hence its advantage cannot be explained only by the root value. **A 2-step variable couples two consecutive edges and their intermediate time-state, so fixing an edge through the linking equations removes many incompatible local transitions simultaneously. In contrast, the big-$M$ time constraints of the 2-index relaxation are weak when edge variables are fractional. This gives the 2-step formulation stronger descendant-node relaxations and more effective branching on these instances.**

- **Why the 2-index formulation is usually faster.** Full 2-step enumeration retains essentially the same binary edge variables and adds hundreds of thousands of continuous step variables. **For the difficult instances, the 2-step model has approximately 31--48 times as many columns as the 2-index model. It therefore takes 28--244 seconds merely to finish root processing and begin branching, while the 2-index model usually begins within 1--4 seconds. For example, A10 has the same root bound 1277 in both models, but the 2-index formulation proves 1297 in 11.37 seconds whereas the 2-step formulation stops at LB 1293.** For the 11 instances solved by both, their total main-solver times are 13.94 and 277.00 seconds, respectively; compact 2-step generation adds only 3.15 seconds, so set generation is not the main bottleneck.

- **B1, C3, and C4.** For B1, the 2-step root bound is slightly stronger, but its expensive node relaxations permit only 4,103 processed nodes and yield final LB 1262.31, compared with 1286 from the 2-index model. For C3, the raw 2-step root bound is 9 higher, but root cutting raises the 2-index bound to approximately 2253.75 and the 2-step bound to only 2191.38. The 2-index model then finds 2269 within 5 seconds and proves optimality in 6.21 seconds, whereas the much larger 2-step model finds only 2279 and terminates with LB 2222.58 and a 2.48% gap. Thus its difficulty is caused by weaker generic cutting, expensive node relaxations, and slower primal search rather than by the initial root bound. C4 benefits from the 2-step formulation, whose final LB is 1480.87 rather than 1474, but remains difficult for both. Its two incumbent routes have durations 453 and 475 under $T=480$, indicating that route-level time packing is particularly restrictive and is not captured exactly by either fractional relaxation.

- **Other observations.** Neither request count nor the initial root gap alone predicts difficulty: A9 has only a 0.32% initial gap but is extremely hard for the 2-index model, whereas the larger-root-gap C3 is solved quickly. Once the correct route count is already enforced, however, further improvement requires inequalities addressing sequencing and route-level resource feasibility rather than another aggregate route-count bound.
