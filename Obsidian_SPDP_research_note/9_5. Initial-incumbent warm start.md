
## Motivation

In most SPDP instances considered here, the fixed cost per vehicle dominates the travel cost. Using the minimum feasible number of vehicles is therefore essential for obtaining a low-cost solution. Nevertheless, for some instances the original-cost MILP has difficulty finding an incumbent that uses this minimum number, even when VI 44 provides the corresponding route-count lower bound. The resulting weak upper bound can make the reported root-LP gap appear much larger than the weakness of the LP relaxation itself. To address this issue, a feasible solution is first generated by solving a duration-minimization IP with the smallest known vehicle count fixed, and the resulting solution is supplied to the main original-cost MILP as an initial incumbent.

## Fixed-vehicle-count incumbent problem

Let $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{IP}}$ denote the integer feasible region of the time-extended 2-index formulation on the multigraph, and define

$$
K(y)=
\sum_{\substack{e\in E:u_e=0\\v_e\in C^p}}y_e,
\qquad
D(y)=\sum_{e\in E}t_ey_e.
$$

After computing the selected valid lower bound $\widehat k$ for VI 44, the following auxiliary IP is solved:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_{y,B}\quad &D(y)\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &(y,B)\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{IP}},\\
&K(y)=\widehat k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

The duration objective guides the feasibility search; its optimal value is not required. The solver terminates at the first feasible solution. If (1) finds a solution, the validity of $\widehat k$ gives

$$
\widehat k\le k_{\min}^{*}\le K(y)=\widehat k,
$$

and hence $k_{\min}^{*}=\widehat k$. Failure to find a solution within the time limit does not establish infeasibility and the main algorithm simply continues without an initial incumbent.

The selected edge support is decomposed into depot-to-end routes and checked for complete action coverage, state consistency, capacity feasibility, empty terminal states, and the route-duration limit $T$. Its original cost

$$
C(y)=\sum_{e\in E}c_ey_e
$$

is then evaluated and the solution is mapped to the formulation used by the main solver. For the direct 2-index model, the binary edge values are supplied as a complete MIP start. For a $p$-step enumeration master, the recovered routes are decomposed into compatible $p$-step columns; **any required valid column omitted by enumeration pruning is restored and its coefficients are appended before the master is constructed**. In branch-and-price, the corresponding columns seed the initial restricted master and the recovered route solution supplies the incumbent upper bound.

## Experiment

### For LP optimal gap

The procedure was tested on all 31 instances having more than 20 and at most 50 requests. The main problem was the direct 2-index original-cost MILP with modified VI 44 and a 30-second time limit. The time-extended duration sub-IP used to compute $\widehat k$ had a 300-second limit. It was solved to optimality for 30 instances; C12 reached the limit with incumbent 2347 and certified lower bound 2337, which still gives $\widehat k=5$. The fixed-$\widehat k$ incumbent problem had a 60-second limit in the initial experiment.

All 31 incumbent solutions passed route validation and were explicitly accepted by Gurobi as MIP starts. The main solver improved the original cost of every start; thus the start supplied an upper bound without restricting subsequent optimization. Sixteen instances were solved to optimality within 30 seconds. The mean and median root gaps, measured against the final incumbent, were 0.99% and 0.72%, respectively; the largest was 3.56% for C11. The mean final gap was 0.45%, and the largest was 3.17% for C11.

Incumbent generation required 707.39 seconds in total, with a mean of 22.82 seconds and a median of 4.31 seconds. The main incumbent-generation outliers are C12 and C8, which required 284.37 and 212.98 seconds, respectively. This is consistent with the distinction between the two auxiliary problems: the duration sub-IP used to derive $\widehat k=\lceil\underline H/T\rceil$ does not fix the number of routes, whereas the incumbent problem must construct an explicit feasible packing into exactly $\widehat k$ routes.

All 48 incumbent problems found route-valid solutions and the main solver improved every initial cost. The duration sub-IP was solved to optimality for 47 instances; only C12 reached 600 seconds, with incumbent 2347 and certified lower bound 2346, yielding $\widehat k=5$. Incumbent generation required 762.92 seconds in total (mean 15.89, median 2.28), dominated by C12 (288.24 seconds) and C8 (213.82 seconds).

**The 3600-second main IP proved optimality for 36 of 48 instances**. The mean and median final gaps were 0.15% and 0.00%, and the largest was 1.40% for C4. On the same 31 larger instances used above, 23 were solved to optimality, compared with 16 under the 30-second limit; the mean final gap decreased from 0.45% to 0.14%, and the largest decreased from 3.17% to 1.22% (C12). Thus the warm start consistently supplied a valid minimum-route upper bound, while the remaining one-hour branch-and-bound gaps were small.
