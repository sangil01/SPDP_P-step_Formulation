
## 1. Atomic-action anchored-circuit CP-SAT formulation

### 1.1 Purpose

The current minimum-fleet search uses a time-extended 2-index MILP on the state-expanded multigraph. For a candidate fleet size $K$, the MILP must construct exactly $K$ complete, state-consistent, duration-feasible routes. This fixed-$K$ feasibility problem becomes difficult on several large instances: C19 and D5 were proved infeasible at the tested values, while C16, C17, C18, C20, and D6 reached the time limit without an incumbent.

The purpose of the CP model is to replace this auxiliary MILP feasibility check. Whenever CP finds a feasible exact-$K$ solution, that solution is converted into the existing multigraph edge variables and supplied to the main MILP as a complete warm start. Whenever CP proves the exact-$K$ model infeasible at the current certified frontier, the fleet-size lower bound can be increased.

The proposed model is an **atomic-action anchored-circuit CP-SAT formulation**. Pickup, emptying, and delivery are explicit actions. A single global circuit, separated by $K$ pairs of route anchors, represents exactly $K$ open routes. Ordinal reservoir constraints enforce vehicle capacity and type-compatible empty-container inventories. Reified timing equalities enforce route durations without big-$M$ constraints.

The formulation is a pure satisfaction problem: it has no objective function. A feasible solution is sufficient for incumbent generation, and a solver status of `INFEASIBLE` is used as an exact infeasibility conclusion for the CP model.

### 1.2 Modeling assumptions

The formulation uses the following properties of the SPDP instances studied in this project.

1. Each request generates one full skip at its recycling center and specifies one treatment facility.
2. After emptying, skip identity is irrelevant. An empty skip can satisfy any delivery demand of the same container type on the same vehicle.
3. Vehicle capacity is two skips. A full-to-empty conversion does not change the number of skips onboard.
4. Routes are open: there is no travel time from a physical depot to the first pickup and no travel time from the last delivery to a depot.
5. Every route starts empty and must end empty.
6. There are no action-specific time windows. The only temporal restriction is the route-duration limit $T$.
7. Emptying time is additive per emptied skip.
8. The travel-time matrix is nonnegative, has zero diagonal, and satisfies the triangle inequality.
9. Treatment facilities have no additional congestion or simultaneous-processing capacity constraints.

## 2. Notation

### 2.1 Requests, locations, and types

Let

$$
I=\{1,\ldots,n\}
$$

be the request set, and let

$$
\mathcal L
$$

be the physical-location set. For each request $i\in I$:

- $\alpha_i\in\mathcal L$ is its recycling-center location;
- $\beta_i\in\mathcal L$ is its designated treatment location;
- $f_i\in\mathcal F$ is its container type, where $\mathcal F$ is the set of container types.

Let

$$
t_{\ell\ell'}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}
$$

be the travel time from location $\ell$ to location $\ell'$. The data satisfy

$$
t_{\ell\ell}=0,
$$

and

$$
t_{\ell\ell'}
\le
t_{\ell h}+t_{h\ell'}
\qquad
\forall \ell,\ell',h\in\mathcal L.
$$

The service times are

$$
s^P,
\qquad
s^H,
\qquad
s^D,
$$

for pickup, emptying, and delivery, respectively. The maximum duration of one route is $T$.

### 2.2 Atomic actions

Each request $i$ creates three mandatory actions:

$$
P_i: \text{pickup of the full skip at }\alpha_i,
$$

$$
H_i: \text{emptying of that full skip at }\beta_i,
$$

$$
D_i: \text{one empty-skip delivery demand at }\alpha_i.
$$

Define

$$
P=\{P_i:i\in I\},
\qquad
H=\{H_i:i\in I\},
\qquad
D=\{D_i:i\in I\},
$$

and

$$
A=P\cup H\cup D.
$$

The action locations are

$$
\ell(P_i)=\alpha_i,
\qquad
\ell(H_i)=\beta_i,
\qquad
\ell(D_i)=\alpha_i,
$$

and their durations are

$$
d(P_i)=s^P,
\qquad
d(H_i)=s^H,
\qquad
d(D_i)=s^D.
$$

The delivery action $D_i$ is a delivery **demand**, not the required return of the physical skip picked up by $P_i$. Consequently, the formulation does not impose $P_i\prec D_i$ or require $P_i$ and $D_i$ to use the same vehicle. Flexible return is instead enforced through the type-inventory reservoir in Section 6.6.

### 2.3 Vehicles and exact fleet size

For a tested fleet size $K$, let

$$
\mathcal K=\{1,\ldots,K\}
$$

be the vehicle-index set. The CP model uses exactly $K$ nonempty routes. This is stronger than an at-most-$K$ formulation and is intentionally chosen to improve propagation in the fixed-fleet feasibility search.

## 3. Anchored circuit construction

### 3.1 Route anchors

For every $k\in\mathcal K$, introduce a start anchor $S_k$ and an end anchor $Z_k$. The global circuit node set is

$$
N
=
A
\cup
\{S_k,Z_k:k\in\mathcal K\}.
$$

The anchors partition one global circuit into $K$ ordered open-route segments.

### 3.2 Allowed arcs

Let $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{CP}}$ contain the following arcs.

**Route-start arcs**

$$
(S_k,P_i)
\qquad
\forall k\in\mathcal K,\ i\in I.
$$

Only a pickup may follow a start anchor.

**Internal action arcs**

$$
(a,b)
\qquad
\forall a,b\in A,\ a\ne b,
$$

**Route-end arcs**

$$
(D_i,Z_k)
\qquad
\forall i\in I,\ k\in\mathcal K.
$$

Only a delivery may precede an end anchor.

**Artificial connector arcs**

$$
(Z_k,S_{k+1})
\qquad
k=1,\ldots,K-1,
$$

and

$$
(Z_K,S_1).
$$

These connector arcs join the $K$ open routes into one circuit. They do not represent physical travel and do not carry time or inventory transitions.

No arc $(S_k,Z_k)$ is created. Hence every anchor segment contains at least one physical route, and the model uses exactly $K$ nonempty vehicles.

**Arc pruning**

The following arcs are excluded from $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{CP}}$ and are therefore not passed to `AddCircuit`.

1. **Direct precedence violation.** For every $i\in I$, exclude

$$
(H_i,P_i),
$$

because it contradicts the required precedence $P_i\prec H_i$.

2. **Consecutive same-action ordering (COR constraints (40a)--(40c)).** If two consecutive actions of the same kind occur at the same physical location, retain only increasing request-index order:

$$
x_{P_iP_j}=0
\qquad
i,j\in I,\quad \alpha_i=\alpha_j,\quad i>j,
$$

$$
x_{H_iH_j}=0
\qquad
i,j\in I,\quad \beta_i=\beta_j,\quad i>j,
$$

and

$$
x_{D_iD_j}=0
\qquad
i,j\in I,\quad \alpha_i=\alpha_j,\quad i>j.
$$

Adjacent actions in each pair have the same location and service duration, so exchanging their execution order preserves travel time, route duration, and container feasibility. These rules select one canonical order without removing a physical SPDP solution. In implementation, the corresponding Boolean arc variables are not created.

3. **Same-location cross-action ordering (COR constraints (41a)--(41c)).** In the notation of this formulation, the paper's delivery location $\omega_i$ is $\ell(D_i)=\alpha_i$. Exclude the following immediate transitions:

$$
x_{P_iD_j}=0
\qquad
i,j\in I,\quad \alpha_i=\alpha_j,
$$

$$
x_{H_iP_j}=0
\qquad
i,j\in I,\quad \beta_i=\alpha_j,
$$

and

$$
x_{D_iH_j}=0
\qquad
i,j\in I,\quad \alpha_i=\beta_j.
$$

These rules choose a canonical ordering for different actions performed consecutively at the same physical location. The corresponding local exchanges add no travel and do not worsen load or empty-container availability. As with constraints (40), they are implemented by omitting the prohibited arcs rather than by creating arc variables and fixing them to zero.

### 3.3 Arc variables and the global circuit

For every $(a,b)\in\mathcal E^{\mathrm{CP}}$, let

$$
x_{ab}\in\{0,1\}
$$

indicate whether the arc is selected.

The routing structure is imposed by the CP-SAT global constraint

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Circuit}
\left(
N,
\{(a,b,x_{ab}):(a,b)\in\mathcal E^{\mathrm{CP}}\}
\right)
}
\tag{CP-1}
$$

together with

$$
x_{Z_kS_{k+1}}=1
\qquad
k=1,\ldots,K-1,
$$

and

$$
x_{Z_KS_1}=1.
\tag{CP-2}
$$

In OR-Tools, (CP-1) is one `AddCircuit` constraint containing all allowed arcs. Equations (CP-2) are Boolean equalities fixing the artificial connector literals to one. Because action nodes have no self-loops, every pickup, emptying, and delivery action is visited exactly once.

The resulting circuit has the form

$$
S_1
\rightarrow \text{route}_1
\rightarrow Z_1
\rightarrow S_2
\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow
Z_K
\rightarrow S_1.
$$

## 4. Decision variables

For every action $a\in A$:

$$
v_a\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
$$

is the vehicle assigned to $a$;

$$
p_a\in\{1,\ldots,3n\}
$$

is the ordinal position of $a$ within its route; and

$$
\tau_a\in\{0,\ldots,T-d(a)\}
$$

is the start time of $a$.

For every action $a\in A$ and vehicle $k\in\mathcal K$, define

$$
q_{ak}\in\{0,1\}
$$

with the interpretation

$$
q_{ak}=1
\iff
v_a=k.
$$

The one-hot vehicle assignment is imposed by

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}q_{ak}=1
}
\qquad
\forall a\in A,
\tag{CP-3}
$$

and the integer vehicle variable is channeled by

$$
\boxed{
v_a=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}kq_{ak}
}
\qquad
\forall a\in A.
\tag{CP-4}
$$

Equations (CP-3)--(CP-4) are ordinary CP-SAT linear constraints. The Boolean variables $q_{ak}$ are also used as activation literals in the reservoir constraints.

## 5. Circuit channeling and time propagation

### 5.1 First action of each route

For every route-start arc,

$$
\boxed{
x_{S_kP_i}=1
\Longrightarrow
\left(
q_{P_i k}=1,
\ p_{P_i}=1,
\ \tau_{P_i}=0
\right)
}
\tag{CP-5}
$$

for all $k\in\mathcal K$ and $i\in I$.

The implication is implemented by three constraints with `OnlyEnforceIf(x[S_k,P_i])`. The first action starts at time zero because routes are open and incur no travel from a depot.

### 5.2 Consecutive physical actions

For every internal action arc $(a,b)$,

$$
\boxed{
x_{ab}=1
\Longrightarrow
v_b=v_a
}
\tag{CP-6}
$$

$$
\boxed{
x_{ab}=1
\Longrightarrow
p_b=p_a+1
}
\tag{CP-7}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
x_{ab}=1
\Longrightarrow
\tau_b
=
\tau_a+d(a)+t_{\ell(a),\ell(b)}
}
\tag{CP-8}
$$

are imposed.

These are reified equality constraints, each implemented with `OnlyEnforceIf(x[a,b])`. Constraint (CP-8) contains no big-$M$ term. Equality is valid because the problem has no action-specific time windows; any waiting can be removed without affecting feasibility.

### 5.3 Last action and route duration

For every route-end arc,

$$
\boxed{
x_{D_iZ_k}=1
\Longrightarrow
q_{D_i k}=1
}
\tag{CP-9}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
x_{D_iZ_k}=1
\Longrightarrow
\tau_{D_i}+s^D\le T
}
\tag{CP-10}
$$

are imposed.

The connector arcs $(Z_k,S_{k+1})$ have no timing constraints. Constraint (CP-10) therefore limits the duration of each open route independently.

## 6. Container feasibility

### 6.1 Pickup-to-treatment linkage

The full skip created by pickup $P_i$ must be emptied by $H_i$ on the same vehicle:

$$
\boxed{
q_{P_i k}=q_{H_i k}
}
\qquad
\forall i\in I,\ k\in\mathcal K.
\tag{CP-11}
$$

The pickup must precede its treatment action:

$$
\boxed{
p_{P_i}<p_{H_i}
}
\qquad
\forall i\in I.
\tag{CP-12}
$$

Because (CP-11) places the two actions on the same route, their ordinal positions are directly comparable. Constraints (CP-11)--(CP-12) retain the identity of a full skip only until it reaches its designated treatment location. After $H_i$, only its container type matters.

### 6.2 Total onboard-load changes

For interpretation, define the action-dependent load change

$$
\Delta^L(P_i)=+1,
$$

$$
\Delta^L(H_i)=0,
$$

$$
\Delta^L(D_i)=-1.
\tag{11}
$$

Equation (11) is a **definition**, not an additional CP-SAT constraint. It explains the event list supplied to the global reservoir constraint. Pickup adds one skip to the vehicle, delivery removes one skip, and emptying changes only the status of a skip.

### 6.3 Total-load reservoir: the actual CP constraint

For each vehicle $k\in\mathcal K$, impose

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Reservoir}
\left(
\{p_{P_i},p_{D_i}\}_{i\in I},
\{+1,-1\}_{i\in I},
\{q_{P_i k},q_{D_i k}\}_{i\in I},
0,2
\right)
}
\qquad
\forall k\in\mathcal K.
\tag{CP-13}
$$

The four arguments after the event list have the following CP meanings:

- **event time:** $p_{P_i}$ or $p_{D_i}$;
- **level change:** $+1$ for pickup and $-1$ for delivery;
- **activation literal:** $q_{P_i k}$ or $q_{D_i k}$;
- **level bounds:** minimum $0$ and maximum $2$.

No $H_i$ event is inserted because its level change is zero.

Thus, the boxed `Reservoir` expression is the constraint that actually enters the CP model. The following level function is only its mathematical interpretation:

$$
L_k(r)
=
\sum_{i\in I}
q_{P_i k}\mathbf 1[p_{P_i}\le r]
-
\sum_{j\in I}
q_{D_j k}\mathbf 1[p_{D_j}\le r].
\tag{12}
$$

The global constraint enforces

$$
0\le L_k(r)\le2
\qquad
\forall r,
\tag{13}
$$

without explicitly creating a level variable for every ordinal position $r$. Equations (12)--(13) explain the semantics of (CP-13); they are not separately expanded into linear constraints in the implementation.

Ordinal positions, rather than service start times, are used as reservoir event times. This avoids ambiguity when two consecutive actions can have the same physical location and zero travel time.

### 6.4 Empty-container type changes

For each type $f\in\mathcal F$, define

$$
\Delta^{E_f}(H_i)
=
\begin{cases}
+1,&f_i=f,\\
0,&f_i\ne f,
\end{cases}
$$

and

$$
\Delta^{E_f}(D_i)
=
\begin{cases}
-1,&f_i=f,\\
0,&f_i\ne f.
\end{cases}
\tag{14}
$$

Again, (14) is a definition used to construct the reservoir event lists. Emptying produces one empty skip of type $f_i$, while a delivery demand consumes one empty skip of its required type.

### 6.5 Type-inventory reservoirs: the actual CP constraints

For every vehicle $k\in\mathcal K$ and type $f\in\mathcal F$, impose

$$
\operatorname{Reservoir}
\left(
\{p_{H_i}:f_i=f\}_{i\in I}
\cup
\{p_{D_j}:f_j=f\}_{j\in I},
\{+1:f_i=f\}_{i\in I}
\cup
\{-1:f_j=f\}_{j\in I},
\{q_{H_i k}:f_i=f\}_{i\in I}
\cup
\{q_{D_j k}:f_j=f\}_{j\in I},
0,2
\right)
\quad
\forall k\in\mathcal K,\ f\in\mathcal F.
\tag{CP-14}
$$

The resulting inventory level is interpreted as

$$
E_{kf}(r)
=
\sum_{i:f_i=f}
q_{H_i k}\mathbf 1[p_{H_i}\le r]
-
\sum_{j:f_j=f}
q_{D_j k}\mathbf 1[p_{D_j}\le r],
\tag{15}
$$

and the global reservoir enforces

$$
0\le E_{kf}(r)\le2
\qquad
\forall r.
\tag{16}
$$

Equations (15)--(16) are explanatory semantics, not separately generated position-indexed constraints. In particular, the lower bound in (CP-14) prevents a delivery from consuming an empty skip of type $f$ before such a skip has been produced by an $H_i$ action on the same vehicle.

### 6.6 Why the two reservoir families are sufficient

At any route prefix, the number of full skips onboard is

$$
F_k(r)
=
\sum_{i\in I}
q_{P_i k}\mathbf 1[p_{P_i}\le r]
-
\sum_{i\in I}
q_{H_i k}\mathbf 1[p_{H_i}\le r].
$$

It is nonnegative because each $P_i$ precedes its same-vehicle $H_i$. The total number of empty skips onboard is

$$
E_k(r)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal F}E_{kf}(r),
$$

which is nonnegative because of the type reservoirs. Their sum is

$$
F_k(r)+E_k(r)=L_k(r).
$$

Consequently, (CP-13) enforces the physical capacity of two skips, while (CP-14) enforces the availability of a type-compatible empty skip for every delivery. Explicit pickup-to-delivery matching variables are unnecessary.

## 7. Redundant strengthening

The constraints in this section are not required for formulation exactness. They expose consequences of the core formulation to additional CP-SAT propagators and may therefore detect infeasible partial assignments before the complete circuit order is fixed.

### 7.1 Terminal-balance constraints

For every vehicle $k$, impose

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{i\in I}q_{P_i k}
=
\sum_{j\in I}q_{D_j k}
}
\qquad
\forall k\in\mathcal K.
\tag{CP-15}
$$

For every vehicle $k$ and type $f$:

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{i:f_i=f}q_{H_i k}
=
\sum_{j:f_j=f}q_{D_j k}
}
\qquad
\forall k\in\mathcal K,\ f\in\mathcal F.
\tag{CP-16}
$$

Unlike the derived level functions, (CP-15)--(CP-16) are actual linear constraints added to the model. They are redundant because the global action counts are balanced, every reservoir has a nonnegative terminal level, and the sum of the vehicle terminal levels is zero. Their purpose is to expose the implied per-vehicle terminal balance directly.

### 7.2 Full-skip reservoir

For each vehicle $k$, add a reservoir that tracks only full skips:

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Reservoir}
\left(
\{p_{P_i},p_{H_i}\}_{i\in I},
\{+1,-1\}_{i\in I},
\{q_{P_i k},q_{H_i k}\}_{i\in I},
0,2
\right)
}
\qquad
\forall k\in\mathcal K.
\tag{CP-17}
$$

Its level is interpreted as

$$
F_k(r)
=
\sum_{i\in I}q_{P_i k}\mathbf 1[p_{P_i}\le r]
-
\sum_{i\in I}q_{H_i k}\mathbf 1[p_{H_i}\le r].
$$

The core formulation already implies $0\le F_k(r)\le2$ because $F_k(r)=L_k(r)-\sum_fE_{kf}(r)$. Constraint (CP-17) nevertheless exposes this inventory to one dedicated propagator and can reject a partial route containing too many pickups without sufficiently early treatment actions.

### 7.3 Vehicle-assignment container-workload bound

Define the set of type-compatible pickup--delivery pairs

$$
\mathcal M
=
\{(i,j)\in I\times I:f_i=f_j\}.
$$

For each $(i,j)\in\mathcal M$ and $k\in\mathcal K$, introduce

$$
\lambda_{ijk}\in\{0,1\},
$$

where $\lambda_{ijk}=1$ means that, for the purpose of this workload lower bound, the empty skip produced by $H_i$ is matched to delivery demand $D_j$ on vehicle $k$. Impose

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{j:f_j=f_i}\lambda_{ijk}
=q_{P_i k}
}
\qquad
\forall i\in I,\ k\in\mathcal K,
\tag{CP-18a}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{i:f_i=f_j}\lambda_{ijk}
=q_{D_j k}
}
\qquad
\forall j\in I,\ k\in\mathcal K.
\tag{CP-18b}
$$

The service workload assigned to vehicle $k$ is

$$
S_k
=
\sum_{i\in I}
\left(
s^Pq_{P_i k}
+s^Hq_{H_i k}
+s^Dq_{D_i k}
\right),
$$

and its container-travel workload is

$$
W_k
=
\sum_{i\in I}t_{\alpha_i,\beta_i}q_{P_i k}
+
\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal M}
t_{\beta_i,\alpha_j}\lambda_{ijk}.
$$

Each term in $W_k$ follows one container from its pickup location to its treatment location and then to a compatible delivery location. Because a vehicle carries at most two skips, its route travel time is at least $W_k/2$. The vehicle-duration strengthening is therefore

$$
\boxed{
2S_k+W_k\le2T
}
\qquad
\forall k\in\mathcal K.
\tag{CP-18c}
$$

The doubled form is used so that CP-SAT receives only integer coefficients. A separate constraint $S_k\le T$ is not included: it is implied by (CP-18c) and is rarely binding for the studied instances because service time alone is small relative to $T$.

The matching variables construct an assignment-based travel lower bound; they do not identify the actual empty skip consumed by a delivery. Consequently, no implication $\lambda_{ijk}=1\Rightarrow p_{H_i}<p_{D_j}$ is required. Every feasible SPDP route induces at least one matching satisfying (CP-18a)--(CP-18c), so these constraints do not remove any feasible SPDP solution.

### 7.4 Aggregate duration bound

Let

$$
\mathcal E_A^{\mathrm{CP}}
=
\{(a,b)\in\mathcal E^{\mathrm{CP}}:a,b\in A\}
$$

be the internal physical-action arcs. Summing the duration limits of the $K$ routes gives the redundant inequality

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{a\in A}d(a)
+
\sum_{(a,b)\in\mathcal E_A^{\mathrm{CP}}}
t_{\ell(a),\ell(b)}x_{ab}
\le KT
}.
\tag{CP-19}
$$

The service term is constant because every action is mandatory. The second term is the total travel time of all selected internal action arcs. Start, end, and artificial connector arcs contribute no physical travel under the open-route convention. Constraint (CP-19) exposes the global duration budget before the selected actions have been fully partitioned into ordered routes.

### 7.5 Optional implementation

Every redundant family in this section is implemented as an independent model-construction option so that it can be enabled or disabled for ablation experiments. A disabled family is omitted from the CP-SAT model rather than enforced through an additional activation literal. In particular, the $\lambda$ variables are created only when the container-workload strengthening (CP-18) is enabled.

## 8. Symmetry breaking

### 8.1 Vehicle symmetry breaking

The $K$ route anchors label otherwise identical vehicles. Let

$$
g_k
=
\sum_{i\in I} i\,x_{S_kP_i}
$$

be the index of the first pickup on route $k$. Since every start anchor has exactly one outgoing pickup arc, $g_k$ is well defined.

Impose

$$
\boxed{
g_k+1\le g_{k+1}
}
\qquad
k=1,\ldots,K-1.
\tag{CP-20}
$$

This orders the vehicle routes by their first pickup index and removes route-label permutations. It is an actual linear symmetry-breaking constraint.

### 8.2 Request symmetry breaking

Under flexible return, two pickup requests $i$ and $j$ are interchangeable up to their treatment actions when

$$
\alpha_i=\alpha_j,
\qquad
\beta_i=\beta_j,
\qquad
f_i=f_j.
$$

Their pickup locations, designated treatment locations, container types, and service durations are identical. The pair labels $(P_i,H_i)$ and $(P_j,H_j)$ can therefore be exchanged without changing the physical route or its feasibility. For every such pair with $i<j$, impose the flexible-return request-ordering rule corresponding to COR constraint (43):

$$
\boxed{
\tau_{P_i}\le \tau_{P_j}
}
\qquad
i<j,\quad
i,j\in I,\quad
\alpha_i=\alpha_j,\quad
\beta_i=\beta_j,\quad
f_i=f_j.
\tag{CP-21}
$$

The delivery actions are deliberately absent from the equivalence condition. Under flexible return, $D_i$ is not paired with the full skip collected by $P_i$; after $H_i$, only the empty skip's type remains relevant. Constraint (CP-21) therefore orders interchangeable pickup--treatment pairs without imposing the invalid precedence $P_i\prec D_i$ or requiring $P_i$ and $D_i$ to use the same vehicle.

Because the selected-arc timing constraints (CP-8) are equalities, $\tau_{P_i}$ is the exact route-elapsed start time of $P_i$, rather than a freely delayed lower bound. Consequently, (CP-21) removes request-label permutations instead of being satisfied through artificial waiting.

The symmetry arc-pruning families (40) and (41) and constraints (CP-20)--(CP-21) are implemented as independent options so that their effects can be measured separately. The direct precedence exclusion $(H_i,P_i)$ is part of the core allowed-arc definition and is never disabled. Arc pruning removes prohibited literals at model-construction time; disabled symmetry families do not create auxiliary activation literals.

## 9. Treatment normalization and equivalence to the current SPDP semantics

### 9.1 Difference between the action model and the multigraph

The CP model schedules one explicit $H_i$ action for each request. The current multigraph instead embeds treatment visits inside an edge and, at a visit to location $\ell$, empties all currently onboard full skips whose designated treatment is $\ell$.

Therefore, a raw CP action sequence may postpone one $H_j$ even though another skip is already being emptied at the same treatment location. This does not represent the canonical multigraph behavior directly, but it can be normalized without losing feasibility under the assumptions in Section 1.2.

### 9.2 Normalization rule

Traverse each CP route from left to right. When the route visits treatment location $\ell$ for an $H_i$ action, define

$$
\mathcal C_\ell
=
\left\{
j\in I:
\begin{array}{l}
P_j\text{ has already been performed},\\
H_j\text{ has not yet been performed},\\
\beta_j=\ell,\\
P_j\text{ and the current action use the same vehicle}
\end{array}
\right\}.
$$

Replace the current treatment visit by a consecutive block containing $H_j$ for all $j\in\mathcal C_\ell$, and delete the corresponding later $H_j$ actions.

An $H_j$ action cannot be moved to a visit occurring before $P_j$. A later visit remains necessary when its associated full skip was not onboard at the earlier visit.


### 9.3 Exactness statement

Under the stated assumptions, the fixed-exact-$K$ CP formulation and the original SPDP feasibility problem have the same feasible solutions up to treatment normalization.

**SPDP to CP.** Expand every embedded treatment visit of a feasible SPDP route into one $H_i$ action for each skip emptied at that visit. Insert the route anchors and the artificial connector arcs, and assign the corresponding vehicle, position, time, and reservoir events. This produces a feasible CP solution.

**CP to SPDP.** Decompose a feasible CP circuit into $K$ anchored routes, apply treatment normalization, and interpret each normalized treatment block as one physical treatment visit. Constraints (CP-11)--(CP-14), together with the globally balanced action counts, guarantee correct pickup-to-treatment movement, vehicle capacity, type-compatible empty delivery, and empty terminal states. The optional constraints (CP-15)--(CP-19) only expose consequences of these core constraints. Constraints (CP-5)--(CP-10) guarantee a valid open route of duration at most $T$.

The two-direction argument establishes formulation exactness. In production, only the CP-to-SPDP conversion is required because the CP solution is used to create a MILP warm start.

## 10. Complete formulation

For a tested exact fleet size $K$, the CP-SAT feasibility problem is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{find}\quad
&x,v,p,\tau,q
\quad\text{and, if (CP-18) is enabled, }\lambda\\
\text{subject to}\quad
&\text{anchored global circuit constraints }(\mathrm{CP\text{-}1})\text{--}(\mathrm{CP\text{-}2}),\\
&\text{one-hot vehicle assignment }(\mathrm{CP\text{-}3})\text{--}(\mathrm{CP\text{-}4}),\\
&\text{circuit channeling and time propagation }(\mathrm{CP\text{-}5})\text{--}(\mathrm{CP\text{-}10}),\\
&\text{pickup-to-treatment linkage }(\mathrm{CP\text{-}11})\text{--}(\mathrm{CP\text{-}12}),\\
&\text{total-load reservoirs }(\mathrm{CP\text{-}13}),\\
&\text{type-inventory reservoirs }(\mathrm{CP\text{-}14}),\\
&\text{optional redundant strengthening }(\mathrm{CP\text{-}15})\text{--}(\mathrm{CP\text{-}19}),\\
&\text{vehicle and request symmetry breaking }(\mathrm{CP\text{-}20})\text{--}(\mathrm{CP\text{-}21}).
\end{aligned}
$$

There is no objective function. In OR-Tools terminology, the core global constraints are one `AddCircuit` constraint and $K(1+|\mathcal F|)$ reservoir constraints. Enabling (CP-17) adds $K$ full-skip reservoirs, and enabling (CP-18) adds the matching variables and linear matching constraints. All other conditions are linear equalities, linear inequalities, or reified linear equalities implemented with `OnlyEnforceIf`.

The status interpretation is:

- `FEASIBLE` or `OPTIMAL`: a valid exact-$K$ CP witness was found;
- `INFEASIBLE`: the exact-$K$ CP model was proved infeasible;
- `UNKNOWN`: no feasibility conclusion was proved before a limit stopped the solver;
- `MODEL_INVALID`: the generated CP-SAT model is invalid and the result must not be used.

## 11. Iterative exact-$K$ search

Let $K^{\mathrm{search}}$ be the fleet size currently tested, and let $K^{\mathrm{cert}}$ be the smallest fleet size not ruled out by a contiguous sequence of valid infeasibility proofs.

The operational search intentionally solves an **exact-$K^{\mathrm{search}}$** formulation at every attempt.

1. Initialize $K^{\mathrm{search}}$ with the selected valid route-count lower bound.
2. Solve the exact-$K^{\mathrm{search}}$ CP formulation.
3. If the status is feasible, convert the solution into a multigraph warm start.
4. If the status is infeasible and $K^{\mathrm{search}}=K^{\mathrm{cert}}$, update

   $$
   K^{\mathrm{cert}}\leftarrow K^{\mathrm{cert}}+1.
   $$

5. If the status is `UNKNOWN`, the implementation may still try

   $$
   K^{\mathrm{search}}\leftarrow K^{\mathrm{search}}+1
   $$

   to search for an incumbent with a larger exact fleet size, but it must not increase $K^{\mathrm{cert}}$.

This policy deliberately prefers the stronger propagation of exact-$K$ route anchors over an at-most-$K$ model. The trade-off is that an `UNKNOWN` attempt creates a certification gap. For example, `UNKNOWN` at $K=8$ followed by a feasible solution at $K=9$ provides a nine-vehicle upper bound but does not prove $k_{\min}=9$.

## 12. Conversion to the multigraph MILP warm start

Every accepted CP incumbent is converted into the binary edge variables of the existing 2-index multigraph formulation.

### 12.1 Route extraction

Cut the global circuit at the artificial connector arcs $(Z_k,S_{k+1})$ to recover the $K$ ordered action routes.

### 12.2 Treatment normalization

Apply the normalization rule in Section 9 to every route. This makes each treatment visit consistent with the current multigraph rule that all eligible onboard full skips are emptied at that visit.

### 12.3 Construction of embedded treatment sequences

Remove the explicit $H_i$ actions from the physical service-node sequence. Between each consecutive pair of pickup/delivery service actions $u$ and $v$, collect the normalized treatment blocks in their route order. Their ordered physical locations form the multigraph treatment sequence

$$
\pi_e=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r).
$$

If several $H_i$ actions are processed in one block at the same treatment location, that location appears once in $\pi_e$, while the edge duration charges $s^H$ once per emptied skip.

### 12.4 State reconstruction and edge lookup

Starting from the empty vehicle state, replay every pickup, treatment block, and delivery. For every consecutive physical service-node pair $(u,v)$, reconstruct

$$
(u,v,\sigma_u,\sigma_v,\pi_e).
$$

First attempt to identify an existing multigraph edge with this exact embedded sequence. If that edge was removed by parallel-edge dominance, use a surviving edge with the same endpoint-state key

$$
(u,v,\sigma_u,\sigma_v)
$$

and no greater duration. Such a replacement preserves both endpoint states and physical service coverage, although its embedded treatment sequence may differ from the CP-derived $\pi_e$. Store the original multigraph edge ID of the selected surviving representative.

### 12.5 MIP-start vector

Set

$$
y_e^{\mathrm{start}}=1
$$

for every recovered route edge and

$$
y_e^{\mathrm{start}}=0
$$

for all other multigraph edges. The complete edge vector is passed to the direct 2-index original-cost MILP as its MIP start.

Before the start is submitted, the existing route recovery and validation logic must accept the selected edge support. A CP witness that cannot be mapped to the current pruned multigraph or fails route validation is not supplied to the MILP.

## 13. Initial validation protocol

The initial implementation should be validated against already known feasibility results.

1. On small instances with known $K^*$, the CP model must be feasible at $K^*$.
2. On the same instances, it must be infeasible at $K^*-1$.
3. Every feasible CP solution must survive treatment normalization, multigraph-edge lookup, and the existing route validator.
4. D5 must reproduce infeasibility at $K=7$ and feasibility at $K=8$.
5. C19 must reproduce infeasibility at $K=12$ before testing $K=13$.
6. The symmetry families corresponding to COR constraints (40), (41), and (43), together with the first-pickup vehicle symmetry constraint (CP-20), should each be tested independently on small instances before being enabled by default.

These checks validate the implementation of the already specified exact formulation; they are not additional production constraints. The triangle inequality is treated as a problem assumption and is not included as a runtime validation requirement.

### Threshold-optimization mode

The initial implementation uses a satisfaction model with the hard route-duration constraints

$$
C_k\le T
\qquad
\forall k\in\mathcal K,
$$

where $C_k$ is the completion time of route $k$. A later experiment should compare this formulation with a threshold-optimization mode that uses the CP-SAT objective bound as an additional infeasibility certificate.

Define the practical route-duration upper bound

$$
\boxed{
U=\left\lceil1.5T\right\rceil.
}
$$
The value $U$ is a deliberately moderate computational upper bound on every represented route completion time. It is not claimed to be a valid upper bound on the unrestricted minimum-makespan problem. Such an unrestricted bound is unnecessary for the threshold decision. The only required property is

$$
U>T,
$$

so that every solution satisfying the original limit $C_k\le T$ remains in the optimization model, while the model also contains a useful band of solutions with makespan above $T$. The factor $1.5$ is chosen as a compromise: it gives the solver room to construct over-threshold incumbents and raise an objective lower bound past $T$, without using an excessively loose time domain. For the studied instances, $T=480$ and hence $U=720$.

For every vehicle $k$, introduce a route-completion variable

$$
C_k\in\{0,\ldots,U\}.
$$

If delivery $D_i$ is the last action of route $k$, impose

$$
\boxed{
x_{D_iZ_k}=1
\Longrightarrow
C_k=\tau_{D_i}+s^D
}
\qquad
\forall i\in I,\ k\in\mathcal K.
$$

Introduce the makespan variable

$$
M\in\{0,\ldots,U\},
$$

and impose the maximum equality

$$
\boxed{
M=\max_{k\in\mathcal K}C_k
}
$$

In OR-Tools this is one `AddMaxEquality` constraint. The weaker inequalities $M\ge C_k$ would be sufficient under minimization, but the global maximum equality states the intended semantics directly and may propagate more strongly.

The threshold-optimization model is

$$
\boxed{
\min M
}
$$

subject to

$$
C_k\le U=\left\lceil1.5T\right\rceil
\qquad
\forall k\in\mathcal K.
$$

The original hard constraints $C_k\le T$ are removed. The action-time domains must also be enlarged consistently:

$$
0\le\tau_a\le U-d(a)
\qquad
\forall a\in A.
$$

If the original domains $\tau_a\le T-d(a)$ were retained, over-threshold routes could not be represented and the model would collapse back to the original satisfaction formulation. Optional duration-based strengthening constraints must likewise use $U$, rather than $T$, when they are intended to remain redundant for the threshold model.


Let $M^{UB}$ be the objective value of the best incumbent and let $M^{LB}$ be the valid objective lower bound returned by CP-SAT. The exact-$K$ threshold result is interpreted as follows.

A validated incumbent satisfying

$$
\boxed{
M^{UB}\le T
}
$$

proves that the original exact-$K$ SPDP is feasible.

A valid objective bound satisfying

$$
\boxed{
M^{LB}>T
}
$$

proves that no exact-$K$ solution can have every route completed by $T$. Therefore, the original exact-$K$ SPDP is infeasible.

If the threshold-optimization model itself is proved infeasible under $U=\lceil1.5T\rceil$, the original exact-$K$ SPDP is also infeasible, because every solution satisfying $C_k\le T$ is contained in the restricted horizon.

Finding an incumbent with

$$
T<M^{UB}\le U
$$

does not by itself prove original infeasibility. It only provides a feasible solution to the relaxed-horizon problem. The original result remains unresolved until either the lower bound exceeds $T$ or the restricted model is proved infeasible.

Consequently, the stopping rules are

$$
M^{UB}\le T
\Longrightarrow
\text{FEASIBLE},
$$

$$
M^{LB}>T
\Longrightarrow
\text{INFEASIBLE},
$$

and otherwise

$$
\text{UNKNOWN}.
$$

The threshold-optimization mode may guide the search toward balanced routes and may certify original infeasibility through the objective lower bound before the relaxed problem is solved to optimality. However, it is not guaranteed to dominate the satisfaction model. Replacing the hard constraints $C_k\le T$ with $C_k\le U$ weakens direct threshold propagation, and the objective lower bound may still progress slowly. The factor $1.5$ is therefore an experimental upper-bound choice and should be compared with the satisfaction formulation rather than treated as universally optimal.

The two modes should therefore be implemented as alternatives:

1. **Satisfaction mode**
   $$
   C_k\le T,
   $$
   with no objective;

2. **Threshold-optimization mode**
   $$
   C_k\le \left\lceil1.5T\right\rceil,
   \qquad
   \min M.
   $$


**Implementation.** A feasible-solution observer with $M^{UB}\le T$ must save the complete CP witness before calling `StopSearch`; it is classified as `OriginalFeasible` only after conversion and route validation succeed.
A best-bound callback with $M^{LB}>T$ stores the proving bound, calls `StopSearch`, and is classified by the wrapper as `OriginalInfeasibleByObjectiveBound` regardless of the solver's externally stopped status.

### Multigraph-based CP formulation

#### Multigraph and CP arc set

Let

$$
G=(V,E),
$$

be the same state-expanded directed multigraph used by the direct 2-index MILP. Its physical service-node set is

$$
\bar V=P\cup D,
$$

with original start node $0$ and end node $z$. Every edge

$$
e=
\bigl(
u_e,v_e,
\sigma_e^-,\pi_e,\sigma_e^+,
t_e,c_e
\bigr)
$$

stores its tail and head service nodes, the post-service state at the tail, the embedded ordered treatment-location sequence $\pi_e$, the post-service state at the head, its duration $t_e$, and its original cost $c_e$. The duration includes travel through $\pi_e$, $s^H$ for every emptied skip, and service at the destination node. The edge set therefore already enforces capacity, designated treatment locations, treatment batching, type-compatible delivery, and the full-to-empty state transition.

Partition the non-dummy edges into

$$
E^S=\{e\in E:u_e=0,\ v_e\in P\},
$$

$$
E^I=\{e\in E:u_e,v_e\in\bar V\},
$$

and

$$
E^Z=\{e\in E:u_e\in D,\ v_e=z\}.
$$

The original dummy edge $(0,z)$ is not used. For an exact fleet size $K$, introduce route anchors $S_k,Z_k$ for every $k\in\mathcal K$. Construct the CP arc set as follows.

1. Every internal multigraph edge $e\in E^I$ gives one CP arc $(u_e,v_e)$ with a distinct literal, even when another parallel edge has the same physical endpoints.
2. Every $e\in E^S$ is copied to an anchored start arc $(S_k,v_e)$ for every $k$.
3. Every $e\in E^Z$ is copied to an anchored end arc $(u_e,Z_k)$ for every $k$.
4. The artificial connector arcs $(Z_k,S_{k+1})$ and $(Z_K,S_1)$ are fixed to one.

No arc $(S_k,Z_k)$ is created, so every route is nonempty. Only the start and end edge sets are copied by vehicle; the much larger internal edge set $E^I$ is not replicated $K$ times.

Let

$$
x_e\in\{0,1\}
\qquad e\in E^I,
$$

$$
x^S_{ek}\in\{0,1\}
\qquad e\in E^S,\ k\in\mathcal K,
$$

and

$$
x^Z_{ek}\in\{0,1\}
\qquad e\in E^Z,\ k\in\mathcal K
$$

be the corresponding arc literals. Parallel multigraph edges remain distinct CP arcs because their endpoint states, embedded treatment sequences, or durations may differ.

#### Anchored multigraph circuit

Let

$$
N^{MG}=\bar V\cup\{S_k,Z_k:k\in\mathcal K\}.
$$

Impose one CP-SAT global circuit:

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Circuit}
\left(
N^{MG},
\widehat E^{MG}
\right)
}
\tag{MGCP-1}
$$

where $\widehat E^{MG}$ contains all internal, anchored-start, anchored-end, and fixed connector arcs defined above. Because the service nodes have no self-loops, every pickup and delivery is visited exactly once. The fixed connectors partition the circuit into exactly $K$ open routes:

$$
S_1\rightarrow\text{route}_1\rightarrow Z_1
\rightarrow S_2\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow
Z_K\rightarrow S_1.
$$

#### State continuity

For every service node $i\in\bar V$, let $\Sigma_i^+$ be its feasible post-service state set. Start-edge copies inherit the head state of their original edge, end-edge copies inherit the tail state, and internal edges retain both endpoint states.

For every $i\in\bar V$ and $\sigma\in\Sigma_i^+$, impose

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{e\in E^I:v_e=i\\\sigma_e^+=\sigma}}x_e
+
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
\sum_{\substack{e\in E^S:v_e=i\\\sigma_e^+=\sigma}}x^S_{ek}
=
\sum_{\substack{e\in E^I:u_e=i\\\sigma_e^-=\sigma}}x_e
+
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
\sum_{\substack{e\in E^Z:u_e=i\\\sigma_e^-=\sigma}}x^Z_{ek}
}
\tag{MGCP-2}
$$

The circuit chooses one incoming and one outgoing physical arc at each service node; (MGCP-2) additionally requires the incoming end state and outgoing start state to agree. Consequently, incompatible parallel edges cannot be joined. No load, full-skip, or empty-type reservoir is needed: the corresponding inventory transition is part of each edge and state continuity is imposed directly.

For each service node $i\in\bar V$, introduce

$$
v_i\in\mathcal K,
\qquad
p_i\in\{1,\ldots,2n\},
$$

and one-hot assignment literals

$$
q_{ik}\in\{0,1\},
\qquad
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}q_{ik}=1,
\qquad
v_i=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}kq_{ik}.
\tag{MGCP-3}
$$

For every anchored start edge,

$$
\boxed{
x^S_{ek}=1
\Longrightarrow
\left(q_{v_e k}=1,\ p_{v_e}=1\right)
}
\qquad
e\in E^S, k\in\mathcal K.
\tag{MGCP-4}
$$

For every internal edge,

$$
\boxed{
x_e=1
\Longrightarrow
\left(v_{v_e}=v_{u_e},\ p_{v_e}=p_{u_e}+1\right)
}
\qquad
e\in E^I.
\tag{MGCP-5}
$$

For every anchored end edge,

$$
\boxed{
x^Z_{ek}=1
\Longrightarrow q_{u_e k}=1
}
\qquad
e\in E^Z, k\in\mathcal K.
\tag{MGCP-6}
$$

The $q$ variables are not required for basic route construction, but they expose the vehicle partition to the redundant strengthening and vehicle-symmetry constraints below.

#### Exact timing and makespan

For every service node $i\in\bar V$, let

$$
B_i\in\{0,\ldots,U\}
$$

be the cumulative route duration immediately after serving $i$. A single $B_i$ is sufficient because the circuit visits node $i$ exactly once and (MGCP-2) selects exactly one compatible state there.

For each anchored start edge,

$$
\boxed{
x^S_{ek}=1
\Longrightarrow
B_{v_e}=t_e
}
\qquad
e\in E^S, k\in\mathcal K.
\tag{MGCP-7}
$$

For each internal edge,

$$
\boxed{
x_e=1
\Longrightarrow
B_{v_e}=B_{u_e}+t_e
}
\qquad
e\in E^I.
\tag{MGCP-8}
$$

These are reified equalities implemented with `OnlyEnforceIf`; no big-$M$ coefficient is used. For every vehicle, introduce

$$
C_k\in\{0,\ldots,U\},
$$

and impose

$$
\boxed{
x^Z_{ek}=1
\Longrightarrow
C_k=B_{u_e}+t_e
}
\qquad
e\in E^Z, k\in\mathcal K.
\tag{MGCP-9}
$$

Under the open-route convention, the final edge normally has zero travel to the virtual end node, but (MGCP-9) uses its stored duration and therefore remains consistent with the graph data.

For threshold optimization, define

$$
U=\left\lceil1.5T\right\rceil,
$$

introduce

$$
M\in\{0,\ldots,U\},
$$

and impose the CP-SAT maximum equality

$$
\boxed{
M=\max_{k\in\mathcal K}C_k
}
\tag{MGCP-10}
$$

with objective

$$
\boxed{
\min M.
}
\tag{MGCP-11}
$$

In OR-Tools, (MGCP-10) is one `AddMaxEquality` constraint. The satisfaction alternative sets the time-variable upper bound to $T$, imposes $C_k\le T$, and omits (MGCP-10)--(MGCP-11).

#### Redundant strengthening

The atomic-action strengthening families have the following multigraph counterparts. Let

$$
L=
\begin{cases}
T,&\text{satisfaction mode},\\
U,&\text{threshold-optimization mode}.
\end{cases}
$$

Using $U$ in the threshold mode keeps the constraints redundant for the relaxed-horizon model and does not remove the over-threshold incumbents needed by the objective search.

**Terminal balance.** For every vehicle $k$,

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{i\in I}q_{P_i k}
=
\sum_{j\in I}q_{D_j k}
}
\tag{MGCP-12}
$$

and, for every container type $f$,

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{i:f_i=f}q_{P_i k}
=
\sum_{j:f_j=f}q_{D_j k}
}
\qquad
k\in\mathcal K, f\in\mathcal F.
\tag{MGCP-13}
$$

The explicit treatment count from (CP-16) is replaced by the pickup count because each selected multigraph route carries every picked-up full skip through its designated embedded treatment before it can end empty.

**Full-skip-state strengthening.** The logical information exposed by the optional full-skip reservoir (CP-17) is already part of the multigraph core. Every endpoint state records its full skips, every edge applies the exact treatment transition, and (MGCP-2) propagates that state between consecutive edges. Adding a second reservoir over reconstructed treatment actions would duplicate the state-expanded graph and is therefore not included. This strengthening is retained structurally rather than as an optional global constraint.

**Vehicle-assignment container-workload bound.** For every type-compatible pair $(i,j)$ and vehicle $k$, retain the matching variables

$$
\lambda_{ijk}\in\{0,1\},
$$

with

$$
\sum_{j:f_j=f_i}\lambda_{ijk}=q_{P_i k},
\qquad
\sum_{i:f_i=f_j}\lambda_{ijk}=q_{D_j k}.
\tag{MGCP-14}
$$

Define

$$
S_k
=
\sum_{i\in I}(s^P+s^H)q_{P_i k}
+
\sum_{j\in I}s^Dq_{D_j k},
$$

and

$$
W_k
=
\sum_{i\in I}t_{\alpha_i,\beta_i}q_{P_i k}
+
\sum_{\substack{i,j\in I\\f_i=f_j}}
t_{\beta_i,\alpha_j}\lambda_{ijk}.
$$

Then impose

$$
\boxed{
2S_k+W_k\le2L
}
\qquad
k\in\mathcal K.
\tag{MGCP-15}
$$

The treatment service term is charged once per pickup even though treatment is embedded in $\pi_e$, matching the current graph duration convention.

**Aggregate duration.** Let the physical selected-edge duration be

$$
D^{\mathrm{all}}
=
\sum_{e\in E^I}t_ex_e
+
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\sum_{e\in E^S}t_ex^S_{ek}
+
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\sum_{e\in E^Z}t_ex^Z_{ek}.
$$

Add

$$
\boxed{
D^{\mathrm{all}}\le KL.
}
\tag{MGCP-16}
$$

This is redundant because the selected edge durations telescope to $\sum_k C_k$ and every route completion is bounded by $L$.

The optional terminal-balance, container-workload, and aggregate-duration families are independently switchable and are omitted at model-construction time when disabled. The full-skip information is always present because it belongs to the multigraph state definition itself.

#### Symmetry breaking and graph preprocessing

The multigraph CP model uses the same graph instance and therefore inherits the MILP's infeasibility filtering, parallel-edge dominance, and COR arc pruning before any CP literal is created. A removed edge is absent from the CP model rather than represented by a literal fixed to zero.

The multigraph interpretations of COR (40)--(41) are as follows.

1. Consecutive same-location pickup--pickup and delivery--delivery edges with empty $\pi_e$ retain only the increasing request-index order corresponding to (40a) and (40c). Repeated identical treatment actions are already batched and represented canonically inside $\pi_e$, which provides the counterpart of (40b).
2. A same-location pickup-to-delivery transition with empty $\pi_e$ is removed as in (41a).
3. An edge whose nonempty $\pi_e$ ends at the location of its head pickup is removed as the embedded-treatment counterpart of (41b).
4. An edge leaving a delivery whose nonempty $\pi_e$ begins at that same location is removed as the embedded-treatment counterpart of (41c).

The CP backend follows the graph builder's enabled preprocessing configuration exactly; it does not create a second, inconsistent pruning layer.

For vehicle symmetry, define the first-pickup index

$$
g_k
=
\sum_{e\in E^S}\operatorname{req}(v_e)x^S_{ek},
$$

where $\operatorname{req}(P_i)=i$. Impose

$$
\boxed{
g_k+1\le g_{k+1}
}
\qquad
k=1,\ldots,K-1.
\tag{MGCP-17}
$$

For requests with identical pickup location, treatment location, and type, retain the COR (43) global ordering:

$$
\boxed{
B_{P_i}\le B_{P_j}
}
\qquad
i<j,\quad
\alpha_i=\alpha_j,\quad
\beta_i=\beta_j,\quad
f_i=f_j.
\tag{MGCP-18}
$$

The variable $B_{P_i}$ is the cumulative completion time after pickup rather than its arrival time. Because equivalent pickups have the same service duration, (MGCP-18) induces the same ordering. The vehicle and request symmetry families remain independently switchable for ablation experiments.

For a tested exact fleet size $K$, the threshold-optimization variant is

\text{s.t.}\quad

&\text{vehicle and position channeling }(\mathrm{MGCP\text{-}3})
\text{--}(\mathrm{MGCP\text{-}6}),\\

\text{--}(\mathrm{MGCP\text{-}16}),\\

The threshold result uses the same stopping rules as above:

\Longrightarrow

\Longrightarrow

and otherwise the result is `UNKNOWN`. A proof that the restricted $U$-bounded model is infeasible also proves original infeasibility.

#### Exactness and warm-start conversion

Every selected CP arc is an existing multigraph edge or an anchor-specific copy of an existing start/end edge. The global circuit supplies exactly $K$ ordered depot-to-end paths, (MGCP-2) joins only compatible states, and (MGCP-7)--(MGCP-9) sum the exact stored edge durations. Hence a feasible multigraph CP solution is directly a feasible solution of the existing edge-based SPDP model under the selected horizon.

Conversely, any exact-$K$ feasible multigraph solution can be ordered by its $K$ depot-to-end routes, assigned to the anchors, and used to satisfy the circuit, channeling, state, and timing constraints. The optional strengthening and symmetry rules preserve at least one representative of every physical solution.

Unlike the atomic-action adapter, this formulation requires neither treatment normalization nor a search for a surviving parallel edge. To create the MILP warm start, collapse each anchored start/end copy back to its original edge ID and set

$$
y_e^{\mathrm{start}}=1
$$

for every selected original multigraph edge. The resulting edge vector is still passed through the existing route recovery and validator before being accepted.

#### Expected computational trade-off

The multigraph formulation removes the explicit $H_i$ actions, all reservoir constraints, and the post-solution treatment adapter. It also gives each circuit literal a complete local state transition rather than a location-only action adjacency. These properties can strengthen propagation and eliminate the reservoir precedence expansion observed in the atomic-action model.

Its risk is graph size. Large instances may contain hundreds of thousands of surviving multigraph edges, and each parallel edge becomes a CP Boolean literal with reified state, vehicle, position, and time consequences. The formulation should therefore be implemented as a separate backend rather than replacing either the atomic-action CP model or the existing MILP before controlled experiments.
