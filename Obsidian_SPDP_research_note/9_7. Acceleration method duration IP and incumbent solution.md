
# Acceleration of the duration IP and incumbent search

## 1. Setting

Let $G=(V,E)$ be the state-expanded multigraph. Each edge $e\in E$ stores its tail and head service nodes, endpoint states, embedded treatment sequence, duration $t_e$, and original cost $c_e$. The duration IP is

$$
H^*=\min_{(y,B)\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{IP}}}
\sum_{e\in E}t_ey_e,
\tag{1}
$$

where $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{IP}}$ is the integer feasible region of the time-extended 2-index formulation. Its duration-based vehicle lower bound is

$$
\widehat k_D=\left\lceil\frac{H^*}{T}\right\rceil.
\tag{2}
$$

## 2. Arc elimination

### 2.1 Parallel-edge dominance

Partition the edge set by the endpoint-state key

$$
\chi(e)=
\bigl(u_e,v_e,\sigma_e(u_e),\sigma_e(v_e)\bigr).
$$

For each parallel class $P(\chi)=\{e\in E:\chi(e)=\chi\}$, retain one representative

$$
e^*(\chi)\in\arg\min_{e\in P(\chi)}t_e
\tag{3}
$$

and eliminate the remaining edges. In the present multigraph, parallel edges with the same endpoint-state key perform the same endpoint service and empty the same number of skips. Their duration difference is therefore determined only by the embedded routing travel, so (3) is equivalent to retaining a minimum-travel-time edge.

Any feasible solution using $e\in P(\chi)$ can replace it by $e^*(\chi)$. Node degrees, state flow, service coverage, and route count are unchanged, while every affected route duration weakly decreases. Hence this elimination preserves both feasibility and the optimal value of (1).

The same argument applies to the exact-$K$ incumbent problem. Although that problem only requires a feasible solution, replacing a removed parallel edge by its retained representative preserves the same $K$ routes. Parallel-edge elimination is therefore safe for both the duration IP and fixed-$K$ incumbent generation.

### 2.2 Empty-state connector elimination

Consider an internal delivery-to-pickup connector $e=(u,v)$ such that the vehicle is empty immediately after delivery $u$ and immediately before pickup $v$. Splitting the route at this connector replaces $e$ by the free open-route arcs $u\rightarrow d$ and $0\rightarrow v$. All service and state transitions are preserved, and

$$
D(q^L)+D(q^R)=D(q)-\tau_e^{\mathrm{tr}}
\le D(q),
\tag{4}
$$

where $\tau_e^{\mathrm{tr}}\ge0$ is the travel time from $u$ to $v$. Repeated splitting therefore shows that, when the fleet size is not fixed or upper-bounded, an optimal solution of (1) exists without internal empty-state delivery-to-pickup connectors. Such edges may consequently be omitted from the duration IP.

This elimination is **not generally valid for the exact-$K$ incumbent problem**. Splitting one connector increases the number of routes by one and may violate the equality $K(y)=K$. The fact that the incumbent problem is used only as a feasibility check does not remove this restriction. Thus the fixed-$K$ incumbent model may use the parallel-edge elimination in Section 2.1, but must retain empty-state connectors unless a separate route-count-preserving argument is available.

## 3. Bound-based early termination

During the solution of (1), let $H^{\mathrm{UB}}$ be the objective value of the best feasible incumbent and let $H^{\mathrm{LB}}$ be the certified global lower bound. Then

$$
H^{\mathrm{LB}}\le H^*\le H^{\mathrm{UB}}.
\tag{5}
$$

Since $x\mapsto\lceil x/T\rceil$ is nondecreasing, if

$$
\boxed{
\left\lceil\frac{H^{\mathrm{LB}}}{T}\right\rceil
=
\left\lceil\frac{H^{\mathrm{UB}}}{T}\right\rceil
=\widehat k_D,
}
\tag{6}
$$

then (5) implies

$$
\left\lceil\frac{H^*}{T}\right\rceil=\widehat k_D.
\tag{7}
$$

The duration IP can therefore terminate as soon as (6) holds. This certifies the exact rounded duration bound $\widehat k_D$ without certifying $H^*$ itself. It also does not prove that $\widehat k_D$ is the exact minimum feasible fleet size, because route-level packing incompatibilities may require more than $\lceil H^*/T\rceil$ vehicles.

Using a conservative lower bound can only delay early termination; it cannot overstate $\widehat k_D$. The callback terminates the solve only after a feasible incumbent exists and the two rounded values agree. By contrast, fixed-$K$ incumbent generation already terminates at the first route-valid feasible solution and does not use criterion (6).
