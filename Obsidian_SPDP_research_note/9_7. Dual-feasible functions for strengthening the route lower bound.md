
## 1. Purpose and scope

VI 44 imposes a lower bound on the number of actual routes:

$$
K(\theta)
:=
\sum_{\substack{e\in E:\\ u_e=0,\ v_e\in C^p}}\theta_e
\ge k_{\min}.
$$

The current duration-based Sub-LP computes a lower bound on total route duration and converts it into an integer route lower bound. This note extends that idea using **dual-feasible functions (DFFs)** in two complementary ways:

1. compute a stronger fixed integer lower bound $k_{\min}$ by solving DFF-weighted versions of the current Sub-LP;
2. add DFF route-duration valid inequalities directly to the master formulation.

---

## 2. Dual-feasible functions

### 2.1 Definition

A function

$$
f:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
$$

is a **dual-feasible function** if, for every finite set of values $x_i\in[0,1]$,

$$
\sum_{i\in I}x_i\le 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sum_{i\in I}f(x_i)\le 1.
\tag{1}
$$

Thus, any collection of items that fits into a unit-capacity bin remains feasible after their sizes are transformed by $f$.

The identity function

$$
f^{\mathrm{id}}(x)=x
\tag{2}
$$

is the simplest DFF.

### 2.2 Bin-packing lower bound

Consider items with normalized sizes $x_i\in[0,1]$. Suppose that they can be packed into $K$ bins. Applying (1) separately to each bin and summing over the bins gives

$$
\sum_i f(x_i)\le K.
$$

Since $K$ is integer,

$$
K\ge
\left\lceil
\sum_i f(x_i)
\right\rceil.
\tag{3}
$$

With $f^{\mathrm{id}}(x)=x$, (3) reduces to the material bound

$$
K\ge
\left\lceil
\sum_i x_i
\right\rceil.
\tag{4}
$$

A nonlinear DFF may strengthen (4) by capturing packing indivisibilities that are lost in the total-size bound.

For example, consider three items of size $0.51$. The material bound is

$$
\lceil 3(0.51)\rceil=2,
$$

although three bins are required. The threshold DFF with $\eta=1/2$,

$$
f^{\mathrm{MT}}_{\eta}(x)=
\begin{cases}
0, & x<\eta,\\
x, & \eta\le x\le 1-\eta,\\
1, & x>1-\eta,
\end{cases}
\qquad
0\le\eta\le\frac12,
\tag{5}
$$

assigns value $1$ to each item of size $0.51$, yielding the exact lower bound $3$.

### 2.3 Dual interpretation

Let $\mathcal P$ be the set of feasible bin patterns, and let $a_{ip}=1$ if pattern $p$ contains item $i$. The LP relaxation of the pattern-based set-covering formulation is $$ \min \sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\lambda_p $$ subject to $$ \sum_{p\in\mathcal P}a_{ip}\lambda_p\ge1 \qquad \forall i, $$ $$ \lambda_p\ge0 \qquad \forall p\in\mathcal P. $$ Its dual is $$ \max \sum_i\pi_i $$ subject to $$ \sum_i a_{ip}\pi_i\le1 \qquad \forall p\in\mathcal P, $$ $$ \pi_i\ge0 \qquad \forall i. $$ For an item with normalized size $x_i$, set $\pi_i=f(x_i)$. Every feasible pattern $p$ satisfies $$ \sum_i a_{ip}x_i\le1. $$ By dual feasibility of $f$, this implies $$ \sum_i a_{ip}f(x_i)\le1. $$ Hence, $\pi_i=f(x_i)$ satisfies every constraint of the dual pattern-based formulation. Therefore, $$ \sum_i f(x_i) $$ is **a valid lower bound on the number of bins**. The DFF property establishes dual feasible solution without explicitly enumerating all feasible patterns. However, the resulting dual solution is not necessarily optimal, so its bound may be weaker than the full pattern-based LP bound.

## 3. Use of DFFs in rounded capacity inequalities

Consider a CVRP customer subset $S$. Customer $i$ has demand $q_i$, and each vehicle has capacity $Q$. Define normalized demands

$$
x_i=\frac{q_i}{Q}.
$$

The standard rounded capacity lower bound is

$$
r(S)
=
\left\lceil
\frac{\sum_{i\in S}q_i}{Q}
\right\rceil
=
\left\lceil
\sum_{i\in S}x_i
\right\rceil.
\tag{6}
$$

For a DFF $f$, define

$$
r_f(S)
=
\left\lceil
\sum_{i\in S}
f\!\left(\frac{q_i}{Q}\right)
\right\rceil.
\tag{7}
$$

Every vehicle route can serve only a customer set whose total demand is at most $Q$. By dual feasibility, the transformed demand assigned to a single route is also at most one. Hence at least $r_f(S)$ routes are required to serve $S$.

The directed inequalities are

$$
\sum_{(i,j)\in\delta^-(S)}x_{ij}
\ge r_f(S),
\tag{8}
$$

$$
\sum_{(i,j)\in\delta^+(S)}x_{ij}
\ge r_f(S),
\tag{9}
$$

and the corresponding undirected inequality is

$$
\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\delta(S)}x_{ij}
\ge 2r_f(S).
\tag{10}
$$

For a family $\mathcal F$ of DFFs, the strongest available DFF right-hand side is

$$
r_{\mathcal F}(S)
=
\max_{f\in\mathcal F}r_f(S).
\tag{11}
$$

The exact bin-packing number for the demands in $S$ is at least as large as every DFF bound, but computing it is generally harder. DFFs provide inexpensive valid lower bounds that may be stronger than the standard material bound.

## 4. Notation for the SPDP multigraph formulation

Let:

- $E$ be the multigraph edge set;
- $t_e$ be the total duration represented by edge $e$, including its embedded travel, emptying operations, and service at its head node;
- $T$ be the maximum route duration;
- $\theta_e$ be the edge-selection variable;
- $K(\theta)$ be the number of actual routes:

$$
K(\theta)
=
\sum_{\substack{e\in E:\\u_e=0,\ v_e\in C^p}}\theta_e.
\tag{12}
$$

For every edge that can appear in a feasible route,

$$
0\le \frac{t_e}{T}\le 1.
$$

Any edge with $t_e>T$ cannot belong to a feasible route and should be removed or fixed to zero before applying a classical DFF.

For each $f\in\mathcal F$, define the DFF edge coefficient

$$
\alpha_e^f
=
f\!\left(\frac{t_e}{T}\right).
\tag{13}
$$

---

## 5. Direct DFF route-duration valid inequalities

### 5.1 Validity

Consider a feasible route $r$, and let $E(r)$ denote its selected multigraph edges. Route-duration feasibility gives

$$
\sum_{e\in E(r)}t_e\le T,
$$

or equivalently,

$$
\sum_{e\in E(r)}\frac{t_e}{T}\le1.
\tag{14}
$$

By the definition of a DFF,

$$
\sum_{e\in E(r)}
f\!\left(\frac{t_e}{T}\right)
\le1.
\tag{15}
$$

Summing (15) over all routes yields

$$
\sum_{e\in E}
f\!\left(\frac{t_e}{T}\right)\theta_e
\le K(\theta).
$$

Therefore, the following inequality is valid for every $f\in\mathcal F$:

$$
\boxed{
K(\theta)
\ge
\sum_{e\in E}
f\!\left(\frac{t_e}{T}\right)\theta_e
}
\qquad
\forall f\in\mathcal F.
\tag{16}
$$

These inequalities are solution-dependent in the sense that their effective right-hand sides vary with the selected edge composition.

### 5.2 Why the variable expression is not rounded in the LP model

For an integer solution, $K(\theta)$ is integer, so

$$
K(\theta)
\ge
\sum_e\alpha_e^f\theta_e
$$

is equivalent to

$$
K(\theta)
\ge
\left\lceil
\sum_e\alpha_e^f\theta_e
\right\rceil.
\tag{18}
$$

However, the right-hand side of (18) is a discontinuous nonlinear function of decision variables. 
Moreover, evaluating a fractional incumbent $\theta^*$ and adding

$$
K(\theta)\ge
\left\lceil
\sum_e\alpha_e^f\theta_e^*
\right\rceil
$$

as a global constant cut is generally invalid: another feasible integer solution may have a smaller transformed edge sum and use fewer routes.

The fixed DFF Sub-LP bound introduced next is therefore needed to obtain a globally valid rounded constant right-hand side.

### 5.3 Compatibility with the $p$-step pricing problem

Inequality (17) is written only in edge variables. Its coefficient is additive by edge, so it does not introduce a new resource or destroy the shortest-path structure of the $p$-step pricing problem. Its dual contribution can be absorbed into the edge reduced costs through the existing edge-linking constraints.

Thus, (17) is a robust inequality for the current $p$-step formulation.

## 6. DFF generalization of the duration-based Sub-LP

### 6.1 Current Sub-LP feasible region

Let $\mathcal Y$ denote the feasible region of the current Sub-LP. Using continuous variables $y_e\in[0,1]$, it is defined by

$$
\sum_{e:v_e=i}y_e=1
\qquad
\forall i\in\bar V,
\tag{19}
$$

$$
\sum_{e:u_e=i}y_e=1
\qquad
\forall i\in\bar V,
\tag{20}
$$

$$
\sum_{\substack{e:v_e=i\\\sigma(v_e)=\sigma}}y_e
-
\sum_{\substack{e:u_e=i\\\sigma(u_e)=\sigma}}y_e
=0
\qquad
\forall i\in\bar V,\ \sigma\in\Sigma_i^+,
\tag{21}
$$

$$
\sum_{\substack{e:u_e=0\\v_e\in C^p}}y_e
=
\sum_{\substack{e:u_e\in C^d\\v_e=2n+1}}y_e,
\tag{22}
$$

$$
y_e=0
\qquad
\forall e\in E_{0,2n+1},
\tag{23}
$$

$$
0\le y_e\le1
\qquad
\forall e\in E\setminus E_{0,2n+1}.
\tag{24}
$$

This relaxation omits integrality, $p$-step variables and their time labels, global route connectivity, and individual route-duration constraints. Every feasible integer routing solution induces a feasible point in $\mathcal Y$.

### 6.2 DFF-weighted Sub-LP

For each $f\in\mathcal F$, solve

$$
\boxed{
L_f
=
\min_{y\in\mathcal Y}
\sum_{e\in E}
f\!\left(\frac{t_e}{T}\right)y_e
}
=
\min_{y\in\mathcal Y}
\sum_{e\in E}\alpha_e^f y_e.
\tag{25}
$$

Consider any feasible integer routing solution $\theta$. Since $\theta\in\mathcal Y$,

$$
L_f
\le
\sum_e\alpha_e^f\theta_e.
\tag{26}
$$

By the direct route-wise DFF argument,

$$
K(\theta)
\ge
\sum_e\alpha_e^f\theta_e.
\tag{27}
$$

Combining (26) and (27),

$$
K(\theta)\ge L_f.
$$

Since $K(\theta)$ is integer,

$$
\boxed{
K(\theta)\ge\lceil L_f\rceil.
}
\tag{28}
$$

Thus, a valid DFF-based route lower bound is

$$
k_f^{\mathrm{SubLP}}
=
\lceil L_f\rceil.
\tag{29}
$$

### 6.3 Relationship with the current duration-based Sub-LP

If the identity DFF is included,

$$
f^{\mathrm{id}}(x)=x,
$$

then

$$
L_{\mathrm{id}}
=
\min_{y\in\mathcal Y}
\sum_e\frac{t_e}{T}y_e
=
\frac{1}{T}
\min_{y\in\mathcal Y}
\sum_e t_ey_e
=
\frac{\underline H}{T}.
\tag{30}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\left\lceil L_{\mathrm{id}}\right\rceil
=
\left\lceil\frac{\underline H}{T}\right\rceil,
\tag{31}
$$

which exactly reproduces the current duration-based Sub-LP route lower bound.

Consequently, when $f^{\mathrm{id}}\in\mathcal F$,

$$
\max_{f\in\mathcal F}
\lceil L_f\rceil
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{\underline H}{T}\right\rceil.
\tag{32}
$$

The DFF-generalized Sub-LP is therefore never weaker than the current Sub-LP, provided that all LPs are solved correctly and the identity function is included.

### 6.4 Numerical validity

For the minimization problem (25), an incomplete feasible primal objective is an upper bound and must not be used to derive $k_{\min}$. The implementation must use either:

- the proven optimal value $L_f$; or
- a certified dual lower bound $\underline L_f$.

With numerical tolerance $\varepsilon>0$, use

$$
k_f^{\mathrm{SubLP}}
=
\left\lceil
\underline L_f-\varepsilon
\right\rceil.
\tag{33}
$$

## 7. Initial DFF family for computational testing

The initial experiment should use a small and interpretable family.

### 7.1 Identity DFF

$$
f^{\mathrm{id}}(x)=x.
\tag{34}
$$

This function is required because it reproduces the existing Sub-LP bound and provides a baseline.

### 7.2 Martello--Toth threshold family

$$
f^{\mathrm{MT}}_{\eta}(x)=
\begin{cases}
0, & x<\eta,\\
x, & \eta\le x\le1-\eta,\\
1, & x>1-\eta,
\end{cases}
\qquad
0\le\eta\le\frac12.
\tag{35}
$$

A practical initial grid is

$$
\eta\in
\{0.05,0.10,\ldots,0.50\}.
\tag{36}
$$

This family:

- ignores sufficiently small edge-duration fractions;
- preserves medium-sized fractions;
- treats sufficiently large fractions as consuming a complete route.

It can therefore capture route-packing indivisibilities missed by the identity function.

### 7.3 Optional rounding family

The following rounding function is dual feasible for $C\ge1$:

$$
f^{\mathrm{R}}_C(x)
=
\frac{\lfloor Cx\rfloor}{\lfloor C\rfloor}.
\tag{37}
$$

Indeed, if $\sum_i x_i\le1$, then

$$
\sum_i\lfloor Cx_i\rfloor
\le
\left\lfloor C\sum_i x_i\right\rfloor
\le
\lfloor C\rfloor.
$$

For integer $C$, $f^{\mathrm{R}}_C(x)\le x$ pointwise, so it cannot improve the identity bound by itself. Therefore, integer values of $C$ are not a priority. Noninteger values may be tested later, but the identity and threshold families are the recommended first experiment.

The initial family is therefore

$$
\mathcal F
=
\{f^{\mathrm{id}}\}
\cup
\left\{
f^{\mathrm{MT}}_{\eta}:
\eta\in\{0.05,0.10,\ldots,0.50\}
\right\}.
\tag{38}
$$

## 8. Final combined formulation

### 8.1 Fixed DFF route lower bound

For every $f\in\mathcal F$, solve the DFF-weighted Sub-LP and obtain a certified lower bound $\underline L_f$. Define

$$
k_{\min}^{\mathrm{DFF}}
=
\max_{f\in\mathcal F}
\left\lceil
\underline L_f-\varepsilon
\right\rceil.
\tag{39}
$$

The fixed route lower bound used in VI 44 is

$$
\boxed{
k_{\min}^{\mathrm{fixed}}
=
\max
\left\{
k_{\min}^{\mathrm{COR}},
k_{\min}^{\mathrm{DFF}}
\right\}.
}
\tag{40}
$$

Because the identity DFF reproduces the current duration-based Sub-LP, $k_{\min}^{\mathrm{DFF}}$ theoretically dominates its existing bound. If the proven dominance of the current Sub-LP over $k_{\min}^{\mathrm{COR}}$ applies under the implemented assumptions, the COR term is redundant mathematically. It may nevertheless be retained as a numerical safeguard and consistency check.

The strengthened fixed VI 44 is

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{e\in E:\\u_e=0,\ v_e\in C^p}}\theta_e
\ge
k_{\min}^{\mathrm{fixed}}.
}
\tag{41}
$$

### 8.2 Direct DFF route-duration inequalities

For the fixed-$K$ two-index MILP used to generate the initial incumbent warm start, add

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{e\in E:\\u_e=0,\ v_e\in C^p}}\theta_e
\ge
\sum_{e\in E}
f\!\left(\frac{t_e}{T}\right)\theta_e
}
\qquad
\forall f\in\mathcal F.
\tag{42}
$$

In the present experiment, (42) strengthens only the incumbent warm-start MILP; it is not added to the main MILP.

### 8.3 Why both types are retained

The fixed and direct inequalities are complementary in the LP relaxation.

The fixed inequality gives

$$
K(\theta)\ge
\left\lceil\underline L_f-\varepsilon\right\rceil,
$$

which preserves the integer-rounding effect at the root node.

The direct inequality gives

$$
K(\theta)\ge
\sum_e\alpha_e^f\theta_e,
$$

whose effective right-hand side may substantially exceed the global Sub-LP minimum for a particular fractional edge composition.

Neither linear inequality dominates the other in the LP relaxation:

- if $\underline L_f=2.1$ and the current transformed edge sum is $2.4$, the fixed cut gives $K\ge3$, while the direct cut gives only $K\ge2.4$;
- if the current transformed edge sum is $3.7$, the direct cut gives $K\ge3.7$, which is stronger than the fixed cut $K\ge3$.

Accordingly, (41) supplies the fixed VI 44 route bound, while the present experiment uses (42) only to strengthen the fixed-$K$ incumbent warm-start MILP.

## 9. Computational procedure

1. **Preprocess infeasible edges.**  
   Remove or fix to zero every edge satisfying $t_e>T$.

2. **Construct the DFF family.**  
   Use the identity function and the Fekete--Schepers functions.

3. **Solve the DFF Sub-IPs.**  
   Retain the existing duration IP as the identity case and solve the additional Fekete--Schepers weighted IPs. Use a certified safe lower bound, and stop early once the rounded lower and upper bounds coincide. Identical coefficient vectors are solved only once.

4. **Compute the fixed route bound.**  
   Take the maximum of the COR bound, the existing duration-IP bound, and the Fekete--Schepers bounds.

5. **Add the fixed VI 44.**  
   Add (41) once.

6. **Add the direct DFF inequalities to the incumbent warm-start MILP.**  
   Add (42) to the fixed-$K$ two-index MILP used to find an initial incumbent. This experiment does not add these inequalities to the main MILP.

7. **Evaluate.**  
   Compare the resulting route lower bound and fixed-$K$ incumbent performance against the corresponding formulation without DFFs.
